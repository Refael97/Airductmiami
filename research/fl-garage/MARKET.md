## 0. The five findings that change the plan

1. **Spanish is not empty in Miami-Dade.** Precision Garage Door of Miami
   runs a full Spanish mirror with per-city review pages, ADCO runs a full
   `/es/` site, and three smaller Miami companies have Spanish homepages.
   Spanish is empty everywhere else: Tampa Bay, Central Florida, Southwest
   Florida and North Florida return Texas, Georgia, Spain and Miami results
   for Spanish city queries. Push Spanish city pages outside Miami-Dade
   first, and push Spanish cost, symptom and hurricane articles statewide,
   where no Florida company has written a word. See section 5.
2. **Prices are almost, not entirely, unpublished.** Precision South
   Florida publishes an installed door "starting at $1795" and a
   four-hour-or-the-fee-is-waived promise. A1 publishes "$400 off". A
   directory called FL Garage Pros publishes Florida cost pages. Nobody
   publishes a repair price table. The lane is open but a franchise has
   one foot in it. See section 2.
3. **Two quiet storm seasons in a row.** 2025 was the first season since
   2019 with no tropical cyclone hitting Florida. 2026 has produced three
   named storms through 6 September, none near Florida, and NOAA holds a
   below-normal forecast. There is no post-storm replacement surge to ride
   this autumn. The hurricane cluster is a pre-positioning play for the
   next threat, not a launch traffic play. Lead with repair and cost.
4. **Three price lines are out of market.** Labor at $45 per hour is below
   anything published (market $75 to $150). The emergency premium at $150
   to $300 is high (market $75 to $200). The hurricane-rated floor of
   $1,800 is high for a single-car wind-rated door ($1,200 to $1,800
   installed in Florida guides). Section 4.
5. **A "Garage Doors Fixers" exists in Sunrise, Broward County**, on Yelp
   with an address, plus an active "Garage Door Fix, LLC" in Cape Coral
   with a ranking website. Section 6.

## 3. Demand patterns and trends

### 3a. Seasonality

- National pattern: the primary peak is spring, March to May, when
  temperature swings finish off fatigued springs; a secondary peak in
  midsummer; the trough is January and February
  ([Moonrank](https://www.moonrank.ai/seo-for/garage-door-repair),
  [Livepage PPC case](https://livepage.net/cases/sti-garage-ppc-case-study.html),
  [Valley Marketing Group cost-per-lead benchmarks](https://thevalleymarketinggroup.com/blog/garage-door-repair-google-ads-cost-per-lead-2026/)).
  Florida has no winter trough to speak of because it has no cold snap
  failure and its garages cycle year round; expect a flatter curve with the
  spring peak intact.
- Hurricane overlay: every named-storm forecast from August to October
  produces a measurable spike in wind-rated door searches within hours, and
  the companies positioned in May and June own it
  ([Media Spearhead, St. Petersburg](https://mediaspearhead.com/locations/st-petersburg-fl/garage-door-repair/),
  [Cape Coral](https://mediaspearhead.com/locations/cape-coral-fl/garage-door-repair/)).
  The same source claims behaviour changed after 2022 to 2024: homeowners
  who watched a neighbour's door fail now replace before the season.
- Summer heat: 100°F garages cook opener logic boards and plastic gears;
  the brief already says so and the trade press agrees
  ([press release, 2026](https://www.mymalonetelegram.com/online_features/press_releases/2026-canyon-lake-garage-door-repair-demand-rises-amid-summer-heat/article_8398b0d2-adf7-587e-87b1-d8efc80f1da5.html)).
- Seasonal residents: Naples, Bonita Springs, Venice and Marco Island owners
  return in November and find the failure then. No competitor writes a
  "returning for the season" checklist. This is a real, timed content slot
  for month two.

hurricane cluster should exist on day one so they are indexed and aged

and Spanish, not from storms. The Ian and Helene and Milton replacement
doors are now one to three years old; the live storm-related question in
2026 is warranty and first-service on a recently installed door, not
replacement.

### 3e. Insurance pressure

- **Citizens is shrinking fast, and rates are falling, which weakens the
  "insurance is exploding" framing.** Citizens peaked at just over 1.4
  million policies in October 2023, had 777,592 in June 2025
  ([Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/florida-citizens-exposure-shrinks-43-in-last-year-as-depopulation-accelerated/)),
  fell under 400,000 by December 2025
  ([WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-12-27/citizens-property-insurance-now-has-fewer-than-400-000-policies)),
  and was around 336,000 in early 2026
  ([Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/florida-citizens-hopes-for-lower-reinsurance-costs-in-2026-on-exposure-reduction-and-softening/)).
  A secondary source puts it near 278,000 by June 2026
  ([PropertyExemption](https://www.propertyexemption.com/housing-market/florida-homeowners-insurance-rates-2026/));
  treat that figure as unconfirmed. Citizens' 2026 rates fell 8.7% on
  average, the first cut since 2015, with 150,000+ policyholders getting
  10% or more from spring 2026 (same source, and
  [Citizens' own discounts page](https://www.citizensfla.com/discounts) for
  the mitigation rules). Private carriers have filed 5 to 10% reductions
  after tort reform ([GreatFlorida](https://www.greatflorida.com/blog/2026/how-much-is-home-insurance-in-florida/)).
- Premiums are still two to three times the national figure: Insurify puts
  a $300,000 dwelling policy at $5,688 in Florida against $2,580 nationally
  ([MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/homeowners/average-cost-home-insurance-florida/));
  OIR-based figures run around $3,757 with wind coverage (GreatFlorida,
  above). A July 2026 study says premiums will climb sharply by 2035 and
  that mitigation discounts are not enough
  ([Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/07/09/338693.htm)).
  So: wind mitigation interest stays high, but the copy should say
  "premiums are falling for the first time in a decade and the mitigation
  credit is still worth chasing", not "rates are skyrocketing".
- **The wind mitigation form changed.** OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26) took
  effect 1 April 2026, the first rewrite since 2012. It adds design wind
  speed region, roof slope and FORTIFIED designations and requires more
  photos and product documentation
  ([Simpson Strong-Tie](https://seblog.strongtie.com/2026/06/florida-office-of-insurance-regulations-uniform-mitigation-verification-inspection-form-oir-b1-1802-rev-04-26/),
  [CFB Inspect](https://cfbinspect.com/floridas-new-wind-mitigation-reporting-requirements-what-every-homeowner-needs-to-know-in-2026/),
  [OIR form PDF](https://floir.gov/docs-sf/property-casualty-libraries/product-review/all-forms/form-oir-1802-adopted-version-(no-watermark).pdf?Status=Master),
  [Citizens' guide to the 04/26 form](https://training.citizensfla.com/lms/courses/core/CIS_Resources/Job_Aids/PolicyCenter/Wind_Loss_Mitigation_Guide_Rev0426.pdf)).
- **Opening protection is all or nothing.** One unprotected glazed opening
  (window, skylight, glass in a garage door) zeroes the whole credit. A
  solid wind-rated garage door is a non-glazed opening and does not count
  against the glazed credit; a garage door with non-impact glass inserts
  can cost the entire credit even with impact windows everywhere else
  ([Latent Insurance](https://www.latentinsure.com/florida-homeowners-insurance/wind-mitigation),
  [Armor Pro](https://www.armorprowindows.com/blog/florida-garage-door-wind-code)).
  This is the single most useful sentence the insurance article can
  contain, and no garage door company says it.
- **My Safe Florida Home.** Statute
  [s. 215.5586](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/215.5586) lists
  opening protection "including exterior doors, garage doors, windows, and
  skylights" as eligible, lets the department require that all openings be
  improved as a condition of reimbursement, caps grants at $10,000 and
  waives the match for low-income owners. Funding: $352 million distributed
  in the 2025 to 2026 round and $405 million or more reappropriated for
  2026 to 2027 in the budget passed 29 May 2026
  ([PropertyExemption, June 2026 update](https://www.propertyexemption.com/guides/my-safe-florida-home-2026-update/),
  secondary). The portal at mysafeflhome.com could not be fetched from
  here; the condo-program portal closed in May 2026 and moved to email
  ([Revolution](https://www.revolutionflorida.com/blog/my-safe-florida-home-grant-guide)).
  The brief's "when the program is open" wording stands; add "only the
  openings flagged on your inspection are eligible, and the state can
  require all of them be done together".

### Days 1 to 14: ship and get indexed

1. **Ship the whole build**, both languages, with the price corrections
   from section 4 (labour, emergency premium, hurricane floor, service call
   policy) applied before the first crawl. Do not launch with a price you
   will change; the cost pages get quoted.
2. **Indexing order**, submitted by hand in Search Console after the
   sitemap:
   - Spanish region pages for Tampa Bay, Central Florida, Southwest
     Florida, Palm Beach and Broward (five URLs).
   - Spanish city pages: Tampa, Kissimmee, Orlando, Brandon, Riverview,
     Wesley Chapel, Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Naples, West Palm Beach,
     Boynton Beach, Hollywood, Pembroke Pines, Miramar (fourteen URLs).
   - Spanish cost articles: `cuanto-cuesta-reparar-puerta-de-garaje-florida`,
     spring cost, opener install cost, new door cost, hurricane door cost.
   - English tier-1 cities and the seven English regions.
   - Miami-Dade Spanish city pages last in the queue; they are contested.
3. **Cost cluster first, all of it**: the English cost guides plus their
   Spanish twins, each opening with the number. These are the pages
   directories own in English and nobody owns in Spanish.
4. **Hurricane cluster on day one, both languages**, all twelve articles,
   so they carry age before the next named-storm forecast. Do not feature
   them on the homepage in a quiet season; link them from the services and
   the regions.
5. **Internal links**: every service page links to its cost article; every
   city page links to the region, three neighbours, the cost guide and the
   storm-damage page; every article links to two services. Run the
   redirect check from the audit before submit: zero internal links that
   return 301.

### Day 14 in Search Console

- Coverage: indexed versus discovered-not-indexed, by language. If Spanish
  is being indexed faster than English, that is the duct pattern and it is
  fine; if Spanish is stuck in "discovered", check hreflang and canonicals.
- Any Spanish page with impressions at all. On the duct site the first
  Spanish impressions arrived in the second week.
- Soft 404s and redirect errors: should be zero.
- Queries: look for "reparacion de puertas de garaje {city}" and "cuanto
  cuesta" strings. Note which cities appear first; those get the second
  paragraph of local detail written next.

### Day 30 in Search Console

- Impressions by page type: Spanish cities, Spanish articles, English
  cities, English articles, services. Expect Spanish articles and non-Miami
  Spanish cities to lead.
- Pages at average position under 10: list them, and tighten titles on any
  with impressions above 50 and CTR under 3%.
- English "garage door repair {city}" positions for the tier-1 cities: if
  they sit past page three like the duct site's English, that is expected;
  keep writing local detail, do not chase with new English pages.
- If `fort-myers` shows nothing like the brief's 9,900, that confirms 3h
  item 5. Resize Southwest Florida to Naples-class.
- Watch for Miami-Dade Spanish city pages: if they sit past position 10
  while Tampa Bay Spanish sits inside it, the Miami plan is articles and
  cost, not city pages, for the rest of the quarter.

- "Garage door permits after 31 December 2026: what the 9th Edition

  is a real date and the search will exist.
- Pre-season pages for March to May, written now so they are indexed by

- Symptom cluster in Spanish (won't open, spring snapped, opener hums,
  off the track, remote), one page per symptom, listicle titles, the
  Precision recipe from the New York competitor notes.
- Review the price table against the day-60 quote log from the partner and
  correct any line that the partner cannot honour.

### What not to do

- No Google Business Profile with a borrowed or virtual address, no
  reviews, no stars, no testimonials. The duct site got to five or six
  leads a week without any of it.
- No Miami-Dade-first Spanish push. It is the one region where a franchise
  already ranks in Spanish.
- No storm-chasing copy in a quiet season: no "this could be the year", no
  countdown, no urgency theatre. Two quiet seasons make that copy read as
  sales, and the brief bans it anyway.
- No "hurricane proof", no promised insurance dollar saving, no promise of
  My Safe Florida Home money.
- No publishing $45 per hour labour or a $150 minimum emergency premium;
  both are outside what Florida readers can check against Angi in one
  click.
- No new English city pages for volume's sake in the first 60 days; the
  English side ranks late, and the fix is local detail, not more pages.
- No competing on age or reviews with companies founded in 1938, 1955,
  1984 or 1991. The trust block is prices, written estimate, permit pulled
  by a licensed contractor, warranty on the invoice.
- No cross-links to any other network site, and no internal link that
  passes through a redirect.
