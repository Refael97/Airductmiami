## 0. The five findings that decide the answer

1. **Demand is real but small, and the money is in the head term, not the
   city terms.** "sliding glass door repair" measures 5,400 a month
   nationally at $7.02 CPC. The Florida city terms are 40 to 210 a month
   each, but they carry **$10 to $21 CPC**, which is the highest per-click
   value of any local term in any of this network's research files except
   garage doors. Small pond, expensive fish.
2. **The SERP is not thin. It is saturated with specialists.** Every metro
   checked (Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Tampa, Orlando, Naples, Fort Myers)
   returns eight to ten dedicated sliding-door companies on exact-match
   domains. Not handymen, not glass shops, not directories. Directories are
   pushed to the second screen. This is the opposite of the garage door cost
   SERPs, where directories owned everything.
3. **The two openings this network normally exploits are already taken.**
   Published prices: taken, by at least four competitors including regional
   price tables broken out inland / coastal / HVHZ. Spanish: taken in Miami,
   Broward, Palm Beach and Tampa, by four separate operators.
4. **The Florida story checks out on code and salt, but not on the
   statistics competitors publish about it.** HVHZ impact requirements do
   apply to sliding glass doors, permits and product approval are real, and
   marine corrosion of rollers is physically sound. The specific numbers in
   circulation ("18 to 30 months within 5 miles of saltwater") come from
   competitor marketing pages, not from any independent source found. Under
   `TRUST-AND-CLAIMS.md` we could not republish them as facts.
5. **Sliding door repair is a different trade from duct cleaning, with
   near-zero skill overlap and a licensing cliff.** A duct crew carries
   negative-air machines and brushes. A slider crew carries a 200-part van,
   suction cups, and a second body to lift a 150 pound impact panel. Whether
   the current fulfillment partner can do this work is a question only the
   owner can answer, and it is the question that decides the whole thing.

**Sliding glass door repair is 1/45th the volume of garage door repair at

opportunity is roughly **1/300th** the size of the vertical this network

and the whole Florida city set adds to roughly 1,900 measured searches a
month across nineteen terms, before the long tail of smaller cities and
symptom queries that Semrush units ran out before I could pull. It makes it
a **niche**, not a **vertical**.

- **People Also Ask and autocomplete patterns.** Not verifiable from this

## 2. Is it a real Florida vertical

Three claims in the brief, checked one at a time.

### 2a. "Nearly every Florida house has a patio slider"

**Not verified as stated.** No independent statistic for the share of
Florida homes with a sliding patio door was found. Searches for it returned
only vendor marketing pages.

The nearest measured proxy found is
[Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-windows-patio-doors-market-single-family-homes-report),
which puts **sliding doors at 43.4% of US patio door revenue share in 2024**,
the largest share of any patio door type. That is a national revenue share,
not a Florida installed-base count, and it is not the same claim.

Vendor pages describe the slider as "the workhorse of most Florida patios"
([All Glass & Windows](https://allglasswindows.com/blog/best-patio-doors-florida-homes/)).
That is plausible and consistent with Florida's housing stock, but it is
marketing copy and we could not publish it as a fact.

**What we could publish:** that sliding patio doors are the most common
patio door type sold in the US, sourced to Grand View. Nothing stronger.

### 2b. "Salt air destroys rollers and tracks"

**Mechanism verified, numbers not.** Marine-atmosphere corrosion of steel
bearings and aluminium tracks is uncontroversial engineering. What is in
circulation online is competitor marketing, and it does not agree with
itself:

| Claim | Source | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Rollers within 5 miles of saltwater last 18 to 30 months, versus 4 to 6 years inland | [Alpha Sliding Doors](https://alphaslidingdoors.com/florida-sliding-glass-door-repair-statistics/) | Competitor marketing |
| Bearings within 1 mile of saltwater fail in 18 months, 60% faster than inland | [Alpha Sliding Doors](https://alphaslidingdoors.com/sliding-door-roller-replacement-florida/) | Competitor marketing |
| Standard steel rollers destroyed by salt corrosion in 5 to 7 years | [Speedy Sliding Door](https://speedyslidingdoor.com/guide/) | Competitor marketing |
| Plan roller service every 24 months within 5 miles of the coast | [Mr Door Pro](https://mrdoorpro.com/blog/sliding-door-roller-types-guide/) | Competitor marketing |

Two of those come from the same site and contradict each other (18 to 30
months at 5 miles, versus 18 months at 1 mile described as the same finding).
None cites a test, a study, or a warranty dataset. Under
`docs/TRUST-AND-CLAIMS.md` ("Statistics get a real source") **none of these
is publishable**. The defensible version is qualitative: coastal salt air
corrodes steel roller bearings and aluminium tracks faster than inland air,
which is why stainless rollers are specified near the coast.

### 2c. "HVHZ impact code applies to sliding doors the way it does to garage doors"

**Verified, with one technical correction.**

Sliding glass doors are **glazed openings**. In the Wind-Borne Debris Region
and the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (Miami-Dade and Broward), every exterior
glazed opening must be impact resistant or protected. Per
[FBC Residential 2023, Chapter 3, R301.2.1.2](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-residential-code-2023/chapter/3/building-planning),
glazed openings in wind-borne debris regions must meet **TAS 201, 202 and
203**, or **ASTM E1886 and E1996**, with the large missile test applying
within 30 feet of grade and the small missile test above it.

The correction: **garage doors are tested to a different standard.** The same
code section names **ANSI/DASMA 115** for garage doors and rolling doors,
and TAS 201/202/203 or ASTM E1886/E1996 for glazed openings. So the *rule* is
identical (protect the opening) but the *test standard* is not. A page that
says "sliding doors are covered by the same code as garage doors" is right;
one that says "the same test" is wrong.

**Permits and product approval.** Also verified in substance:

- A Miami-Dade **Notice of Acceptance (NOA)** is the county's product
  approval, issued after TAS 201/202/203 large-missile testing, and
  [each unit must carry a permanent label reading "Miami-Dade County Product
  Control Approved"](https://vvwindows.com/miami-dade-noa/), with a copy of
  the full NOA available at the job site on request of the building
  official. NOAs are searchable at the county Product Approval database.
- Outside the HVHZ, the equivalent is **Florida Product Approval**, and in
  the HVHZ the approval must specifically list HVHZ compliance
  ([Statewide Impact Windows](https://www.statewideimpactwindows.com/blog/p.260420001/do-you-need-a-permit-for-door-replacement-in-florida-2026-guide/)).
- **A permit is required for impact window and door installation in
  effectively every Florida jurisdiction**, same source. Orange County lists
  a "Windows\Doors Replacement size for size" work type with sliding glass
  doors named as an example
  ([Orange County](https://www.ocfl.net/PermitsLicenses/Permits/Exterior-Windows-Doors.aspx),
  page not fetchable, description from the indexed snippet).
- **The 25% rule applies.** Replacing more than 25% of a home's total glazed
  opening area within 12 months in the WBDR or HVHZ forces every replacement
  unit to meet current new-construction impact standards
  ([Karoly Windows](https://karolywindows.com/florida-25-window-rule-wind-borne-debris-region/)).
  A large three-panel slider is a big share of a small house's glazing, so
  this rule bites harder on sliders than on windows.
- **In Miami-Dade and Broward you cannot replace a non-impact unit with
  another non-impact unit** once a permit is pulled.

**Where the permit line falls for repair work.** No authoritative government
source could be read from this sandbox. Competitor and permit-service pages
consistently say that **repair** (rollers, tracks, handles, locks, glass in
kind) does not trigger a permit, while **replacing the door assembly** does.
That is consistent with the code logic (a permit attaches to altering the
opening or its pressure-bearing capability, not to servicing hardware), but
it is **inferred, not verified**, and it varies by jurisdiction. Any page we
publish on this must say "check with your building department" rather than
assert a statewide rule.

**Verdict on section 2: yes, this is a genuinely Florida-shaped vertical.**
The code hook is real and it is the strongest content angle available, for
exactly the reason it was on the garage door site. It is also the smallest
part of the demand: "hurricane sliding glass doors" is 320 a month and
"impact sliding doors" is 590.

The honest version of this list is short, which is the point.

specialists per metro, a franchisor rolling up the category, and prices and

## 5. Licensing

**Short answer: repair generally does not require a state contractor licence.
Installation does. The line runs through the words "structurally anchored".**

- Florida **removed Glass and Glazing from the mandatory state licence types**
  in 2012 via Senate Bill 704, amending
  [s. 489.105, F.S.](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2022/489.117).
  DBPR's own guidance states a state certified licence is not required for
  **non-structural** glass and glazing work such as installing glass and
  mirrors
  ([DBPR answer 1725](https://myfloridalicense.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/1725/~/is-a-certified-glass-and-glazing-specialty-contractor%E2%80%99s-license-required-to-do)).
  The corresponding rule,
  [61G4-15.018](https://flrules.org/gateway/ruleno.asp?id=61G4-15.018),
  is marked **Repealed**.
- **The exception is the whole story.** The same DBPR guidance says
  installation of **doors, windows, storefront frames and structurally
  anchored opening protections still requires a Division I contractor
  licence or an appropriate specialty licence.** A sliding glass door
  assembly anchored into the opening is exactly that.
- **Local licensing survives the state repeal.** DBPR states counties and
  municipalities may still require a local licence for glass and glazing
  work ([DBPR answer 1840](https://myfloridalicense.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/1840/~/can-my-county-or-municipality-still-require-me-to-have-a-license-to-perform)).
  Miami-Dade and Broward in particular should be assumed to require one until
  checked.

**Where the line falls, practically:**

| Work | Licence position |
|---|---|
| Roller, track cap, handle, lock, weatherstrip, screen | No state contractor licence required. Local licence may apply. Insurance is the real gate |
| Replacing glass in an existing frame, non-structural | No state licence under the 2012 repeal. In the HVHZ, the replacement glazing still has to satisfy the opening's approval |
| Replacing the door assembly, anchoring into the opening | **Division I or appropriate specialty licence required.** Permit required. Florida Product Approval or Miami-Dade NOA required |
| Enlarging or altering the opening | Structural. General contractor territory |

Competitors reflect this split. Alpha Sliding Doors says "licensed, insured
and bonded" without naming a class. 24hr Sliding Door Repair publishes
**CGC1534821**, a Certified General Contractor number, which is Division I.
[Vision Aluminum & Glass](https://visionaluminumglass.com/sliding-glass-door-services-at-doral/)
publishes CGC1536404. So the serious operators in this niche carry general
contractor licences, because they sell replacement, not only repair.

**Caveat.** None of the DBPR or statute pages could be fetched; all of the
above is from indexed snippets of those pages. Before any page is published
making a licensing claim, one of them must be read directly.

## 6. The verdict

### 6a. Does it belong on the air duct site

**No. Not in any form.**

Three reasons, in order of weight.

**1. There is no topical bridge.** The air duct site's eleven services are
`air-duct-cleaning`, `dryer-vent-cleaning`, `dryer-vent-installation`,
`hvac-cleaning`, `air-duct-repair`, `mold-remediation`,
`air-duct-sanitizing`, `commercial-air-duct-cleaning`,
`uv-light-installation`, `indoor-air-quality-testing`, `attic-insulation`.
Every one of those sits inside one entity: **the air in your house and the
system that moves it**. Attic insulation is the furthest reach and it is
still an HVAC-load argument. A sliding glass door is a structural opening in
an exterior wall. It shares no vocabulary, no customer trigger, no
technician, no supplier and no schema type with anything on that site. The
site would be telling Google it is an indoor air quality company that also
does exterior door hardware, and Google has no reason to believe either
claim after that.

**2. The site cannot afford dilution, because it has not cashed in the focus
it has.** `research/fl-airduct/GSC-2026-09-07.md` measures the English side
at **average position 32.4 across 166 pages, 25,841 impressions, 63 clicks**.
There are 27 queries between position 21 and 40 carrying 4,421 impressions
and zero clicks. Deerfield Beach pulls 2,460 impressions at position 34.7
and returns nothing. That is a site sitting one push away from daylight on
work it has already done. Every hour and every internal link spent on
sliding doors is an hour not spent moving Bradenton from 19.8 to 9. The
GSC document already concluded that "more pages is the wrong lever". A
sliding door cluster is the most off-topic version of more pages available.

**3. It breaks the playbook's own rule.** `docs/SITE-PLAYBOOK.md`: "Every
page earns its place. A page exists because a real query exists for it."
There is a real query, so that half passes. But the same playbook's site
architecture is 8 to 12 services "one per real service, each a money page",
in one vertical. And `docs/TRUST-AND-CLAIMS.md` requires that "each site is
defensible on its own: real service, real fulfillment, real contact path". A
duct company page offering impact slider replacement, with no licence class
and no NOA experience behind it, is not defensible.

There is no small version of this that is safe either. One article
("sliding glass door repair in Florida") on an air duct domain would not
rank, because it would be one page of slider content against nine
specialists per metro, on a domain with no topical claim to the subject. It
would earn nothing and cost coherence.

### 6b. Is it worth its own site

**No, not next. Probably not at all in this form.**

The garage door rebuild proved the template re-points in a day, so the build
cost is genuinely low, and that is the strongest argument for saying yes.
Against it:

| Test | Garage doors, FL | Sliding doors, FL |
|---|---|---|
| Head term volume | 246,000 | 5,400 |
| Head term CPC | $51.67 | $7.02 |
| Directories own the cost queries | Yes, that was the opening | No, specialists do |

The garage door decision rested on four openings: volume, unpublished
prices, empty Spanish, empty hurricane content. **Sliding doors offer a
fraction of the volume and only one of the four openings, and that one (the
permit and product-approval cluster) is the smallest slice of the demand.**

There is a narrower version worth naming, because it is the only one that
survives the evidence: **Southwest Florida in Spanish.** Naples at $20.98
CPC, Cape Coral at $15.96 with KD 8, Fort Myers at $12.46 with KD 17, and no
Spanish slider competitor found in Collier or Lee. That is a real, small,
defensible pocket. It is also perhaps 400 measured searches a month, and it
still requires a fulfillment partner who does not exist yet.

**If the network has capacity for one more site, the evidence in this
folder says spend it on the air duct site's own stranded 4,421 impressions
and its Spanish depth, not on a new vertical at 1/300th the size of the last
one.**

### 6c. Fulfillment

**This is a different trade, and the part that matters most is the part I
cannot answer.**

What can be established:

- **The skills do not overlap.** Duct cleaning is negative-air machines,
  rotary brushes, HEPA collection, antimicrobial fogging, working inside a
  mechanical system. Slider repair is hardware: pulling a panel off a track
  with suction cups, setting roller height, capping a corroded sill, cutting
  and setting glass. Different tools, different failure modes, different
  liability. No company was found that sells both.
- **The van is different.** Alpha stocks 200+ parts for 10 door brands per
  truck. That is an inventory business. A duct van carries no inventory.
- **Panel weight forces a second body.** An impact panel can exceed 150 lb
  and sources describe a two-person crew for a standard 6 ft unit. Duct
  cleaning is commonly a one or two person visit with a very different
  physical task.
- **The licensing cliff sits in the middle of the service list.** Repair
  needs insurance and possibly a local licence. Replacement needs a
  Division I or specialty licence, a permit, and product approval. A partner
  who can do rollers cannot necessarily do the job the customer actually
  wants when the door turns out to be beyond repair, and that is the
  expensive half of the ticket.

**What I cannot know and the owner can:** whether the existing Florida
fulfillment partners, or anyone in their network, already runs a slider or
glazing crew, or holds a CGC. `docs/CHANGELOG-SEO.md` records that Florida
was chosen over New York partly because it "has fulfillment partners", but
nothing in this repository says which trades those partners cover. If one of
them holds a general contractor licence and a glass crew, the answer to 6b
changes from "no" to "the Southwest Florida Spanish pocket is worth a
cheap test". If not, this is a lead-generation site with no one to send the
leads to, which `docs/TRUST-AND-CLAIMS.md` treats as a phone number that
goes nowhere.

**Recommendation: ask the fulfillment partner one question before anything
else is decided.** Do you, or does anyone you would sub to, repair sliding
glass doors in Florida, and do they hold a Division I or specialty licence.
Everything above is downstream of that answer.
