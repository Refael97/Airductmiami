## The thirty second test

Press the wall button inside the garage.

**Wall button works, remote does not.** The opener is fine. It is the remote, its battery, its pairing, or the lock setting on the wall console.

**Neither works.** The opener has a problem. Check power first, including whether the garage outlet is on a GFCI that tripped during the last storm.

**Both work but the remote only works from the driveway.** That is a range problem, which has its own short list of causes below.

## If it is the remote

**Battery.** Remote batteries fail suddenly rather than fading. Replace it before assuming anything else, even if the LED on the remote still lights, because the LED needs far less power than the transmitter. In Florida this is the first answer more often than anywhere else, because a remote that lives in a car parked outside spends the summer in a 140°F cabin, and coin cells do not like that.

**The lock button.** Most wall consoles have a lock or vacation mode that disables every remote while keeping the wall button working. It is easy to press without noticing, especially on the newer consoles with three or four small buttons, and it produces exactly this symptom. Look for a lock icon or a slowly blinking light on the console.

![A smart wall console for a garage door opener, showing the door button, the light button and the lock button that disables remotes when pressed.](/images/smart-wall-console.webp)

**Pairing.** Remotes lose their pairing when someone presses the learn button and holds it too long, which clears every stored code, and on some models after repeated power interruptions. Re-pairing takes under a minute: press the learn button on the motor unit, then press the remote button within thirty seconds. Our guide to [programming a remote](/blog/how-to-program-a-garage-door-remote/) has the steps for each major brand.

**The remote itself.** Buttons wear out, cases crack, and remotes get dropped in the driveway and rained on in an afternoon storm. If a second remote works and this one does not, you have your answer. A replacement remote is a small part that we program on the spot.

## If it is a range problem

Range problems are their own category and they have two common causes.

**The antenna wire.** A short wire hangs down from the motor housing. It should hang freely and be undamaged. Tucked up out of the way, coiled around the rail, or snapped off, and range collapses. Letting it hang straight down is a free fix.

**LED bulbs.** This catches people out constantly. Many LED bulbs emit radio interference in the same band the remote uses. Fitting an LED bulb in the opener and then losing remote range is a very common sequence, and the bulb swap usually happens because the old incandescent kept blowing in the heat. Use a bulb rated for garage door openers, and the range usually returns immediately.

Other causes we see: a new WiFi router or mesh node mounted on the garage wall, metal hurricane shutters left closed on the garage windows, an aluminum or steel impact door on a long three car garage where the distance to the opener is genuinely marginal, and a neighbor's new device in a townhouse row where the garages share a wall.

## If neither the remote nor the wall button works

**Power.** Check that the opener is plugged in and that the outlet has power. Garage outlets in Florida are on GFCI circuits, and a GFCI trips when the outlet or anything on the circuit gets wet. After a storm that pushed water under the door, or a hose used near the outlet, the GFCI stays tripped until someone presses reset. Find the button on the outlet with the reset, which may be in the garage, the laundry room or a bathroom.

**Power outage.** If the neighborhood is dark after a storm, nothing on the opener will work until power returns unless the unit has a battery backup. Pull the manual release and open the door by hand. Our article on [battery backup and power outages](/blog/garage-door-battery-backup-power-outage/) explains why we fit it on every new opener here.

**The wall console wiring.** Two thin wires run from the wall button to the opener along the wall and ceiling. They get stapled through, caught by a ladder, and in a Florida garage the bare copper corrodes green at the terminals from humidity. A corroded terminal gives an intermittent wall button that works some days and not others.

**The logic board.** Florida summers bring more lightning than most of the country, and a surge through the power line or the wall console wiring is the usual way a board dies. A board that has spent several summers at ceiling height in a 100°F garage is already weakened, and the surge finishes it. Signs: the opener light flickers or the unit clicks but does nothing, and the learn button LED will not light. A logic board replacement is an [opener repair](/services/garage-door-opener-repair/) in the $95 to $300 range on most units. The blinking pattern on the motor unit often tells us which part failed before we open it, and our guide to [opener blinking light codes](/blog/garage-door-opener-blinking-light-codes/) decodes the common ones.

## When to replace instead of repair

If the opener was made before 1993, it has no photo eye safety sensors and should be replaced regardless of what is wrong with the remote. Read [old garage door opener safety](/blog/old-garage-door-opener-safety/) if you are not sure how old yours is.

If parts are no longer made for the model, or the repair quote approaches half the cost of a new unit, replacement is the better spend. A new chain or belt drive opener runs [$275 to $600 installed](/services/garage-door-opener-installation/). In Florida we recommend spending the extra $60 to $150 for battery backup on any new unit, because the first thing a storm takes is the power and the second thing you need is the car.

If the remote problem is really a "my phone should just do this" problem, a [smart opener](/services/smart-garage-door-opener-installation/) at $350 to $750 installed replaces the remote with an app, adds a camera option, and includes the battery backup. It also fixes the range problem permanently, because the phone talks to the opener over WiFi rather than radio.
