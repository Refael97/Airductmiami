## Same place every time, or different places

This one distinction narrows it down faster than anything else.

**Same point, every time.** Something physical is in the way at that height. Look at both tracks at that exact position for a dent, a bulge, a rust patch, a loose bracket, or a roller that is not sitting fully in the track. Then look at the hinges on the section joint that is level with the stop point.

**Different points, or gradually worse.** More likely the spring balance, a stretching cable, or an opener that is losing force in the heat. A door that used to run cleanly and now stops a little higher each month is telling you the springs are tired, and our article on a [door that opens slowly](/blog/garage-door-opens-slowly/) covers that path.

## The manual test

Pull the manual release with the door closed if you can get it there. Then lift the door by hand, slowly, through its whole travel.

You are feeling for two things: a point where it becomes hard to move, and whether it holds position when you let go.

A rough spot at one height confirms the mechanical cause. A door that will not hold halfway confirms the spring balance is off, and that needs to be fixed before anything else is worth doing.

If the door will not move by hand at all, stop. Something is jammed hard enough that pulling on it will make the damage worse.

## The usual causes, in order

**Seized roller.** A roller with a dead bearing does not turn, it slides. It drags, it wears a groove in the track, and eventually it jams at the tightest point in the run. Florida humidity kills roller bearings early, and steel rollers within a few miles of the coast rust solid. Replacing the full set is usually done on a [tune-up visit](/services/garage-door-tune-up/), with the rollers quoted as parts on top of the $85 to $160.

![Worn garage door rollers pulled from a track, showing flattened wheels and a seized bearing packed with rust and grit.](/images/worn-garage-door-rollers.webp)

**Rusted or bent track.** A bump from a car bumper, a ladder leaned against it, or years of a dragging roller. In Florida the more common version is rust at the bottom two feet of the vertical track, where water from the daily afternoon storm runs under the bottom seal and sits against the slab. The flaking metal catches the rollers. Track can sometimes be cleaned and straightened; badly rusted or bent sections get replaced. This is an [off-track or track repair](/services/garage-door-off-track-repair/) from $140, running to $600 for a new section.

**Loose track bracket.** The track spreads under load and the roller comes half out. Common on doors that have never been serviced, and on older CBS houses where the lag bolts into the jamb have worked loose over decades of cycling.

**Frayed or stretched cable.** One side loses support, the door goes crooked, and it binds in the tracks. Rust on a cable is the most common visible warning we see in Florida, and a rusted cable frays strand by strand until it lets go. This one is urgent because the next step is the door coming off the track entirely. [Cable repair](/services/garage-door-cable-repair/) is $95 to $300, or $225 to $400 with the drum. If the door is already visibly crooked, read our guide to a [crooked or uneven door](/blog/garage-door-crooked-uneven/).

![A frayed garage door lift cable at the bottom bracket, with broken strands standing out from the rust-stained wire.](/images/frayed-lift-cable.webp)

**Cracked hinge.** End hinges carry the roller. A cracked one lets the roller shift out of line, and the door binds at that section every time. Hinges are quoted as parts on a service visit with labor at $45 to $120 per hour.

**Travel limits.** If the opener stops the door at the same point and the door moves freely by hand through that spot, the limit setting is the answer. Limits drift on older units, and on some models they reset after repeated power cuts. Resetting limits is part of a tune-up or an [opener repair](/services/garage-door-opener-repair/) at $95 to $300.

**Opener force in the heat.** An opener whose force setting is marginal will move the door fine at 7 in the morning and stop halfway at 4 in the afternoon when the garage is 105°F and the motor has lost torque. If the problem is worse on hot afternoons and the door moves freely by hand, look at the opener before the hardware.

## Getting the car out

If the door is stuck high enough to drive under, do not. A door held by a jam is not a door you want a car or a person under.

The safe order is: pull the manual release, get the door either fully up and securely propped with locking clamps on both tracks, or fully down, and only then move the vehicle.

If you cannot move the door at all by hand, stop and call. Forcing a jammed door is how a $140 repair becomes a [panel replacement](/services/garage-door-panel-replacement/) at $250 to $800 per section. Our article on a [car trapped in the garage](/blog/car-trapped-in-garage/) covers the options when you need the car now.

## Why we see so much of this in Florida

A door in Cape Coral or Clearwater lives in salt air, gets wet at the bottom seal almost every afternoon from June to October, cycles more than a northern door because the garage is the front door, and sits in a room that stays above 100°F all summer. Each of those shortens the life of the rollers, the track and the cables. Our article on [salt air and garage door corrosion](/blog/salt-air-garage-door-corrosion/) explains what to look for on a coastal door, and a tune-up every spring, before hurricane season, catches most of it before the door stops halfway with the car inside.
