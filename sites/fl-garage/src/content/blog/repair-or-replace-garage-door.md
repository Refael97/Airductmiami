## The default is repair

Most of the time the thing that failed is a wear part. Springs, cables, rollers, hinges and bearings all have finite lives and all have standard replacements. None of them is a reason to buy a new door, and on a Florida door the wear part that failed is usually the one that cycled the most or rusted the fastest.

## The six cases where replacement is right

**Sections are rusted through.** Not surface rust, but corrosion through the steel, usually starting at the bottom section where the seal holds water against it every afternoon for half the year. Within a few miles of either coast this happens years sooner than inland. Once the section has lost its integrity there is nothing to repair, and a new bottom section on a door whose other sections are going the same way is money spent twice. Our guide to [rust and Florida humidity](/blog/garage-door-rust-florida-humidity/) shows what through rust looks like against the surface kind.

**A damaged panel cannot be matched.** If the profile is discontinued, the honest options are a visible mismatch or a full replacement. On the front of the house most people choose replacement. [Panel replacement](/services/garage-door-panel-replacement/) is $250 to $800 per section when the profile is still made.

**More than one section is damaged.** At two sections you are usually past half the price of a new door, and after a storm that is the common case: the wind pushes the middle sections in and bends the top one against the track.

**The door has no wind rating.** This is the Florida case and it gets its own section below.

**The repair approaches half the price of a new door.** A $700 repair on a fifteen year old door with original springs, cables and rollers is not a good buy, and if that door is also unrated the arithmetic gets worse.

**The garage touches living space and the door is a thin uninsulated skin.** In Florida this is a comfort and heat question rather than a heating bill question, and on its own it is not a reason to replace. If you are replacing anyway, it is the moment to read our guide on [insulated versus uninsulated doors](/blog/insulated-vs-uninsulated-garage-door/).

## The Florida case: an unrated door before the season

The Florida Building Code requires a garage door to be rated for the design wind pressure at the site. Miami-Dade and Broward counties are the High Velocity Hurricane Zone, where a door needs a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance. Elsewhere it needs a Florida Product Approval, an FL number. A door that carries neither was installed before the rules applied to it, or was installed without a permit.

How to check: open the door and look at the inside face of the sections and the end stiles for a label. It should name the manufacturer and model, a design pressure as a positive and a negative number, and an approval number. Our guide to [wind rated versus impact rated doors](/blog/wind-rated-vs-impact-rated-garage-door/) walks through reading it. No label and no permit record means you should treat the door as unrated.

There are a lot of these doors. The statewide Florida Building Code took effect in 2002, so a door installed before then outside South Florida was often never rated at all. In [Miami-Dade County](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) many of the doors that went in after Hurricane Andrew are now more than thirty years old, and while the rules in South Florida were tightened sooner, a thirty year old door has thirty year old springs, cables and steel to go with its label.

An unrated door changes the repair or replace decision in a way nothing else does. The garage door is the largest opening in the house. When it fails in a storm the house pressurizes and the roof is at risk, which is why the code treats it as structural. Spending $600 on springs and cables for an unrated door in Cape Coral or Melbourne keeps the door working; it does not change what happens to it when the wind arrives.

Outside the HVHZ there is a middle path. Retrofit bracing kits with a Florida Product Approval exist and are accepted in most of the state, and for a sound but unrated door they are a real option. Our guide to [bracing kits](/blog/garage-door-hurricane-bracing-kits/) covers where they are accepted and where they are not. In Miami-Dade and Broward the accepted route is a rated door.

Timing matters. Hurricane season runs 1 June to 30 November, and the best time to replace a door is March to May, when lead times are shorter and we are not working around watches and warnings. A door ordered in the spring is on the house before the first storm. A door ordered in September may not be. Our guide to the [best time to replace a garage door in Florida](/blog/best-time-to-replace-garage-door-florida/) explains the calendar.

## The half the price rule

![A new steel garage door installed on a Florida house, with the rating label on the inside face of the section.](/images/new-garage-door-installed.webp)

Add up the repair, including everything that is worn and would be quoted on the same visit, and compare it to a new door of the kind you would actually buy. If the repair is under half, repair. If it is over half, replace, and choose the rated door.

Two numbers make that comparison honest. A new wind rated steel single starts at $950 and typically runs to $4,200 installed, permit included. A hurricane rated door, meaning impact rated or an HVHZ door with the right approval, starts at $1,800 and typically runs to $6,500. Our [hurricane rated garage door cost guide](/blog/hurricane-rated-garage-door-cost/) breaks down what moves the price inside that range, and our [new garage door cost guide](/blog/new-garage-door-cost-florida/) does the same for standard doors.

A rated door may also qualify for a wind mitigation credit on your homeowner insurance. The inspection form records whether openings, including the garage door, have rated protection. We say may qualify, because the credit schedule is your insurer's; ask them for it before you decide. Our guide to the [wind mitigation insurance credit](/blog/garage-door-wind-mitigation-insurance-credit/) covers the form.

## The question people forget to ask

How old is everything else?

A twenty year old door that needs a spring is a door where the spring, the cables, the rollers, the hinges, the bottom brackets and the bearings are all twenty years old, and in a coastal garage they have been in salt air the whole time. Replacing one of them starts a sequence.

That does not automatically mean replace. It means ask for the rest to be assessed and quoted at the same time, so you are comparing a real total against a new door instead of one line item.

## When someone tells you to replace

Ask two things.

**Which sections are damaged, and can you show me?** A structural problem is visible. A rusted through section, a cracked stile, a bent panel: you can see all of them. A recommendation you cannot see is one you cannot check.

**What would the repair cost, if we did repair it?** A company that will not give you the repair number alongside the replacement number is not helping you decide, it is selling. We give both, in writing, and we will do the repair if you choose it.

## What we would say

If the sections are sound and the door is rated, repair. It is worth several times less to us and it is the right answer.

If the sections are sound and the door is unrated, repair if the budget only stretches that far, brace it if you are outside the HVHZ and the kit is accepted at your address, and put the replacement on the calendar for next spring.

If the sections are gone, replace, with a rated door, and do it before June.
