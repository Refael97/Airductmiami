## How emergency pricing should work

There are two numbers, and they should be quoted separately.

The **call-out premium** covers dispatching same-day or outside normal hours:
$150 to $300 in Florida. It pays for the fact that somebody is coming out at
ten at night, on a Sunday afternoon, or ahead of everyone else on a weekday
when the schedule was already full.

The **repair** is quoted at the same published price as any other day. A
spring is $150 to $350 whether it broke at nine in the morning or nine at
night. A cable is $95 to $300. Putting a door back on its track is $140 to
$600 depending on what got bent.

When those two numbers are rolled into one, that is usually where the
exploitation happens. Ask for them separately, and ask before anyone is sent.

## When paying the premium is worth it

**Door stuck open on an attached house.** In Florida the garage is the second
front door, and it usually opens straight into the kitchen or the laundry.
A door stuck open is a security problem, not an inconvenience. Pay the
premium.

**Car trapped and you need it.** If you need the vehicle before the next
working day, pay the premium. If you do not, do not. Our guide to a
[car trapped in the garage](/blog/car-trapped-in-garage/) covers what you can
safely do yourself first.

**Door hanging or partially off the track.** A door in that state can fall.
It should be secured the same day, especially if children or pets use the
garage.

**A storm warning is in effect and the door will not close.** The garage door
is the largest opening in the house, and a door that cannot latch is the
opening a hurricane looks for. If a tropical storm or hurricane warning has
been issued for your county and your door is stuck open or off its track,
that is an emergency and we treat it as one.

**Door closed, spring broken, car outside.** This can wait until morning. Do
not run the opener and do not force the door. Read
[what to do about a broken spring](/blog/what-to-do-broken-spring/) and book
a normal appointment.

## What to do while you wait

Do not press the button. If a spring is broken or a cable has failed, the
opener will make the damage worse with every attempt, and a $150 cable job
becomes a $600 off-track job in three presses.

Find the manual release cord, the red handle hanging from the opener trolley.
Pulling it disconnects the door from the opener. Do this only when the door is
fully closed, because a door with a broken spring will drop if it is up and
you release it. Our [manual release guide](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/)
walks through it step by step.

If the power is out after a storm and the door is simply stuck because the
opener is dead, that is not a repair emergency. Pull the release, lift the
door by hand, and drive out. A door with sound springs lifts with one hand.
If it does not, stop, because something else has failed.

If the door is stuck open and you cannot get it down, secure the garage
another way and say so when you call. It changes the priority.

## Why Florida emergencies cluster

Three patterns fill the after-hours phone here.

**Heat.** By late afternoon in July a closed garage in Orlando or Tampa is
over 100°F. Opener logic boards and the plastic gears in older units fail in
that heat, and they tend to fail on the evening you get home. An opener repair
is $95 to $300; a replacement is $275 to $600 installed. Our article on
[summer heat and garage doors](/blog/summer-heat-garage-door-problems-florida/)
explains which parts go first.

**Storms.** The afternoon storm season and the hurricane season overlap, and
both produce doors pulled off track by wind pressure, cables snapped by
water-rusted bottom brackets, and dead openers after a surge or an outage.
After a named storm the volume goes up and so does the number of companies
willing to charge for it. Our [storm damage guide](/blog/garage-door-storm-damage-what-to-do/)
explains what we do on those calls and what a fair post-storm invoice looks
like.

**Seasonal returns.** In Naples, Bonita Springs, Venice and Palm Beach
Gardens, doors sit unused from May to November and are discovered broken on
the day the owner flies back. Those calls arrive in a wave, and a same-day
slot in that week is worth booking early.

## What the premium should never be

It should never appear on the invoice without having been stated before
dispatch. It should never be applied twice for the same visit. And it should
never be used to justify a repair price above the published one.

Every one of those is a version of the same thing: using a stressful
situation to charge more than the work is worth. It is the single most common
complaint in this trade, it peaks after every hurricane, and it is the reason
our prices are on the website rather than in a technician's head. If a quote
seems high, our guide to [whether a quote is too high](/blog/is-my-garage-door-quote-too-high/)
gives you the numbers to check it against.
