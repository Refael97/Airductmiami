## The comparison table

These are our published Florida prices for a standard single door in normal condition. They are the same numbers on [the price list](/prices/) and on every service page, so you can check a quote against them from your kitchen table.

| Job | Florida range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| One torsion spring, installed | $150 to $350 | Standard 10,000 cycle spring |
| Both springs, installed together | $270 to $600 | Second spring adds $120 to $250 |
| Cable repair | $95 to $300 | Cable and drum together $225 to $400 |
| Cable tension adjustment | $85 to $140 | No parts |
| Opener repair | $95 to $300 | Logic board, gear kit, sensors, limits |
| Opener installed | $275 to $600 | Unit plus installation, standard rail |
| Smart opener installed | $350 to $750 | WiFi, battery backup, camera options |
| Off-track correction | $140 to $600 | Bent track or damaged sections run higher |
| Section replacement | $250 to $800 | Per section, while the profile is still made |
| Wind-rated steel door, installed | $950 to $4,200 | Permit included |
| Hurricane-rated door, installed | $1,800 to $6,500 | Impact-rated or HVHZ, permit included |
| Storm damage repair | $150 to $1,500 | Assessment, bracing, panel or track |
| Tune-up | $85 to $160 | Annual |
| Emergency call-out premium | $150 to $300 | Stated before dispatch, on top of the repair |

A quote inside those ranges is a fair quote. A quote above them needs a reason you can understand.

## The three questions that settle it

**1. What exactly am I paying for?**

An itemised answer. Parts, labor, call-out, permit, disposal. A single number with no breakdown is not a quote, it is a figure.

**2. What specification are the parts?**

For springs: wire size, inside diameter, length and cycle rating. A 20,000 cycle spring genuinely costs more than a 10,000 cycle one and it should be a deliberate choice you were told about. Our article on [high cycle springs](/blog/high-cycle-springs-worth-it/) explains when it is worth it, which in a Florida garage that opens eight times a day is more often than you might think.

For a door: the material, the gauge, the insulation, and above all the rating. A door with a Florida Product Approval and a door with a Miami-Dade NOA are different products at different prices, and the quote should say which one you are getting.

**3. What is the door?**

Single or double, steel or wood, insulated or not, wind-rated or impact-rated. Every one of these legitimately moves the price, and a quote at the top of the range for a standard single steel door needs a reason.

## Legitimate reasons a quote is high

**Double or oversized door.** Most Florida two car garages have a single 16 foot by 7 foot double door. Twice the weight, two springs, heavier hardware.

**Impact-rated or HVHZ door.** Thicker steel, reinforcing struts, laminated glass if there are windows, and springs specified for the extra weight. That is why [hurricane-rated doors](/services/hurricane-rated-garage-door-installation/) start at $1,800 where a standard wind-rated door starts at $950. In Miami-Dade and Broward the door needs a Miami-Dade NOA, and those doors sit at the upper part of the range.

**Wood or custom door.** Heavier, needs a heavier spring, and parts may be model specific.

**High-cycle springs.** Rated for 20,000 or more cycles rather than 10,000. Worth it on a door used many times a day, and it should be presented as an option rather than assumed.

**Corroded hardware near the coast.** Within a few miles of either coast, salt air takes the cables, the bottom brackets and the drums together. A technician who shows you rust on the cable and quotes cable and drum at $225 to $400 instead of cable alone at $95 to $300 is usually right. Our article on [salt air corrosion](/blog/salt-air-garage-door-corrosion/) covers what to expect.

**More than one thing failed.** Genuinely common on an old door. Every part quoted should be shown to you.

**Difficult access.** A garage that needs the truck parked a long way off, a converted garage with a wall built inside the opening, or a door behind a gate that cannot be opened.

**Genuine emergency dispatch.** $150 to $300, stated separately before the truck leaves, and never folded into the repair price itself.

## Illegitimate reasons

Being in a hurry. Having a trapped car. A hurricane three days out. Being quoted after the door was already taken apart. A discount that expires when the technician leaves. Parts replaced without being shown to you. A "permit fee" that appears on the invoice after the door is in.

None of those change what a spring costs.

## The post-storm version

After a named storm the same quote can arrive with a straight face at twice the price, and the reasoning is always demand. Demand is real: lead times get longer and a same-week install may not be possible. But the spring, the panel and the hour of labor cost what they cost.

If the number is well outside the ranges above during a declared state of emergency, the Florida Attorney General's office takes price gouging complaints. Before that, simply get a second written quote. Our [storm damage repair](/services/storm-damage-garage-door-repair/) is priced at $150 to $1,500 for assessment, bracing and panel or track work, and a full replacement is quoted separately in writing.

## What to do with a quote you think is too high

Say you want to think about it. That is a complete sentence and you do not owe an explanation.

If work has not started, you owe nothing beyond an agreed call-out fee. If a technician becomes difficult at that point, that is worth noting and worth reporting.

Then get a second quote, and give the second company the same description you gave the first, so you are comparing the same job. For a new door, make sure both quotes name the same rating. A $2,400 impact-rated door and a $1,400 wind-rated door are not two quotes for the same thing.
