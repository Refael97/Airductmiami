## What you are actually paying for

A garage door spring is not an expensive part. What you are paying for is
the tension in it and the fact that releasing that tension safely takes
tools and practice most people do not have.

The spring itself is a small share of the invoice. The rest of the price is
the visit, the labor, the disposal, and the fact that the person doing it is
carrying a set of winding bars and knows how many quarter turns your
specific door needs. Our article on
[why torsion springs are never a DIY job](/blog/why-never-diy-torsion-spring/)
explains what goes wrong when they are.

## Why the quotes vary so much

Four things genuinely move the number.

**Door size and weight.** A 16 foot double door weighs roughly twice what a
single does and usually runs two springs. Almost every Florida house built
after 1980 has one, and an insulated wind-rated double is heavier again.
That is a bigger job with more parts, and it costs more. Our article on
[how much a garage door weighs](/blog/how-much-does-a-garage-door-weigh/)
gives the figures.

**Spring type and rating.** A standard spring is rated for about 10,000 open
and close cycles. A high-cycle spring is rated for 20,000 or more and costs
more up front. On a door that opens six times a day, high-cycle is usually
the cheaper choice over ten years, and our article on
[whether high-cycle springs are worth it](/blog/high-cycle-springs-worth-it/)
does the arithmetic.

**What else failed.** Springs rarely fail in isolation on an old door.
Cables fray, drums score, and end bearing plates wear, and in Florida they
all rust together. A good technician quotes those separately and shows you
the parts, so you can decline them.

**When you call.** Same-day and after-hours dispatch carries a premium of
$150 to $300. That premium is stated before anyone is sent, and it is not
applied to the repair price itself. Our
[emergency repair cost guide](/blog/emergency-garage-door-repair-cost/)
explains when it is worth paying.

## Why springs fail in Florida

Springs do not fail from cold here. They fail from two things.

**Cycle count.** A Florida garage is the storage room, the workshop, the
second front door and the place the golf cart lives. A door that opens and
closes six times a day reaches 10,000 cycles in under five years. The 2000s
subdivisions in Wesley Chapel, Riverview, Port St. Lucie and Clermont are
reaching their first spring failures now, and the doors installed together
in Pembroke Pines and Coral Springs in the 1990s are on their second or
third set. Our article on
[how long garage door springs last](/blog/how-long-do-garage-door-springs-last/)
covers the cycle math.

**Salt and humidity.** Within a few miles of either coast, salt air pits the
wire. A pitted spring fails early, and it usually fails with a bang. In Cape
Coral, Fort Lauderdale, Clearwater, Jupiter and Melbourne we see springs
rusted along the whole coil that are nowhere near their cycle life. Our
article on [salt air corrosion](/blog/salt-air-garage-door-corrosion/)
covers what to do about it.

The doors rebuilt after Hurricane Andrew in 1993 across Kendall and
Homestead are now more than thirty years old. Many are on their original
springs, which is remarkable, and it means the failure is overdue rather
than surprising.

## One spring or two

If your door has two springs and one broke, the other has cycled exactly the
same number of times and has sat in the same humidity. It is at the same
point in its life as the one that just failed and it typically follows
within months.

That makes replacing both a genuine recommendation rather than an upsell,
and there is a simple test for whether you are being told the truth: an
honest company will do just the one if you ask, and will tell you what it
costs to come back later. For us that is $150 to $350 again with a second
visit, against $120 to $250 added today.

## Torsion or extension

Most Florida doors installed since the 1990s run a torsion spring on a shaft
above the door. Older single doors in Hialeah, older Kendall, Hollywood east
of I-95 and older St. Petersburg sometimes run a pair of extension springs
along the horizontal tracks. Both are replaced within the same price range.
On an extension spring door we fit safety cables through the springs if they
are missing, because an extension spring that snaps without one becomes a
projectile. Our comparison of
[torsion versus extension springs](/blog/torsion-vs-extension-springs/)
explains the difference.

## What a fair quote looks like

Before anyone is dispatched, you should be able to get:

- A price range for your specific door, not a national average
- Whether the price is for one spring or two
- What happens if cables or drums turn out to be damaged too
- The call-out premium, if any, stated separately
- The warranty on parts and labor, which for us is one year on each

If a company will not give you a number until a technician is standing in
your driveway, that is information about how the rest of the visit is likely
to go. Our guide to [what a fair quote includes](/blog/what-a-fair-quote-includes/)
covers the rest.

## What not to do

Do not run the opener on a broken spring. The springs carry the weight of
the door, not the motor. Forcing the opener strains it, bends panels, and can
drop the door. If the car is inside and you need it, read
[what to do about a broken spring](/blog/what-to-do-broken-spring/) first.

Do not attempt the replacement yourself. A wound torsion spring stores
enough energy to break bones and it releases instantly if it is not unwound
under control with proper winding bars.
