## Why the price range is so wide

Getting a door back on its track is not the expensive part. The expensive
part is whatever got bent in the process.

A door that jumped the track and was left alone is usually an hour of work:
release the tension, re-seat the rollers, check the track, re-tension, test.
That sits at the bottom of the range.

A door that came off and was then run with the opener several more times is
a different job. Track gets bent, rollers get destroyed, the bottom section
gets creased, and the cable that probably caused it in the first place is
now wrapped around the drum.

## The single most useful thing you can do

Stop pressing the button.

Every additional cycle after a door comes off the track converts a
straightforward repair into an expensive one. The opener does not know the
door is off; it will keep pulling until something gives, and on a wind-rated
door with struts the thing that gives is usually the track bracket or the
hinge, not the motor.

Pull the manual release cord, leave the door where it is, and call someone.
Our [manual release guide](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/) shows
where the cord is. If the door is stuck open and the house is exposed, or a
storm warning is in effect, say so when you call, because that is a genuine
emergency and it gets treated as one. Our article on
[what to do when a door is off track](/blog/garage-door-off-track/) covers
the first ten minutes.

## What causes it in Florida

**A rusted cable snapping.** One side loses support, the door lifts crooked
and the rollers on the low side pop out. This is the most common cause we
see, and here it is almost always rust at the bottom bracket rather than
plain wear. Salt air on either coast and daily afternoon rain at the bottom
seal corrode the cable where it anchors. Our
[cable repair cost guide](/blog/garage-door-cable-repair-cost/) explains why
a frayed cable at $95 to $300 is worth fixing before it becomes this job.

**Impact.** A vehicle bumping the door, even lightly, knocks the track out of
alignment. The tight townhouse driveways in Doral and the newer Kendall
subdivisions, and the golf cart traffic in 55 plus communities, produce a
steady stream of these. The door may run for weeks afterwards before it
derails.

**Storm pressure.** During a tropical storm or hurricane the door flexes
inward under wind load, the rollers climb out of the track on the gust, and
the door drops back crooked. After a named storm this is the most common
call we take, often with bent struts and twisted track as well. That is a
[storm damage assessment](/services/storm-damage-garage-door-repair/) rather
than a plain off-track job, and our
[storm damage guide](/blog/garage-door-storm-damage-what-to-do/) explains
what happens on that visit.

**Worn or seized rollers.** A roller that will not turn drags in the track.
Over months it wears the track and eventually climbs out of it. Steel
rollers rust in this climate; nylon ones do not, and our
[roller replacement guide](/blog/garage-door-roller-replacement-guide/)
covers the swap.

**Track alignment.** Loose track brackets let the track spread under load.
On a concrete block garage the lag bolts into the block can work loose over
twenty years of vibration, and if your door has been noisy and rough for a
while, this is often why.

## What should happen afterwards

Re-seating the door is the middle of the job, not the end of it. The visit
should finish with the cause identified and dealt with, the track checked
along its full length and its fixings tightened, the rollers inspected, the
cables and bottom brackets checked on both sides, and a balance test with
the opener disconnected.

If nobody tells you why the door came off, it will come off again. If the
cause was a rusted cable, both cables are replaced, because the other one is
the same age and has sat in the same salt. If the cause was a storm, the
struts and the rating hardware are checked, because a door that has flexed
once under wind may not hold the next time.
