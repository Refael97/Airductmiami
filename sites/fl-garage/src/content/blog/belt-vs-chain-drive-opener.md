## The one question

Is there a bedroom or living space beside or above the garage?

**Yes:** belt drive. **No:** chain drive.

That decides it for the large majority of Florida homes, and everything below is detail.

## The comparison

| | Chain | Belt |
|---|---|---|
| Installed price | $275 to $450 | $350 to $600 |
| Noise | Loud | Very quiet |
| Maintenance | Light lubrication once or twice a year | None on the belt |
| Humid salt air | Surface rust if neglected | No effect |
| 100°F garage | Fine | Fine |
| Durability | Highest | High |
| Typical life | 10 to 15 years | 10 to 15 years |
| Battery backup | Optional, $60 to $150 | Usually included or $60 to $150 |
| Best for | Detached garages, workshops | Attached garages, living space nearby |

## Why the noise difference is so big

A chain drive is metal chain running against a metal rail. The noise is not just airborne, it is vibration transmitted through the mounting into the trusses and the block. In a room that shares a wall or a ceiling with the garage you feel it as much as hear it.

A belt is steel-reinforced rubber. It does not clatter and it transmits far less vibration into the framing. The mechanism is otherwise identical.

That structural transmission is why isolating the mounting helps a chain drive but never fully fixes it, and why people who try to solve chain noise with rubber isolators usually end up buying a belt drive anyway.

## Where this matters most in Florida

Almost every Florida house has an attached garage, and the bedroom next to it is common in both housing eras.

In a single-story ranch, the kind built across Hialeah, Hollywood, St. Petersburg and older Orlando from the 1950s to the 1970s, the garage typically shares a wall with a bedroom or the kitchen. A chain drive on the other side of that wall is a decision people regret.

In two-story builds from the 1990s onward, in Weston, Wesley Chapel, Lake Nona, Parkland and most of the newer subdivisions, the bonus room or a bedroom sits directly over the garage. That is the loudest possible place to put a chain drive.

In a detached garage, a workshop, or a golf cart garage where nobody sleeps nearby, a chain drive is fine and it is the better value.

## What heat and humidity do to each

The summer garage in Florida reaches 100°F and more. That heat does not bother a belt, which is steel cord inside rubber, and it does not bother a chain. What it does bother is the electronics. Logic boards and capacitors age faster in a hot garage, and the plastic drive gear in older units softens and strips. Those failures happen on both drive types and they are the reason [opener repair](/services/garage-door-opener-repair/) at $95 to $300 is a summer job for us. Our article on [summer heat problems](/blog/summer-heat-garage-door-problems-florida/) covers the pattern.

Humidity is where the two differ. Within a few miles of either coast a chain that is never touched picks up surface rust, gets noisier, and wears the sprocket. A wipe of light lubricant once or twice a year, the same visit as a [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/), keeps it fine. A belt needs nothing.

## What the extra money buys

The premium is roughly $75 to $150.

For a garage beside or under a bedroom, that is one of the highest value upgrades available anywhere in a house. For a detached garage, it buys you almost nothing.

## Battery backup, which matters more here than anywhere

Power goes out in Florida. After a named storm it can stay out for days, and a heavy door with no power means pulling the manual release and lifting by hand, which on an impact-rated double door is real work.

Battery backup adds $60 to $150 to any opener and gives you dozens of cycles on a charge. It is included on most belt drives now. We recommend it on every attached garage in the state, and our article on [battery backup and power outages](/blog/garage-door-battery-backup-power-outage/) explains how long a battery actually lasts and how to test it before hurricane season. Learn the [manual release](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/) either way.

## The other drive types, briefly

**Screw drive**, $300 to $500. Few moving parts and strong lift, but the lubricant on the screw thins in heat and thickens when it cools, so it is temperature sensitive in a way that shows up in a hot garage. See the [screw drive guide](/buyers-guide/screw-drive-openers/).

**Direct drive**, $400 to $600. The motor rides the rail, so there is a single moving part. As quiet as a belt with usually a longer warranty. Fewer models available. See the [direct drive guide](/buyers-guide/direct-drive-openers/).

**Jackshaft, wall mount**, $450 to $800. Mounts beside the door on the torsion shaft and needs almost no headroom. Not really a competitor to the others: it is the answer for a low headroom garage in an older single-door house, or when you want the ceiling clear for storage. See the [jackshaft guide](/buyers-guide/jackshaft-wall-mount-openers/) and our article on [low headroom openers](/blog/low-headroom-garage-door-openers/).

## Features to consider alongside

**WiFi control.** Check whether you left it open, and let a delivery in. A [smart opener](/services/smart-garage-door-opener-installation/) installed runs $350 to $750, or a controller can be added to an existing opener for less. Our article on [whether a smart opener is worth it](/blog/smart-garage-door-opener-worth-it/) goes through the trade-offs.

**Soft start and stop.** Reduces both noise and wear on the door hardware. Standard on most belt and direct drives.

**Brand.** [LiftMaster](/brands/liftmaster/) and [Chamberlain](/brands/chamberlain/) make both drive types with battery backup; [Genie](/brands/genie/) belt drives are also common here. The drive type matters more than the badge.
