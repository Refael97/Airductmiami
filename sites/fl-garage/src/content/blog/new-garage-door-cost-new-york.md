## What drives the price

Four things, in order of impact.

**Size.** A sixteen foot double is not two singles. It is a bigger, heavier product with a heavier track and spring system.

**Material.** Steel is cheapest, then vinyl, then fiberglass, then aluminum, then wood. Custom wood is a different category entirely.

**Insulation.** An uninsulated single-skin door is the cheapest thing on the shelf. A polyurethane-cored insulated door costs more and is the right answer whenever the garage touches living space.

**Windows and decorative hardware.** Glass sections and carriage hardware add real cost, and glass reduces the insulation value of the door.

## The line items people do not expect

**A new opener.** An existing opener usually works with a new door provided the door is a similar weight. A heavier insulated door on an old opener is a common source of complaint six months later.

**New springs.** The spring is sized to the door weight. A new door that weighs more than the old one needs a new spring, and that is legitimate rather than an upsell.

**New track.** Reusing twenty year old track on a new door is false economy and most installers will refuse.

**Disposal.** Normally included, but confirm it. A garage door is an awkward thing to get rid of yourself.

**Structural work.** Rare, but a rotted header or a non-square opening has to be dealt with before a door goes in.

## Insulation, and whether you need it

The measure is R-value, and higher is better. An uninsulated door is effectively R-0 to R-2. A decent polyurethane-cored door is R-12 to R-18.

It matters when the garage is attached to living space, when there is a room above it, or when you use the garage as a workshop. It matters much less on a detached garage you park in.

Insulation also has a side effect worth knowing: an insulated door is stiffer and quieter in use, and it dents less easily.

## When a repair is the better spend

A new door is a large purchase and it is not always the right one. If the sections are sound and only the springs, cables or rollers have failed, a repair at $170 to $400 buys you years. We say that even though the replacement is worth several times more to us.

Replace when the sections are rusted through, when a damaged panel cannot be matched because the profile is discontinued, when the garage is attached and the door has no insulation, or when the repair cost is approaching half the price of a new door.
