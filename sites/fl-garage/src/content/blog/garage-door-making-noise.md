## Match the sound to the cause

**Grinding.** Metal on metal without lubrication. Most often a roller bearing that has rusted or run dry and is sliding in the track rather than rolling. Sometimes a worn end bearing plate on the torsion shaft. Both are cheap now and expensive later.

**Squealing or screeching.** Dry hinges and rollers. This is the noise that responds best to lubrication, and it is the most common reason people book a tune-up.

**A single loud bang.** A spring or a cable letting go. If the door then will not open, it is the spring. Stop using the opener. Our guide on [a loud bang from the garage door](/blog/loud-bang-garage-door/) covers what to do next.

**Popping or snapping on the way up.** Section hinges binding as the door articulates around the curve of the track. Usually dry hinges, sometimes cracked ones, occasionally a door that is out of square.

**Rattling.** Loose hardware. A garage door vibrates every time it runs, bolts work loose over years, and in a block wall the lag bolts holding the track brackets can loosen as well. Tightening everything takes twenty minutes and prevents a surprising amount.

**A rumble you feel through the wall.** Chain drive noise transmitted into the structure. This is not a fault, it is what chain drives do.

**Straining or humming with no movement.** The motor is powered but something between it and the door has failed: a stripped gear kit, a broken trolley, or a door so out of balance the opener cannot lift it. Our guide on [an opener that runs but the door does not move](/blog/opener-runs-door-doesnt-move/) covers that one.

## Why Florida doors get loud

Three things here work against quiet hardware.

The first is humidity. A hinge pin or a roller bearing that would stay lubricated for two years in a dry climate rusts in one here, and once there is rust in the bearing the roller stops rolling and starts scraping. Within a few miles of the coast, salt air makes that faster again.

![Worn garage door rollers pulled from a coastal Florida door: rusted bearings, flat spots on the wheels, and one stem corroded in the hinge. Rollers like these grind in the track and eventually climb out of it.](/images/worn-garage-door-rollers.webp)

The second is heat. A garage that sits above 100°F all summer thins out spray lubricant and dries it faster, so a door lubricated in the spring can be squealing by August.

The third is use. A Florida garage door is the front door, the door to the storage and the door to the workshop. In golf cart communities it can cycle several times a day. More cycles means more wear on every moving part, and a door that runs four times a day reaches the point where it needs attention twice as fast as one that runs twice. Our guide on [summer heat problems](/blog/summer-heat-garage-door-problems-florida/) covers the rest of what the season does.

## The five minute check

With the door closed, pull the manual release and move it by hand.

If it is quiet by hand and loud with the opener, the noise is the opener.

If it is loud by hand, walk the door slowly and listen. You can usually put a finger on the exact roller or hinge making the sound. Look for rust streaks below a hinge, a roller that does not turn when the door moves, or a hinge with a crack at the knuckle.

## What to lubricate, and what not to

Use a garage door silicone or white lithium spray. Not WD-40, which is a solvent and displaces the lubricant that is already there, and not grease, which collects sand.

**Do lubricate:** hinge pivot points, roller bearings, the torsion spring itself, the end bearing plates, and the opener rail if it is a chain or screw drive.

**Do not lubricate:** the track. Track needs to be clean and dry. Grease in the track collects the sand and grit that blows into every Florida garage and turns it into an abrasive paste that wears rollers faster than no lubricant at all.

Do it twice a year. The best times are March to May, before the season, and again after hurricane season ends in November. Our step by step on [how to lubricate a garage door](/blog/how-to-lubricate-a-garage-door/) has the full sequence.

## Opener noise into the house

Almost every Florida house is single story with the garage attached, which means a bedroom or a living room shares a wall with the opener. A chain drive bolted to the ceiling joists sends its vibration straight through that wall.

If the door hardware is quiet by hand and the noise is the opener, you have two options. Rubber isolation mounts between the motor unit and the ceiling take some of it out. A belt drive takes almost all of it out, and if the opener is over ten years old and has spent those years in a hot garage, replacing it is often the better spend than quieting it. Our guide on [belt versus chain drive](/blog/belt-vs-chain-drive-opener/) compares them.

## When to call

Call when the noise is grinding rather than squealing, when there is a new noise at one specific point in the travel, when the door has started running [crooked](/blog/garage-door-crooked-uneven/), or when lubrication has stopped helping.
