## The measurement that decides it

Measure from the top of the door opening to the lowest thing in the way above it. Not the ceiling height. The lowest obstruction, which in a Florida garage is often a storage rack, a water heater platform, an air handler, a plumbing run or the front edge of a loft someone built to make up for the basement the house does not have.

| Clearance available | What fits |
|---|---|
| 14 inches or more | Any standard rail opener |
| 10 to 14 inches | Standard opener with a low headroom track kit on the door |
| 8 to 10 inches | Low profile rail opener |
| Under 8 inches | Wall mounted jackshaft opener |

That table is the whole decision. Everything below explains why the number is so often small here and what each option costs.

## Why this comes up so often in Florida

Almost every Florida house has an attached garage on a slab, and a large share of the older ones were built before anyone expected a motor to hang above the door.

**1950s to 1970s ranch houses.** Hialeah, older Kendall, Hollywood east of I-95, the older neighborhoods of Fort Lauderdale, St. Petersburg and the older parts of Orlando are full of single car garages from this era. The header sits close to the ceiling, the opening is often 8 feet wide and sometimes under 7 feet high, and a flat roof or a low pitched one leaves very little above the door. Our [Miami-Dade County](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) and [Broward County](/service-areas/broward-county/) pages name the neighborhoods where we see this every week.

**Converted and re-enclosed garages.** Hialeah in particular has a great many garages that were converted to living space and then partly converted back, or carports that were enclosed later. The framing is rarely to a standard and the clearance above the opening is whatever the builder had left.

**Storage lofts and overhead racks.** This is the modern version of the problem and it shows up in 1990s and 2000s garages in Coral Springs, Pembroke Pines, Brandon and Wesley Chapel as much as in old ones. With no basement, the garage is the storage room, and a loft or an overhead rack built across the front of the garage takes the clearance a rail opener needs. Sometimes the rack was installed after the opener and now blocks the door's travel.

**St. Petersburg alley garages.** Detached garages from the 1920s to 1960s, often with a low flat roof and a door that was replaced once in the 1980s. There is usually room for a torsion shaft and not much else.

## The jackshaft answer

![A wall mounted jackshaft garage door opener bolted to the wall beside the torsion shaft, with no rail across the ceiling.](/images/jackshaft-wall-mount-opener.webp)

A jackshaft opener bolts to the wall beside the door and turns the torsion shaft directly. There is no rail, no motor head hanging from the ceiling and no bracket on the top section of the door. The whole ceiling stays free, which is the reason plenty of people with fourteen inches of clearance choose one anyway.

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| Installed price | $450 to $800 |
| Headroom needed | A few inches beside the shaft |
| Spring type required | Torsion only |
| Noise | Quiet, nothing running across the ceiling |
| Usually includes | An automatic deadbolt on the door, and battery backup on many models |

Its requirements are real. It needs torsion springs, because it drives the shaft they sit on. It needs side room at one end of the shaft, usually 8 inches or so, where the unit mounts. And it needs a door that is properly balanced, because it drives the shaft directly and a heavy door will trip its force limits immediately. If the door has extension springs, the conversion to torsion is part of the job and should be on the quote before we arrive, not discovered on the day. Our guide to [torsion versus extension springs](/blog/torsion-vs-extension-springs/) explains how to tell which you have in thirty seconds.

A jackshaft also suits the Florida garage in one more way. Because nothing hangs above the door, a storage loft or an overhead rack can stay where it is, provided it sits behind the door's open position rather than in its path.

## Low headroom track kits

A separate thing from the opener, and often confused with it. A low headroom track kit changes the geometry of the door's own track so the door needs less clearance to make the turn from vertical to horizontal. It uses a double horizontal track or a rear mounted torsion setup so the top section can start curving sooner.

It can buy you several inches, which is sometimes enough to make a standard rail opener work in a garage that measured 10 or 11 inches. It costs more than standard track, and the spring system has to be reviewed at the same time because rear mounted torsion changes how the cables run. On a door that is already due for replacement, it is usually better to specify low headroom track on the new door than to retrofit it to the old one. Our [standard garage door sizes](/blog/standard-garage-door-sizes/) guide covers the four measurements a new door needs, and headroom is one of them.

## Low profile rail openers

Between 8 and 10 inches, a standard style opener with a low profile rail can fit. The rail sits closer to the door and the motor head mounts tighter to the ceiling. These are ordinary chain or belt openers in the $275 to $600 range, and they install like any other; the difference is the rail kit. They still need a clear path for the rail and a place for the motor head, so a loft or a rack across the front of the garage rules them out just as it rules out a standard opener. Our [belt versus chain drive](/blog/belt-vs-chain-drive-opener/) guide covers the choice between the two.

## Heat, power and the Florida opener

Two things matter more here than in most of the country and they apply to every option above.

**Heat.** A closed Florida garage reaches 100°F and more in summer, and it is the logic board and the plastic gears that fail first. A jackshaft mounts on the wall, lower than a ceiling unit, which puts it a little further from the hottest air under the roof. That is a small advantage, not a reason to choose one. Our article on [summer heat and garage door problems](/blog/summer-heat-garage-door-problems-florida/) covers what heat actually does to an opener.

**Power outages.** After a storm the power goes out and the door still has to open. Battery backup adds $60 to $150 on any opener and it is available on most jackshaft models. If you can only justify one upgrade, choose battery backup over WiFi, and read our guide to [battery backup and power outages](/blog/garage-door-battery-backup-power-outage/) before you decide. Whichever opener you choose, know where the [manual release](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/) is before you need it.
