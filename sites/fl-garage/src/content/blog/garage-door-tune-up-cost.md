## What it should include

A tune-up that costs $85 and takes twenty minutes is worth what you paid.
Here is the full list of what a real one covers.

**Balance test.** Disconnect the opener, lift the door halfway by hand, and
let go. It should stay put. If it drops or rises, the spring is not matched
to the door and everything else is downstream of that.

**Cables and drums.** Check for fraying at the drum, rust at the bottom
bracket, and even tension on both sides. In Florida this is the check that
finds the most, because a rusting cable at the bottom bracket is the most
common visible warning on any door here.

**Rollers.** Check every roller turns freely. Seized rollers drag, wear the
track, and eventually derail the door. Nylon rollers do not rust and run
quieter, which matters on the coast.

**Hinges and brackets.** Check for cracks and rust, particularly at the end
hinges which carry the roller load, and at the bottom brackets which sit in
water for half the year.

**Hardware.** A garage door vibrates every time it runs. Bolts work loose.
On a wind-rated door the strut bolts and track fixings are what hold the
door in the opening under pressure, so tightening them is not cosmetic.

**Track alignment.** Check the track is plumb, evenly spaced and firmly
fixed to the jamb along its full length.

**Lubrication.** Springs, hinges, rollers, bearings and the opener rail. Not
the track itself, which should be clean and dry. Our guide to
[how to lubricate a garage door](/blog/how-to-lubricate-a-garage-door/)
covers what to use between visits.

**Safety sensors.** Check alignment and mounting height. They belong no more
than six inches above the floor, which is the height at which they will
actually catch a child or a pet.

**Opener force and travel.** Check the door reverses on contact and that the
travel limits stop it in the right places. Test the battery backup if the
opener has one, because a backup that has quietly died is found on the
night the power goes out.

**Weather seal.** Check the bottom seal is intact and sitting flat. The
seal is what keeps daily afternoon rain off the bottom section and the
bottom brackets.

## Why it is the best value item on the list

Almost every emergency call we take has a cheaper version of itself that
happened months earlier and was ignored.

A grinding noise is a dry roller bearing, which is a small part replaced
during the visit. Left alone, it seizes, drags in the track, and takes the
door off it. That is now a $140 to $600 off-track job.

A slightly frayed cable is a $95 to $300 replacement. Left alone, it snaps,
the door drops on one side, and the repair becomes the off-track job above
plus the cable.

A door that has gone slightly out of balance is a spring adjustment. Left
alone, the opener compensates for months, then the board or the gear gives
up in the July heat, and that is $95 to $300 to repair or $275 to $600 to
replace. Our article on
[summer heat and garage doors](/blog/summer-heat-garage-door-problems-florida/)
explains why Florida openers die in August.

## Timing it: April or May

The most useful time to have this done in Florida is April or May, and the
reason is the calendar. Hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November.
A door that has been checked in the spring goes into the season with tight
track fixings, sound hinges, an intact seal and a working battery backup.
A door that has not been checked finds out what was loose during the first
tropical storm.

The spring visit also comes before the summer heat, which is when openers
fail, and it is the window in which a
[bracing kit](/blog/garage-door-hurricane-bracing-kits/) or a
[new rated door](/blog/best-time-to-replace-garage-door-florida/) can still
be ordered and installed before June. If the tune-up finds that your door
has no wind rating at all, that is the month to hear it.

Seasonal residents in Naples, Bonita Springs, Venice and Palm Beach Gardens
should book the visit for the week before they leave in the spring, or the
week they return in November. A door that sits closed for six months in
coastal humidity is the door most likely to have a rusted cable waiting.

## Coastal doors: twice a year

Within a few miles of either coast, once a year is not enough. Salt air
works on the cable, the spring coil, the hinges and the bottom brackets all
year, and the difference between a spring and an autumn check is often the
difference between a cable replaced and a door on the floor. Cape Coral,
Fort Lauderdale east of US 1, Clearwater, Pompano Beach, Jupiter, Melbourne,
St. Augustine and Palm Coast are all in that band. Our article on
[rust and Florida humidity](/blog/garage-door-rust-florida-humidity/) covers
the between-visit rinse that slows it down.
