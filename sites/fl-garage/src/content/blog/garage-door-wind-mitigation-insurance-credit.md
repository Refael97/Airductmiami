## What the inspection is

Florida homeowners insurance carriers use a standard form, OIR-B1-1802, to record the features of a house that reduce hurricane damage. The roof shape, the roof deck attachment, the roof to wall connection, the secondary water barrier, and the protection on openings all get a line. Your garage door lives in the opening protection section.

The form exists because Florida's mitigation discount rules require insurers to give credit for features that reduce wind loss. The form is the evidence. Without it, the insurer has no verified record and applies no credit.

The inspection is done by a licensed inspector, and it is your appointment to book. We install doors and pull permits. We do not fill out the form, and you should be wary of any garage door company that says a door purchase comes with a guaranteed discount. It does not. It comes with documentation, and documentation is what may earn the credit.

## The opening protection section

Every opening in the exterior of the house counts: windows, sliding glass doors, entry doors, skylights, and the garage door. The inspector records what protects each one. Impact-rated glass, approved shutters, approved panels, and a rated garage door are all forms of protection.

The section is scored on the whole house, and in practice the weakest opening sets the result. A rated garage door on a house with unprotected windows helps the door line but does not move the overall category. A rated garage door on a house that already has impact windows or shutters everywhere else can be the last piece.

The garage door is the largest opening in the house, which is why it is treated as structural in the Florida Building Code. When it fails in a storm the house pressurizes and the roof is at risk. That same logic is why the form cares about it, and why an unprotected garage door is a common reason a house does not reach the better opening protection category.

## How a rated garage door is recorded

The inspector is looking for proof, not for a nice looking door. Proof means one of these:

| What the inspector checks | Where it is |
|---|---|
| Manufacturer's rating label | Inside of an end stile or the top section |
| Florida Product Approval number (FL number) | On the label and on the permit |
| Miami-Dade NOA number, in the HVHZ | On the label and on the permit |
| Building permit for the door | County building department record |
| Installation invoice naming the door model | Your copy from us |

The label is the primary evidence. It is a small metal or foil plate riveted or stuck to the door at the factory, and it names the manufacturer, the model, and the approval number. If the label is gone, painted over, or unreadable, the inspector falls back on the permit record and the invoice. If none of those exist, the door is recorded as unrated, even if it happens to be a good one.

This is the reason a permitted installation matters for insurance and not only for the code. A door installed without a permit has no county record, and the inspector cannot verify a rating from a receipt alone. We cover that side in [garage door permits in Florida](/blog/garage-door-permit-florida/).

A wind-rated door and an impact-rated door are recorded differently. A wind-rated door resists design pressure. An impact-rated door is also tested against large missile impact, and that is what most inspectors and most insurers mean when they ask whether the garage door is protected. The distinction is explained in [wind-rated vs impact-rated garage doors](/blog/wind-rated-vs-impact-rated-garage-door/).

## What "may qualify" means

We use the words "may qualify" on purpose, and we ask you to hold any company to the same standard.

A rated door can contribute to a premium credit. Whether it does, and by how much, depends on your insurer's credit schedule, the other features on your form, your policy, and your county. Two neighbors with the same door can see different results because one has impact windows and the other does not.

So the honest sequence is:

1. Ask your insurer for its credit schedule before you buy, and ask specifically what opening protection is worth on your policy.
2. Install a rated door with a permit and keep the paperwork.
3. Book a wind mitigation inspection and give the inspector the paperwork.
4. Submit the completed form to your insurer.

We never promise a dollar figure, and the price of the door is the same whether or not the credit comes through. That price is on [our price list](/prices/): [hurricane-rated door installation](/services/hurricane-rated-garage-door-installation/) from $1,800 to $6,500 installed with the permit included in the written quote, and a standard [wind-rated steel door](/services/new-garage-door-installation/) from $950 to $4,200.

## The My Safe Florida Home program

The state's My Safe Florida Home program has, when funded, offered inspections and grants toward opening protection, and garage doors have been within its scope. The program opens and closes with its funding, and its rules on eligibility, matching, and which improvements count have changed between rounds.

We do not track its status on this page because it changes faster than we can keep an article current. Check the Florida Department of Financial Services for whether the program is open, what it covers this round, and how to apply. If it is open and you qualify, the program typically wants the same documentation the wind mitigation inspection wants, so the list below serves both.

## What to keep from an installation

Keep these together in one folder, physical or digital, and keep them for as long as you own the house:

- The written quote and the final invoice, naming the door manufacturer and model
- The permit number and the county's final inspection record
- A photograph of the rating label, taken on installation day while it is clean and readable
- The manufacturer's product approval sheet, the FL number outside the HVHZ or the Miami-Dade NOA inside it
- Our one year parts and one year labor warranty as written on the invoice

We give you the first four on the day the door goes in. The photograph is the one people skip and later regret. Labels fade in a Florida garage, and an inspector five years from now cannot read a faded label.

## The HVHZ difference

In Miami-Dade and Broward counties the door must carry a Miami-Dade NOA, and an inspector there will look for the NOA number specifically. A door with only a Florida Product Approval is not the right document in the [High Velocity Hurricane Zone](/blog/hvhz-garage-doors-explained/), and it will not be permitted there in the first place. If you are in [Miami-Dade County](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) or [Broward County](/service-areas/broward-county/), the paperwork you keep is the NOA.

Outside the HVHZ, including [Southwest Florida](/service-areas/southwest-florida/) where so many doors were replaced after Ian, the FL number is the document. Many doors carry both, which is fine.

## Where bracing kits fit

Retrofit bracing kits have Florida Product Approval in most of the state. Whether an inspector records a braced door as protected depends on the kit's approval, whether it was installed as approved, and whether the inspector can verify it. Some insurers accept it and some do not.

If insurance is part of your reason for a kit, ask your insurer first, because the answer decides whether the kit is worth buying. In the HVHZ the answer is simpler: the accepted route is a rated door. We go through the details in [garage door hurricane bracing kits](/blog/garage-door-hurricane-bracing-kits/).

## A short version to keep

The inspection form records what protects each opening. A rated garage door is recorded from its label and its permit. It may qualify for a credit, and the credit is your insurer's to set. Keep the paperwork, photograph the label, and ask your insurer for the schedule before you spend money expecting a result.
