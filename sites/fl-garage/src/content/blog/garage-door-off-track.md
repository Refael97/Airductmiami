## The most useful thing to know

The re-seating is not the expensive part. What got bent is.

A door that jumped the track and was left alone is roughly an hour of work: release the spring tension, re-seat the rollers, check the track, re-tension, test. That is the $140 to $300 end of the range.

A door that came off and was then run four more times because somebody kept trying is a different job. The track is bent, the rollers are destroyed, the bottom section is creased, and the cable is wound around the drum. That is the $600 end, and if a section has to be replaced it goes past that.

The difference between those two outcomes is entirely about what happens in the first five minutes.

![A garage door with its bottom rollers out of the vertical track, the section hanging at an angle and the cable slack on that side. This is the point to stop and leave it alone.](/images/garage-door-off-track.webp)

## What to do

1. **Stop pressing the button.** The opener does not know the door is off. It will keep pulling.
2. **Pull the manual release** only if the door is closed or nearly closed. If the door is up and partly derailed, leave the release alone: disconnecting it can let the door drop.
3. **Keep people, pets and cars away from it.** A partially derailed door can come down without warning.
4. **Note what happened just before.** A bang, a car bumping it, a storm, a cable that snapped. It tells us where to look.
5. **Say whether it is open or closed when you call.** That decides whether this is an emergency.

Our guide on [the manual release](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/) explains which cord to pull and when not to.

## What causes it in Florida

**A rusted or broken cable.** The most common cause we see across the state. The cable rusts at the bottom bracket, where water from afternoon storms sits against it for half the year, and salt air near the coast speeds it up. One side loses support, the door lifts crooked, and the low side rollers pop out of the track. A [crooked door](/blog/garage-door-crooked-uneven/) is the warning stage.

**Impact.** A car bumping the door, even at walking speed, knocks the track out of alignment. On tight townhouse driveways in [Doral](/service-areas/doral/) or [Kendall](/service-areas/kendall/) this is a regular call. The door often keeps running for weeks before it finally derails, which is why the connection gets missed.

**Seized rollers.** A roller whose bearing has rusted solid does not turn. It drags in the track, wears it, and eventually climbs out. Humidity does this to steel rollers faster here than anywhere else in the country.

**Loose track brackets.** Track brackets are lag bolted into the wall framing or, in a block house, into the block itself. Rust and vibration loosen them, the track spreads under load, and the rollers have room to leave it. Doors that have never had a tune-up are the usual candidates.

**Storm pressure.** Wind loading during a named storm flexes the door in and out and can push the track brackets and the door out of line. Doors that survived the storm often come off the track the first time they are run afterward.

**Something in the way.** A door closing onto a bin, a bike or a bumper it cannot crush lifts on one side, the cable goes slack, and the rollers come out.

## After a storm

Off-track doors after a named storm are their own category. Panels are bent inward by pressure, tracks are twisted, and doors have been pulled off their tracks by wind loading. We assess the door, brace or secure the opening so the house is closed, and quote the repair or replacement in writing.

The insurance claim is yours. We give you the photos and the written assessment your adjuster needs, and we do not inflate storm repairs, because that is the complaint people have after every hurricane. If the door needs replacing, it needs a permit and a door rated for your site's wind pressure, which our guide on [what to do after storm damage](/blog/garage-door-storm-damage-what-to-do/) explains. In [Miami-Dade](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) and [Broward](/service-areas/broward-county/) that means a door with a Miami-Dade NOA.

## What a proper repair includes

Getting the door back on the track is the middle of the job. The visit should end with:

- The cause identified and fixed, not just the symptom
- The full length of both tracks checked for straightness and fixing
- Every roller inspected and the failed ones replaced, with nylon rollers offered if you are near the coast
- Both cables checked and re-tensioned evenly, replaced as a pair if either shows rust
- A balance test with the opener disconnected
- Panels checked for creasing at the section joints
- Opener travel limits and force reset, because they will have been thrown off

If nobody tells you why it came off, it is going to come off again. A yearly [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) catches the rusting cable, the seized roller and the loose bracket before any of them puts the door on the floor.
