## What you heard

People describe it as a gunshot, a car hitting the house, or something heavy falling over in the garage. It is loud, singular, and it comes from the garage, often at 6 in the morning when the door is first used or during an afternoon storm when everyone assumes it was thunder.

What happened is that a wound torsion spring, holding a couple of hundred pounds of stored energy, reached the end of its cycle life and let go. The energy discharges against the shaft in a fraction of a second. The gap in the coil is usually visible from the floor: a clean break with the two halves separated by an inch or two.

## The next ten minutes

**Do not press the opener button.** The springs lift the door, not the motor. Running the opener now will strain the motor, strip the gear, crease the top section, and can pull the door off the track. Every press makes the repair bigger.

**Look at the spring.** It runs along the shaft above the door opening. A broken one has an obvious gap. If you can see that gap, you have your answer. If you have extension springs, which run alongside the horizontal tracks on many older single doors in Hialeah, Hollywood and St. Petersburg, a broken one is stretched, snapped or hanging.

**Leave the door where it is if it is closed.** This is the good outcome. Your car may be trapped, but nothing is going to get worse overnight, and the house is secure.

**If the door is open**, be careful. Without spring support it is dead weight, 130 to 350 pounds depending on the door. Two people can usually bring it down slowly with hands flat on the bottom section and clear of the joints.

**Do not pull the manual release with the door up.** Pulling the release on a raised door with a broken spring lets it drop.

## What else makes a bang

Most of the time it is the spring. These are the others, and how to tell.

**A lift cable snapping.** A sharper, lighter crack. The door drops on one side, hangs crooked, and may jam in the tracks. Rust on a cable is the most common visible warning we see in Florida, and a rusted cable frays strand by strand until it goes. [Cable repair](/services/garage-door-cable-repair/) runs $95 to $300, or $225 to $400 with the drum.

![A frayed garage door lift cable at the bottom bracket, the failure that makes a sharp crack and leaves the door hanging crooked.](/images/frayed-lift-cable.webp)

**The opener header bracket tearing out.** The bracket that holds the opener rail to the wall above the door pulls its lag bolts out of the header, often because the door was already heavy and the opener had been fighting it. The rail drops and hangs. This happens on older CBS houses where the bolts went into a wood buck that has softened with humidity.

**A roller jumping the track.** A bang followed by the door sitting crooked or leaning out of the track on one side. That is an [off-track repair](/services/garage-door-off-track-repair/) from $140, and our guide to a [door off its track](/blog/garage-door-off-track/) explains why you should not touch it.

**Thunder, a transformer, or a neighbor's shutters.** In a Florida summer, a bang during a storm has plenty of candidates. The test is simple: try the wall button the next morning. If the door opens normally, it was not the door.

## Both springs, and why

If your door has two springs, they were installed together and have taken the same number of cycles. The one that did not break is at the same point in its life as the one that did, and in practice it usually follows within a few months.

Replacing both at once adds $120 to $250 rather than doubling the price, because the visit and most of the labor are shared. It is one of the few recommendations in this trade that is arithmetic rather than sales.

You can decline it. A company that will not let you decline it is telling you something.

## Why springs break in Florida

Springs fail on cycles, not on age alone, and never on cold here. A standard spring is rated for about 10,000 open and close cycles, which is seven to ten years for a light user. A Florida family door cycles six to ten times a day because the garage is the storage room and the second front door, and that reaches 10,000 in five to seven years. In golf cart communities like The Villages the count climbs just as fast.

Humidity and salt air pit the surface of the coil, and a pit is where the crack starts. Within a few miles of either coast we replace springs that are visibly rusted at five and six years old. [How long garage door springs last](/blog/how-long-do-garage-door-springs-last/) has the numbers by usage, and [high cycle springs](/blog/high-cycle-springs-worth-it/) explains the 25,000 cycle option that makes sense for a busy door.

## Why it is not a DIY job

A torsion spring is wound to a specific number of quarter turns for the weight of your specific door. Winding and unwinding it takes proper winding bars, and doing it with a screwdriver is how people lose fingers and take blows to the head.

This is not caution for its own sake. It is the single most common serious injury in residential garage door work, and it happens to competent people who underestimated the amount of energy involved.
