## Reset and change are two different jobs

People use "reset the keypad code" for two situations, and the method depends on which one you are in.

**You do not know the current code.** Maybe you bought the house, maybe the code was set years ago and nobody wrote it down. You cannot read it back from anywhere, and you do not need to. You set a new code at the opener itself, using the Learn button, and the keypad takes the new one.

**You know the current code and want a different one.** On many keypads you can do this from the keypad alone, without a ladder.

## If you have lost the code

1. On the keypad, enter the new four digit PIN you want and press Enter. Some models want Enter held, and a few older ones use a program key instead.
2. Get on a ladder and press and release the Learn button on the opener motor unit. It is on the back or side of the unit, often under the light lens, and it is colored.
3. Within 30 seconds, enter the new PIN on the keypad and press Enter again.
4. The opener light flashes or you hear a click. Go outside and test it.

**Press, do not hold.** Holding the Learn button for six seconds or more does not program anything. It erases every remote and keypad stored in the opener, and you then have to reprogram all of them. Our guide on [programming a remote or keypad](/blog/how-to-program-a-garage-door-remote/) has the Learn button colors and what to do when one will not take.

## If you know the code

The sequence varies by brand. In general you press the program key, enter the current PIN, then enter the new one. It is usually printed inside the keypad's sliding cover. If it is not there and you do not have the manual, the Learn button method above works on any keypad, known code or not.

Pick something that is not your house number, not 1234, and not the last four digits of a phone number printed on your mailbox.

## Keypad dead, or lit up and ignored

Before you touch the keypad, press the wall button inside the garage and try a remote. If both work, the opener is fine and the fault is the keypad. If nothing works, it is the opener or the power, and our guide on [an opener that quit working](/blog/garage-door-opener-quit-working/) starts at the storm tripped GFCI. If only the wall button works and every remote and the keypad have stopped, the receiver is the suspect; see [a remote that is not working](/blog/garage-door-remote-not-working/).

When it really is the keypad, work through three things.

**The battery.** The button backlight draws far less than the transmitter, so a keypad can light and beep on a battery too weak to send. Replace it even if it looks alive. While the cover is off, look for moisture, green crust on the battery contacts or ants inside. Any of those means water is getting in and the keypad will keep eating batteries.

**The lock button.** Most modern wall consoles have a lock or vacation mode that disables every remote and keypad while the wall button carries on working. It gets pressed by accident when somebody is feeling for the light button in the dark. A lit padlock or a slow blinking light on the console is the sign. Our guide on [garage door locks and security](/blog/garage-door-locks-and-security/) covers when that mode is worth using on purpose.

**Lost pairing.** A keypad that lights, accepts the code and does not move the door has been forgotten by the opener. Re-pair it with the Learn button steps above.

## What a Florida summer does to a keypad

The keypad is the only part of the opener system that lives outside all year. On a west facing wall it takes direct afternoon sun every day from spring to fall, and an afternoon storm on top of that for half the year.

The printing fades first. Then the plastic goes brittle and the face cracks, and once it cracks the rain gets to the contacts. Within a few miles of the coast, from Miami Beach to the Palm Beaches, salt air speeds that up. The pattern people notice is one or two digits dying while the rest still work, usually worse just after rain. On a keypad that has had five to eight years of that, it is time for a new one.
