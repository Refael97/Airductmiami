## What a panel costs

| Situation | Price |
|---|---|
| Standard steel section, current profile | $250 to $450 |
| Insulated steel section | $350 to $600 |
| Wood or composite section | $500 to $800 |
| Carriage, decorative or glazed section | $500 to $800 and up |
| Section on an impact-rated door | Quoted on the door, usually the top of the range |
| Custom or discontinued profile | Quoted individually, or a new door |

Those are installed prices, and the range on [our price list](/prices/) is
$250 to $800 per section. The section itself is often less than half of it;
the rest is the visit, removing the door section by section, and re-hanging
and re-balancing it afterwards. A double door takes longer than a single
because the sections are wider and heavier.

## The availability question comes first

Before anyone can give you a real number, the profile has to be checked.
Manufacturers discontinue panel designs and colors regularly, and a door
that is ten years old may be built from sections nobody makes any more.

Larger manufacturers keep profiles in production longer, which is one
practical advantage of the big brands. A ten year old section from a major
maker is more often available than a section from a boutique manufacturer.

If the profile is gone, there are three honest options and no fourth: a
visible mismatch, a full replacement, or living with the damage. Our
[repair or replace guide](/blog/repair-or-replace-garage-door/) helps with
that decision.

## The Florida rule: the section must match the rating

This is where Florida differs from the rest of the country. The Florida
Building Code requires a garage door to be rated for the wind pressure at
the site, and in Miami-Dade and Broward, the High Velocity Hurricane Zone,
the door carries a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance. Elsewhere it carries a
Florida Product Approval number. Either way, the door was tested as an
assembly: these sections, these struts, this track, these hinges.

A replacement section has to be the manufacturer's section for that door.
It carries the same struts and the same reinforcement, and it goes in with
the same fasteners. A generic section that happens to fit the opening does
not keep the rating, and the next
[wind mitigation inspection](/blog/garage-door-wind-mitigation-insurance-credit/)
may notice. Our article on
[Miami-Dade NOA versus Florida Product Approval](/blog/miami-dade-noa-vs-florida-product-approval/)
explains what the label on your door means.

The practical consequence is that section replacement on a rated door is a
little more expensive than on an unrated door, because the sections are
heavier and there is no cheap generic alternative. It is also why we ask for
the label before quoting.

## Which section is damaged matters

**Bottom section.** The most common one to be damaged, because it is the one
at bumper height and the one that sits in water. In the tight townhouse
driveways of Doral, the western edge of Kendall and the newer Winter Garden
subdivisions, a reversing car meets the bottom section more often than
anywhere else. It is also the most visible. A mismatch here shows.

**Top section.** Least visible, and the one where a repair rather than a
replacement is most often acceptable. On a door with windows the glazed
section is usually the top one, which raises the price.

**Middle sections.** Visible, and on a storm-damaged door the middle sections
are where inward buckling shows first, because that is where the wind load
concentrates between the struts.

## Storm damage is usually more than one section

After a named storm, panels are bent inward, tracks are twisted, and doors
are pulled off track by pressure. A door that has buckled under wind rarely
has one bad section: the struts are bent, the hinges are strained, and the
track may be spread. That is a
[storm damage assessment](/services/storm-damage-garage-door-repair/) at
$150 to $1,500 for bracing, securing and repair, with a full replacement
quoted separately in writing if the door is beyond it.

Insurance claims are yours to make; we provide the photographs and the
written quote. We do not inflate storm repairs, because that is the
complaint people have after every hurricane, and our
[storm damage guide](/blog/garage-door-storm-damage-what-to-do/) explains
what a fair post-storm invoice looks like.

## When to replace the whole door instead

Do the arithmetic honestly. If a single section is $600 and a new wind-rated
steel door is $950 to $4,200 installed with the permit included, and the
rest of the door is fifteen years old with original springs and rollers, the
section is not the better buy.

Replace the door when:

- The profile is discontinued and the mismatch would be visible from the street
- More than one section is damaged
- The rest of the door is at end of life anyway
- The door predates the current wind code and has no rating label at all
- The door is uninsulated and the garage is attached to living space
- The section price is more than about a third of a new door

That fourth point matters more here than anywhere. A door installed before
the code tightened may have no rating at all, and putting a new section into
it does not change that. A [hurricane-rated door](/services/hurricane-rated-garage-door-installation/)
at $1,800 to $6,500 may qualify for a wind mitigation credit on your
insurance; ask your insurer for the credit schedule. Our
[new door cost guide](/blog/new-garage-door-cost-florida/) prices the
options.

## The bottom section and water

In Florida the bottom section is where doors die. For half the year an
afternoon storm puts water against the bottom seal daily, and on the coast
the water carries salt. Steel corrodes from the inside of the section
outwards, and by the time rust shows through the paint the section is
usually beyond repair.

The prevention is cheap: keep the bottom weather seal intact, make sure the
driveway drains away from the door rather than towards it, and rinse the
bottom of the door with fresh water after any surge or flooding. Our article
on [rust and Florida humidity](/blog/garage-door-rust-florida-humidity/)
covers the rest.
