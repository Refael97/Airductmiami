## What moves each price

**Springs, $150 to $350.** Wire size, length and cycle rating. A single door
usually runs one spring; a double usually runs two, and both together adds
$120 to $250 rather than doubling. High-cycle springs cost more and are the
right choice on a door that cycles six times a day. Full detail in the
[spring replacement cost guide](/blog/garage-door-spring-replacement-cost/).

**Cables, $95 to $300.** Whether the drum is scored and whether the bottom
brackets have rusted with the cable. On the coast they usually have. The
[cable repair cost guide](/blog/garage-door-cable-repair-cost/) explains the
adjustment versus replacement distinction that decides most of the bill.

**Opener repair, $95 to $300.** Which part failed. Sensors and travel limits
are at the bottom; a logic board cooked by summer heat is at the top. If the
unit predates 1993 or the board has failed twice, replacement at $275 to
$600 is the better spend, priced in the
[opener installation cost guide](/blog/garage-door-opener-installation-cost/).

**Off-track, $140 to $600.** What got bent. A clean derailment is an hour of
work; a door that was run with the opener after coming off can need track,
rollers, cable and a section. The
[off-track repair cost guide](/blog/off-track-garage-door-repair-cost/)
breaks the range down.

**Sections, $250 to $800.** Material, whether the profile is still made, and
in Florida whether the door is rated, because the section must match the
rated assembly. See the
[panel replacement cost guide](/blog/garage-door-panel-replacement-cost/).

**New doors, $950 to $6,500.** Size, material, insulation, and above all the
rating: wind-rated, impact-rated or HVHZ. The
[new door cost guide](/blog/new-garage-door-cost-florida/) has the material
table.

## The four things that move every price

**Door size.** A double door is a bigger job than a single: more weight,
more sections, usually two springs instead of one. Most Florida houses built
after 1980 have a 16 foot by 7 foot double, and three car garages with a
double and a single are common in builds after about 2000.

**Door type and model.** A wood or custom door weighs more and needs a
different spring specification. Discontinued models mean parts availability
has to be checked before anything can be promised.

**Condition.** A failure rarely happens alone. On a twenty year old door in
Coral Springs or Kendall, the spring that snapped is surrounded by cables,
drums, bearings and rollers of exactly the same age, and in the same
humidity. A good technician quotes those separately and shows you the parts.

**Access and timing.** A gated community that needs a gate code, a detached
barn in Davie, or a call at ten at night all cost more to serve, and that
should be stated up front rather than discovered on the invoice.

## What is different about Florida

**Hurricane code.** The Florida Building Code requires a garage door to be
rated for the design wind pressure at the site, because the garage door is
the largest opening in the house and when it fails the house pressurises and
the roof is at risk. Miami-Dade and Broward are the High Velocity Hurricane
Zone, where a new door needs a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance. Elsewhere it
needs a Florida Product Approval. This is why a new door here starts at $950
rather than lower, why a hurricane-rated door runs $1,800 to $6,500, and why
replacement sections on a rated door cost more. Our guide to
[HVHZ garage doors](/blog/hvhz-garage-doors-explained/) explains the zone.

**Permits.** Replacing a garage door in Florida requires a building permit,
pulled by a licensed contractor, with the rating documented on the permit.
Our new door quotes include it. Replacing only an opener normally does not,
and neither do springs, cables or rollers. Our article on
[garage door permits in Florida](/blog/garage-door-permit-florida/) covers
what to expect.

**Salt and humidity.** Within a few miles of either coast, springs, cables,
hinges, bottom brackets and the torsion tube corrode. Rust on a cable is the
most common visible warning we see. Corroded hardware pushes a job up the
range because more parts need replacing at once, and it is why a
[tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) twice a year on the coast is
cheap insurance. Our article on
[salt air corrosion](/blog/salt-air-garage-door-corrosion/) covers both
coasts.

**Heat.** Summer garages reach 100°F and more, which cooks opener logic
boards and the plastic gears in older units. Opener calls peak in July and
August, and our article on
[summer heat problems](/blog/summer-heat-garage-door-problems-florida/)
explains which parts go first.

**Storms.** After a named storm, panels are bent inward, tracks are twisted,
and doors are pulled off track by pressure. We assess, brace or secure the
opening, and quote the repair or replacement in writing at $150 to $1,500
for the repair work. Insurance claims are yours; we provide the photographs
and the documentation. Our
[storm damage guide](/blog/garage-door-storm-damage-what-to-do/) explains
the process.

**Cycle count.** Springs do not fail from cold here. They fail from cycles,
and a Florida door cycles hard because the garage is the storage room, the
workshop and the second front door. In the golf cart communities around The
Villages and in 55 plus developments, doors cycle several times a day and
springs reach 10,000 cycles years early.

## Repairs that should be cheap and are not

Two jobs get overcharged more than any others in this market.

**Cable tension adjustment.** If a cable has come off the drum but is not
damaged, resetting and tensioning it is an $85 to $140 job. It is sometimes
billed as a full cable replacement at three times that. Ask to see the cable.

**Safety sensor alignment.** A door that reverses immediately after closing
is very often two misaligned photo eyes, which is a ten minute fix at the
bottom of the $95 to $300 opener repair range. It should not become an
opener replacement. Our guide to a
[door that closes then reopens](/blog/garage-door-closes-then-reopens/)
explains how to check it yourself.

## What a bait quote looks like

The pattern is consistent and easy to spot once you know it. An advertised
repair for $49, or a service charge of $29, gets a technician to your
driveway. Once the door is apart, the quote becomes several hundred dollars
of parts that all apparently need replacing at once. After a hurricane the
same pattern arrives with out-of-state plates.

The defence is simple: ask for the price of the finished repair, in writing,
before anyone is dispatched. A company that will only quote on arrival has
told you what kind of visit it plans to have. Our article on
[the $29 service call](/blog/29-dollar-service-call-explained/) explains the
arithmetic, and
[garage door repair scams](/blog/garage-door-repair-scams/) lists the rest
of the warning signs.

## What you should get for the money

Whatever you pay, the job should include a written estimate before work
starts, the failed part shown to you rather than described, a balance test
at the end with the opener disconnected, and a warranty on both parts and
labor. Ours is one year on each.
