## What it actually does

**Tells you the door is open.** The single most used feature by a wide margin. A notification when the door has been open for a set time ends the doubt on the drive to the airport, and for a house that sits empty from May to November it is the feature that matters.

**Lets you open and close it from anywhere.** Deliveries, a cleaner, a pool service, a family member who forgot the code, and the door you left open when you are already on I-95.

**Logs who opened it and when.** In a vacation rental or a house with a rotating set of contractors, the log is the only record of who came through the garage.

**Shows you the garage.** Camera models let you look at the inside of the garage from the app. After a storm, seeing that the door is closed and the car is where you left it is worth a great deal to someone in another state.

**Works with the rest of the house.** The main voice assistants and home systems close on a voice command; opening usually needs a PIN, deliberately.

## The three Florida situations where it earns its money

**You are a seasonal resident.** In Naples, Bonita Springs, Venice, Boynton Beach and the 55 plus communities across the state, a large share of houses sit empty for six months. A closed door you can confirm from Ohio, a notification if it opens, and a camera view after a storm are exactly what a smart opener is for. The failure we see every November is the door that stopped working in July and nobody knew.

**You run a vacation rental.** Kissimmee, Davenport and the Orlando corridor have thousands of rental homes where the garage is the guest entrance, the game room or both. Guest codes that expire on checkout, a cleaner let in on a Tuesday from your phone, and a log of every open are the difference between managing a property and hoping about it.

**The garage is your front door.** For most Florida families it is. If the door is how you come and go, knowing its state is not a luxury.

## Checking the door from another state

What you can see from the app depends on the model. Every smart opener reports open or closed and lets you close it. A camera model shows you the garage. None of them tells you the door is balanced, the springs are sound or the bottom seal is still keeping the afternoon rain out, which is why we suggest a [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) at $85 to $160 in April or May before you leave, and again when you return.

One thing to arrange before you go: someone local with a code. If the app shows the door open at 2 am and you are 1,200 miles away, you need a person who can go and look, not just a notification.

## Vacation rentals: codes, guests and cleaners

Set up guest access through the opener's own app, with a code or a share that expires at checkout. Do not hand out the account login. Give the cleaner and the pool service their own codes so the log tells you who came in. Turn on the open too long notification, because a guest who leaves the door open at midnight in Kissimmee is the most common call we get from owners.

Check the WiFi at the opener before you buy. Concrete block and stucco construction is hard on WiFi, and a garage on the far side of a CBS house from the router often has no usable signal. An access point in the garage solves it and should be part of the plan, not an afterthought.

## Battery backup comes first

![A smart garage door opener wall console with a screen, mounted inside the garage by the door to the house.](/images/smart-wall-console.webp)

If you can only justify one upgrade on a new opener, battery backup beats WiFi. It means the door still opens when the power is out, which after a storm can be days. It adds $60 to $150 on any opener and most smart units offer it.

The app itself needs power too. When the power is out, the router is down, the opener's WiFi is down and the camera is dark, unless the router is on its own backup. What you keep is the door, opening on the battery from the remote and the wall button. Our guide to [battery backup and power outages](/blog/garage-door-battery-backup-power-outage/) covers how long a battery lasts and how to use the [manual release](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/) if it does not.

## When it is not worth it

If the garage is detached, rarely used, and you can see it from the kitchen, an app adds very little.

If your WiFi does not reach the garage reliably and you do not want to add an access point, the feature will frustrate you. Test the signal at the opener location before buying.

If the existing opener is old enough to have no safety sensors, the right upgrade is a new opener for safety reasons, and the WiFi question comes second. Our guide to [old opener safety](/blog/old-garage-door-opener-safety/) covers that.

## Two practical warnings

**LED bulbs.** Many LED bulbs emit interference that kills remote range, and adding a smart controller to an opener with a bad LED in it produces a confusing set of symptoms. Use a bulb rated for garage door openers. If the remote already struggles, read our guide to a [remote that is not working](/blog/garage-door-remote-not-working/) first.

**Heat.** A closed Florida garage reaches 100°F and more in summer, which cooks the logic board on any opener. A smart opener is not more vulnerable than a standard one, but it has more electronics to lose. Keep the unit out of direct sun through a window and expect the summer heat to shorten the life of any opener here compared with the same unit in a cooler climate. Our article on [summer heat and garage door problems](/blog/summer-heat-garage-door-problems-florida/) covers what to watch for.

## What to ask for

Ask whether the quote is for a WiFi unit with or without battery backup, whether it includes a camera, whether the existing wall control and keypad will be replaced, and whether the WiFi at the opener has been tested. Ask for the price in writing. We give one before dispatch, and every price on this site is a starting price for a standard single door.
