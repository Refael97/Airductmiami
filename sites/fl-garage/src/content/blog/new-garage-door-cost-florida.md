## Standard, wind-rated, impact-rated

In most of the country a new garage door comes in three grades: a cheap
unrated door, a wind-rated door and an impact-rated door. In Florida the
first grade does not exist. The Florida Building Code requires every garage
door to be rated for the design wind pressure at the site, because the
garage door is the largest opening in the house and when it fails the house
pressurises and the roof is at risk. So the baseline door here is a
wind-rated door, and the price starts at $950 rather than lower.

| Rating | What it means | Where it is required | Installed, single door |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wind-rated | Built to resist the design wind pressure at the site, Florida Product Approval | Everywhere outside the HVHZ | $950 to $4,200 |
| Impact-rated | Wind-rated and tested against large missile impact, the "hurricane door" | Miami-Dade and Broward, and where the site's opening protection rules call for it | $1,800 to $6,500 |
| HVHZ with Miami-Dade NOA | Impact-rated door with a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance | Miami-Dade and Broward | $1,800 to $6,500 |

## What the permit adds

Replacing a garage door in Florida requires a building permit, pulled by a
licensed contractor, with the door's rating documented on the permit. The
inspector checks that the door installed matches the Florida Product
Approval or NOA on the paperwork and that it is fixed into the jamb the way
the approval says.

We include the permit in every new door quote. A quote that leaves it out
either means the contractor is not licensed to pull it, in which case walk
away, or that it will appear on the invoice later. Our article on
[garage door permits in Florida](/blog/garage-door-permit-florida/) explains
the process county by county.

## The line items people do not expect

**A new opener.** An existing opener usually works with a new door provided
the door is a similar weight. A wind-rated insulated double is heavier than
the uninsulated door it replaced, and an old opener straining against it is
a common complaint six months later. A new opener is $275 to $600 installed,
and it is the moment to add battery backup at $60 to $150. See the
[opener installation cost guide](/blog/garage-door-opener-installation-cost/).

**New springs.** The spring is sized to the door weight. A new door that
weighs more than the old one needs a new spring, and it is normally supplied
with the door rather than quoted separately.

**New track.** Reusing twenty year old track on a new door is false economy,
and on a rated door the track is part of the tested assembly, so it comes
with the door.

**Disposal.** Included. A garage door is an awkward thing to get rid of
yourself.

**Structural work.** Rare, but a jamb rotted by twenty years of afternoon
rain, or a concrete block opening that is out of square, has to be dealt
with before a door goes in. Our article on
[jamb replacement](/blog/garage-door-jamb-replacement/) covers it.

## Insulation, and whether you need it

The measure is R-value, and higher is better. An uninsulated door is
effectively R-0 to R-2. A polyurethane-cored insulated door is R-12 to R-18.

In Florida the question is heat rather than cold. An attached garage with an
uninsulated door reaches 100°F and more in summer, and that heat goes
through the wall into the kitchen and cooks the opener at the ceiling. An
insulated door keeps the garage closer to the house temperature, matters
most when there is a bedroom above the garage or a workshop or gym in it,
and is stiffer and quieter as well. It also dents less easily. Our
comparison of
[insulated versus uninsulated doors](/blog/insulated-vs-uninsulated-garage-door/)
does the arithmetic.

## Insurance and the wind mitigation inspection

Florida's wind mitigation inspection, form OIR-B1-1802, records whether
openings, including the garage door, have rated protection. A rated door
can contribute to a premium credit under Florida's mitigation discount
rules. We say "may qualify" because the credit schedule is your insurer's,
and we give you the product approval paperwork the inspector needs. Our
article on the
[wind mitigation insurance credit](/blog/garage-door-wind-mitigation-insurance-credit/)
explains what the inspector looks for. The My Safe Florida Home program has,
when open, offered grants toward opening protection including garage doors;
check whether it is taking applications before you order.

## When to buy

March to May. The door arrives and the permit clears before hurricane season
starts on 1 June, and the installers are not yet in the post-storm rush.
After a named storm the lead times stretch and the number of companies
willing to charge for the urgency goes up. Our article on the
[best time to replace a garage door in Florida](/blog/best-time-to-replace-garage-door-florida/)
covers the calendar.

## When a repair is the better spend

A new door is a large purchase and it is not always the right one. If the
sections are sound and the door carries a rating label, and only the
springs, cables or rollers have failed, a repair at $150 to $350 per spring
or $95 to $300 for cables buys you years. We say that even though the
replacement is worth several times more to us.

Replace when the sections are rusted through, when a damaged panel cannot be
matched because the profile is discontinued, when the door has no wind
rating at all, when the garage is attached and the door has no insulation,
or when the repair cost is approaching half the price of a new door. Our
[repair or replace guide](/blog/repair-or-replace-garage-door/) walks
through it.
