## What the cord does

The opener is connected to the door through a trolley that rides along the rail. The manual release, the red cord with the handle, disconnects the door arm from that trolley.

Once released, the door is free of the opener and can be moved by hand. That is exactly what you want during a power outage or when the opener has failed. In Florida, where the power goes out after almost every named storm and after plenty of ordinary summer thunderstorms, this is the one garage door skill everyone in the house should have.

![A dark garage at night with a car inside and the door down, the scene most people are looking at when they first go looking for the red cord.](/images/garage-door-stuck-at-night.webp)

## The one safety rule

**Only pull it with the door fully closed.**

With working springs, a released door is balanced and stays wherever you put it. With a broken spring, the springs are no longer holding anything, and the only thing keeping a raised door up is the opener. Disconnect it and 130 to 350 pounds come down.

If the door is up and you suspect a spring has gone, do not pull the release. Bring the door down with the opener if it will, or leave it alone and call us. Our guide on [what to do with a broken spring](/blog/what-to-do-broken-spring/) covers the safe options.

## Before the season, not during it

The worst time to learn where the cord is and how it feels is at night with a flashlight in your teeth. Do this once in April or May, when a [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) is due anyway:

1. Close the door with the opener.
2. Pull the cord and lift the door by hand. Note how heavy it feels.
3. Let go at waist height. A balanced door stays put.
4. Close it, re-engage, and run a full cycle.

If it is heavy, or sinks when you let go, the springs are worn and you have found out on a calm afternoon instead of the morning after a storm. Our guide on [preparing your garage door for a hurricane](/blog/prepare-garage-door-for-hurricane/) has the rest of the pre-season list.

## During an outage, step by step

| Step | What to do | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make sure the door is fully closed | A door stopped partway up needs the opener or a technician, not the cord |
| 2 | Pull the cord down and back until it clicks | On some models the lever swings toward the motor |
| 3 | Lift the door by hand, both hands, from the center | It should feel like twenty or thirty pounds |
| 4 | Lift it all the way, and keep hold until it is fully up | A door left halfway can roll down |
| 5 | Back the car out, then lower the door by hand | Do not leave the opening unattended with the door up |
| 6 | Engage the slide lock if the door has one | Only while the opener is disconnected |

The door has no lock of its own once it is released. The trolley was the lock. If the outage runs long and you are leaving the house, either re-engage the trolley by hand or use the slide lock, and make sure everyone in the house knows which you did. Our guide on [garage door locks](/blog/garage-door-locks-and-security/) explains why a slide lock and a connected opener must never be engaged at the same time.

## Re-engaging when the power returns

Close the door fully by hand. Then press the wall button. The carriage travels along the rail and clicks back into the trolley as it passes.

Some models require pushing the release lever back toward the door first. If yours does not re-engage after a full cycle, check the lever position.

Once re-engaged, run the door through a full open and close and watch that it stops where it should. Power outages and surges sometimes scramble travel limits on older openers, and a door that now hits the floor hard or stops short is a limit problem, not a door problem. That is [opener repair](/services/garage-door-opener-repair/) territory, $95 to $300.

## Where the cord should hang

No lower than six feet from the floor.

Children pull hanging cords, and a door with a broken spring that is released while up will fall. In older South Florida and St. Petersburg houses with low headroom, the rail sits close to the door top and the cord often ends up within a child's reach. Shorten it. Keep a step stool nearby if you need one.

If your garage has an exterior emergency release, a small keyed cylinder near the top of the door with a cable to the trolley, think about whether you need it. It is a way in as well as a way out. Most Florida garages have a side door and a door into the house, which is usually the better route.

## What to notice while you are down there

Every time you use the release, take ten seconds to check the balance: lift the door to waist height and let go. If it stays, the springs are fine. If it sinks or drops, they are not.

While the door is up by hand, look at the cables on each side. Rust on a lift cable is the most common visible warning we see in Florida, especially within a few miles of either coast, and it means the cable is going to let go.
