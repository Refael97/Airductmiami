## What a bracing kit is

A garage door hurricane bracing kit is a set of vertical steel posts that stand behind the door, from the header to the slab, with brackets that tie each door section to the post. Anchors at the top and bottom are installed once and stay. The posts themselves drop into those anchors when a storm is coming and lift out afterwards, so the door works normally the rest of the year. A single door usually takes one or two posts; a 16 foot double takes two or three.

The kit exists because a lot of Florida doors were installed before the code required a wind rating, or were installed without a permit, and their owners want to do something short of replacing them. That is a reasonable want, and for some doors in some places a kit is a reasonable answer.

## What it does and what it does not do

**It adds pressure resistance.** An unbraced door fails in a storm by bowing inward in the middle until the rollers leave the track or the sections buckle. A post behind the sections stiffens the door against that pressure, both the push and the suction. That is a real improvement on an unrated door.

**It does not add impact resistance.** Nothing about a post behind the door stops a roof tile or a fence board going through the sections. An impact rated door is tested by firing a nine pound two by four at it. A braced unrated door has not been. Our guide to [wind rated versus impact rated doors](/blog/wind-rated-vs-impact-rated-garage-door/) explains the difference.

**It does not make the door rated.** The kit carries its own approval. The door behind it does not acquire one.

**It does not make anything hurricane proof.** Nothing does. A rated door, a braced door and a new house are all designed to a pressure, and a storm can exceed it. The honest phrase is rated, with a number, not proof.

## Where it is accepted

| Where | Bracing kit |
|---|---|
| Miami-Dade and Broward, the HVHZ | Not the accepted route. The accepted route is a rated door |
| Rest of Florida | Kits with a Florida Product Approval are accepted in most of the state |
| Any address | Check with the building department whether a permit is needed for the kit at your address; the door replacement always needs one |

The HVHZ rule is the one people push back on, and it is clear. In [Miami-Dade County](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) and [Broward County](/service-areas/broward-county/), a garage door needs a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance, and the accepted route for an unrated door is a rated door with an NOA. We will not sell a bracing kit as the fix for an unrated door in Hialeah or Hollywood, because it is not one.

Elsewhere, a kit with a Florida Product Approval, an FL number, is accepted in most of the state as a retrofit for a sound unrated door. Look the number up in the state's product approval database, exactly as you would for a door; our guide to [NOA versus Florida Product Approval](/blog/miami-dade-noa-vs-florida-product-approval/) shows how. The approval names the door widths the kit covers and the anchoring it needs, and the slab and the header have to be sound enough to hold the anchors.

The comparison to make is not kit against nothing. It is kit now against a rated door now, or kit now with a rated door next spring. A kit on a door that will be replaced in two years is money you spend twice.

## When a kit is the right call

- The door is unrated, the sections are sound, and the house is outside the HVHZ.
- The kit has a Florida Product Approval that covers your door width and your address accepts it.
- The budget does not stretch to a rated door this year, and you want the door stronger before June rather than after.
- The house is a rental or a second home and a rated door is planned but not yet ordered.
- The door is on a detached garage or workshop where the cost of a rated door is hard to justify.

In those cases we install the anchors, show you how the posts go in, and leave you with a door that resists pressure far better than it did.

## When replacement is the honest answer

- The house is in Miami-Dade or Broward. The accepted route is a rated door.
- The sections are rusted through, cracked at the stiles or bent from a previous storm. A post cannot hold a section that is failing on its own, and the anchors will not hold in a rotted jamb. Our guide to [rust and Florida humidity](/blog/garage-door-rust-florida-humidity/) shows what through rust looks like.
- The door already needs springs, cables and rollers. Our [repair or replace](/blog/repair-or-replace-garage-door/) guide has the rule: when the repair approaches half the price of a new door, replace, and if you are adding a kit on top the arithmetic tips faster.
- You want the insurance credit. Florida's wind mitigation inspection records whether openings have rated protection, and a rated door may qualify for a premium credit; a bracing kit is not a rated door. Ask your insurer and your inspector what, if anything, a kit changes on the form. Our guide to the [wind mitigation insurance credit](/blog/garage-door-wind-mitigation-insurance-credit/) covers it.
- The door has glass that is not impact rated. Bracing the sections does nothing for the windows.

A [hurricane rated door](/services/hurricane-rated-garage-door-installation/) is $1,800 to $6,500 installed, permit included, and our [cost guide](/blog/hurricane-rated-garage-door-cost/) explains what moves the price. Where the code asks for a pressure rating alone, a wind rated steel door through [new door installation](/services/new-garage-door-installation/) is $950 to $4,200, and the [buyer's guide](/buyers-guide/) compares the materials. Order in March to May, when lead times are shortest.

## Installing it before a storm

The anchors go in on a normal day, weeks or months before anything is named. The posts go in when a watch is issued, not when the warning comes, because by then the hardware store is empty and we are working through a list.

Practice once. Put the posts in on a dry afternoon in May, with the door closed and the opener disconnected on the manual release, and time it. Confirm the posts clear the opener rail and the bottom seal and store them where you can reach them in the dark. Our guide to [preparing your garage door for a hurricane](/blog/prepare-garage-door-for-hurricane/) has the full checklist, including the [manual release](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/) and where to leave the car.

After the storm, take the posts out before running the opener. A door that took pressure should be checked before it cycles: read [what to do about storm damage](/blog/garage-door-storm-damage-what-to-do/) and call if the sections are bowed or the track is bent.

We will not tell you a kit makes the door hurricane proof, because nothing does. We will not sell one in the HVHZ as a substitute for a rated door. We will not brace a door whose sections are already failing. And we will not inflate a bracing job into a replacement when the kit is the right answer for your door and your address.
