## What the cable does

Two steel cables run from the bottom brackets of the door up to drums on the
torsion bar. When the spring unwinds, the drums wind the cables and lift the
door. They carry the entire weight of the door, split between them, and on a
16 foot double door that is a lot of weight on two thin wound cables.

That is why a single failed cable is not a small problem: the load transfers
to one side, the door lifts crooked, and it jams or comes out of the track.
Our article on a [crooked or uneven door](/blog/garage-door-crooked-uneven/)
covers what that looks like from the driveway.

## The adjustment versus replacement distinction

This is where cable jobs get overcharged. If the cable came off the drum
because the door hit something, or because it was left slack after a power
outage and a hand lift, and the cable itself is undamaged, it can be
re-seated and tensioned. That is an $85 to $140 job.

If the cable is frayed, kinked or rusted, it has to be replaced. That is the
$95 to $300 job.

The difference between the two is visible in thirty seconds. Ask to see the
cable. A company that will not show you the part it says needs replacing is a
company to be careful with, and our guide to
[what a fair quote includes](/blog/what-a-fair-quote-includes/) lists the
other things you should be shown.

## Why cables fail in Florida

**Rust at the bottom bracket.** The bracket sits at floor level. For half the
year an afternoon storm puts water against the bottom seal every day, and
the garage floor stays damp. Within a few miles of either coast the air
carries salt as well. In Cape Coral, Fort Lauderdale east of US 1, Clearwater,
Jupiter, Melbourne and Palm Coast, this is the single most common cause we
see, and it is the most common visible warning of any failure on a Florida
door. Our article on
[rust and Florida humidity](/blog/garage-door-rust-florida-humidity/) covers
the rest of the hardware that goes the same way.

**A scored drum.** A drum with a rough groove saws through the cable from the
inside. Replacing the cable without replacing the drum buys you a year at
most, which is why the cable and drum job exists as a line on the price list.

**Running out of balance.** A door with a tired spring puts more load on the
cables than they were sized for. Fixing the cable without fixing the spring
is treating the symptom. Spring replacement is $150 to $350 per spring and
we quote it separately so you can see both numbers.

**Cycle count.** Cables are a wear part with a life measured in the same
cycles as the springs around them. Florida doors cycle hard because the
garage is the storage room, the workshop and the second front door. In golf
cart communities and the 55 plus developments around The Villages, Boynton
Beach and Pembroke Pines, a door can cycle six or eight times a day, and
cables age accordingly.

**Storm pressure.** After a named storm we see cables pulled off drums by a
door that flexed inward under wind load. That is usually a
[storm damage assessment](/services/storm-damage-garage-door-repair/) rather
than a plain cable job, because the track and panels need checking too.

## The warning signs

Look at the cable where it wraps the drum at the top of the track. Fraying
there is the earliest sign, and it is what the photo at the top of this page
shows. Near the bottom bracket, look for orange staining on the concrete,
rust on the bracket bolts, and kinks in the cable.

A door that has started closing unevenly, with one side touching down before
the other, is often telling you a cable has stretched or slipped. A door that
makes a slapping sound on the way down often has a cable that is going slack
on one side.

Treat a frayed cable as a same-week job rather than a same-year one. When it
goes, the door drops on one side, and the repair that follows is off-track
correction at $140 to $600 rather than a cable at $95 to $300. Our
[off-track repair cost guide](/blog/off-track-garage-door-repair-cost/)
explains why that range is so wide.

## What the visit includes

A cable replacement is not just the cables. We release the spring tension,
fit both cables, check the drums for scoring, check the bottom brackets for
rust and replace them if they are corroded, re-tension the spring, and
finish with a balance test with the opener disconnected. If the bottom
brackets are rusted through they are replaced at the same time, because a
new cable on a rotten bracket is a wasted visit.

On coastal doors we also look at the spring itself and the torsion tube,
because salt does not stop at the cable. If we recommend anything beyond
the cables, we show you the part and give you the published price, and you
can decline it.

## Preventing the next one

Rinse the bottom of the door and the bottom brackets with fresh water every
month or two if you live near the coast. Keep the bottom seal intact so the
floor under the door stays as dry as it can. Have the door tuned up once a
year, or twice near the coast, at $85 to $160; the cable check is part of
the [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/), and catching fraying at the
drum a year early is the cheapest cable repair there is.
