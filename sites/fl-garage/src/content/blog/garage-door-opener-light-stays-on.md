## What normal looks like

The opener light comes on every time the door moves and switches itself off a few minutes later. On many LiftMaster and Chamberlain units that is about four and a half minutes, and some models let you change the delay from the wall console. It also comes on from the light button on the console and, on units that have one, from a motion sensor.

So before hunting for a fault, decide which of four problems you have: the light never goes off, it flickers while on, it flashes in a pattern and the door misbehaves, or it does not come on at all while the door runs fine. Each points somewhere different.

## The light will not turn off

**The console light button.** The most common cause and the easiest. A light switched on with that button does not time out. It stays on until somebody presses it again, and children press it, as does anyone leaning on the console while unloading groceries.

**The motion sensor.** Some wall consoles have a sensor that switches the light on when someone walks in. If it can see something moving all the time, such as a ceiling fan, a dehumidifier, a curtain or the dog, the light never goes off. Turn the feature off at the console and watch.

**A stuck console button.** Humidity and age harden the console membrane, and a light button that stays pressed keeps the light on. If the console is also doing odd things to the door, our guide to [a wall button that stopped working](/blog/garage-door-wall-button-not-working/) walks through that test.

**An app schedule.** On a smart opener, someone may have set the light from a phone. Check the app before calling anyone.

**A relay stuck closed.** A relay on the logic board switches the light. If its contacts weld together from wear, heat or a lightning surge, the light stays on permanently. To test, unplug the opener, disconnect the console wires at the motor unit and plug it back in. If the light is still on ten minutes later with nothing asking for it, the fault is on the board, which is an [opener repair](/services/garage-door-opener-repair/) at $95 to $300.

## The light flickers

**A loose bulb.** Every cycle shakes the unit, and months of vibration slowly unscrew the bulb. Unplug the opener, let the bulb cool and tighten it.

**An LED not made for openers.** Many general purpose LEDs flicker on an opener circuit, and some also throw off radio noise that cuts remote range or causes phantom openings, as we explain in [garage door opens by itself](/blog/garage-door-opens-by-itself/). Swap it for one sold as safe for garage door openers.

**A burned or corroded socket.** A bulb above the rated wattage cooks the socket until it darkens, and garage humidity corrodes the contact at its base. If you see dark or melted plastic, stop changing bulbs. The socket needs replacing.

**A failing relay.** Good bulb, clean socket and still flickering usually means the relay is making poor contact. That is an opener repair too.

## The light flashes in a pattern

That is the opener talking, not the bulb. On many units the light flashes about ten times when you try to close and the safety sensors cannot see their beam. Some models also flash and beep before closing on a timer or from the app, as a warning to anyone nearby. Our guide to [opener blinking light codes](/blog/garage-door-opener-blinking-light-codes/) explains each pattern.

## The light does not come on

Try a bulb you know works. If a good bulb stays dark and the door runs normally, the socket or the relay has failed. Some recent units have an integrated LED with no bulb to change, and when it dies the light module or the matching part of the opener is replaced.

## The right bulb for a Florida garage

A closed garage in July passes 100°F in the afternoon, and the opener hangs at the ceiling, the hottest spot in the room. An incandescent bulb inside the lens adds heat right beside the logic board, and its filament takes a hit on every cycle. An LED made for openers runs cooler, shrugs off vibration and stays out of the radio band. Always stay within the maximum wattage on the opener label.

Unplug the opener before touching the socket, use a steady ladder, and keep your fingers out of a socket that has turned dark.
