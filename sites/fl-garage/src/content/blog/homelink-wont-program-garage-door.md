## Two pairings, not one

HomeLink is the set of buttons many cars carry on the rear view mirror, the overhead console or the visor. It works in two steps, and most failures come from not knowing which one failed.

**Step one: the car copies your handheld remote.** HomeLink listens to the remote signal and stores it.

**Step two: the opener learns the car.** Rolling code openers, which means nearly everything built since the 1990s, will not accept a simple copy. The opener has to add the car to its list of trusted transmitters, exactly as it would a new remote.

The HomeLink indicator light tells you which step you are on, and reading it is the biggest time saver here.

## The full sequence

1. **Park outside**, facing the door, with the path clear, because the door is going to move. Leave the car on or in accessory mode as the manual says.
2. **Clear the HomeLink memory.** Hold the two outer buttons until the light flashes rapidly, usually 10 to 20 seconds. Do this once when the car is new to you, not every time.
3. **Fit a fresh battery in the handheld remote.** A weak remote still opens the door up close but does not transmit strongly enough for the car to copy it cleanly.
4. **Copy the remote.** Hold it one to three inches from the HomeLink button you want. Press and hold both. The light flashes slowly, then rapidly. When it flashes rapidly, let go of both.
5. **Test the car button.** Press and hold it and watch the light.
6. **If the light flashes rapidly for about two seconds and then goes solid**, the opener is rolling code and step two is missing. Climb the ladder, press and release Learn on the motor unit, and within 30 seconds press the car button for two seconds and release. Repeat up to three times.

The Learn button is on the motor unit at the ceiling, usually under the light lens. Our guide on [how to program a garage door remote](/blog/how-to-program-a-garage-door-remote/) shows where to find it and what its color means.

## What the HomeLink light is telling you

| What the light does | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Never goes from slow to rapid flash | The car did not copy the remote | Fresh battery, move closer, clear and start again |
| Goes solid when pressed and the door opens | Fixed code, you are done | Nothing |
| Flashes rapidly, then goes solid | Rolling code, opener not paired yet | Do the Learn step |
| All of the above fine, door still ignores it | The opener did not accept it | Check the handheld remote and the opener |

## The mistakes we see most

**The 30 seconds ran out.** The opener leaves learn mode half a minute after you press Learn, and getting down the ladder and into the car can take longer. Use two people: one on the ladder, one in the car.

**Holding Learn down.** Six seconds or more does not program anything. It erases every remote and keypad in the opener, so you then need to reprogram everything in the house.

**Training from a universal remote.** The car copies the opener's original remote more reliably than a universal bought later.

**Remotes that need pulses.** Some, notably for certain Genie openers, need to be pressed and released every two seconds during the copy instead of held.

**Lock mode left on.** If the wall console lock is active, the opener ignores the car, the remotes and the keypad. Look for a lit indicator on the console.

## When the car and the opener do not speak

The known exception to universal compatibility is some older vehicles with LiftMaster and Chamberlain openers that have a yellow Learn button, the Security+ 2.0 line. If the car does everything right and the opener never accepts it, check the vehicle manual for the HomeLink version, and ask about the compatibility kit the opener maker sells for this case.

## When the car is not the problem

If the handheld remote has stopped opening the door too, HomeLink has nothing good to copy. Check the remote battery and try the wall button. If the wall button works and no remote does, the opener receiver has failed, often after a nearby lightning strike. ## One security habit

Clear the HomeLink buttons before you sell or trade in a car, or the next owner drives away with a remote to your house. If it is already gone, erase the opener memory by holding Learn for about six seconds and reprogram what you still have.
