## What the price includes

A normal installation price covers the unit, the rail, the mounting
hardware, wiring to an existing outlet, the wall control, two remotes,
safety sensor installation and alignment, travel and force setting, and
removal of the old opener.

What it does not cover, and what should be quoted separately if it applies:
a new electrical circuit, structural reinforcement of the header, converting
extension springs to torsion for a jackshaft, or repairing a door that is
not balanced. If the door needs a spring before the opener goes on, that is
$150 to $350 per spring and we tell you before the visit, not after.

## Which drive type to choose

The decision is mostly about what is above and beside your garage.

If somebody sleeps over or beside the garage, buy a belt drive. In the
two-story houses that fill Weston, Wesley Chapel, Lake Nona and Palm Beach
Gardens, the master bedroom is often directly above the garage, and a chain
drive at six in the morning is heard in that room. The premium over chain is
roughly $75 to $150 and it is one of the few upgrades people consistently
say afterwards was worth it.

If the garage is detached, buy a chain drive. The South Tampa and St.
Petersburg bungalows with a detached garage on the alley, and the Davie and
Wellington properties with a separate barn or workshop, have nobody to
disturb, and nothing lasts longer for less money.

If there is not enough clearance above the door for a rail, you need a wall
mounted jackshaft. The 1950s to 1970s ranch houses in Hialeah, older Kendall,
Hollywood east of I-95 and older St. Petersburg often have single doors with
very little headroom, and a jackshaft mounted beside the torsion bar solves
it. Our guide to [low headroom openers](/blog/low-headroom-garage-door-openers/)
covers the options.

## Why Florida is hard on openers

Three things shorten an opener's life here, and they all argue for buying a
little better than the cheapest unit.

**Heat.** A closed garage in summer reaches 100°F and more, and the opener
lives at the ceiling where the hottest air sits. That heat cooks logic boards
and softens the plastic gears in older chain drive units. A unit with a metal
gear train and a board rated for the heat lasts longer, and belt and direct
drive units generally run cooler.

**Humidity and salt.** Within a few miles of the coast, chains, rails and
trolleys corrode. A belt does not rust. If you are in Cape Coral, Jupiter,
Melbourne or anywhere east of US 1 in Broward, that is a practical reason
for belt over chain quite apart from the noise.

**Power outages.** Florida loses power after storms more than anywhere else
in the country, and it can stay off for days. An opener with a backup
battery still opens when the street is dark. Our article on
[battery backup and power outages](/blog/garage-door-battery-backup-power-outage/)
explains how many cycles you get and how to test it before the season.

## Repair or replace the old one

Repair is worth it when the unit is under about fifteen years old and the
fault is a gear kit, a safety sensor, a capacitor or a logic board. Those are
$95 to $300 jobs on the [opener repair](/services/garage-door-opener-repair/)
line.

Replace when any of these are true:

- The opener predates 1993, meaning it has no photo eye safety sensors
- Parts are no longer made for the model
- The repair quote approaches half the price of a new unit
- The motor has already been rebuilt once
- The board has failed twice from heat, which means the next one will too

The 1993 line is worth taking seriously. Photo eye sensors became mandatory
on new openers that year. An opener without them has no reliable way to stop
for a child or a pet, and there are still plenty of them running in the
older neighborhoods of Miami, Fort Lauderdale and Orlando. Our article on
[old opener safety](/blog/old-garage-door-opener-safety/) explains how to
tell what you have.

## Features worth paying for

**Battery backup.** Covered above. $60 to $150 added to any opener, and the
single most useful option in this state.

**WiFi and smartphone control.** Genuinely useful for checking whether you
left the door open when you are already on I-4, and for letting a delivery
or a cleaner in. On the vacation rental homes around Kissimmee and the
seasonal homes in Naples and Bonita Springs it is close to essential. The
[smart opener installation](/services/smart-garage-door-opener-installation/)
line runs $350 to $750 with camera and battery options. Our article on
[whether a smart opener is worth it](/blog/smart-garage-door-opener-worth-it/)
covers the trade-offs.

**Soft start and stop.** Reduces wear on the door hardware as well as the
noise. Standard on most belt and direct drives.

**A stronger motor than you think you need.** An insulated wind-rated double
door is heavier than the uninsulated door it replaced. If you are planning a
[new door](/blog/new-garage-door-cost-florida/) in the next few years, buy
the opener for that door, not the current one.

## What happens on the visit

We check the door balance first, because an opener fitted to an unbalanced
door is a warranty argument waiting to happen. Then we remove the old unit,
mount the new rail and motor, wire it to the existing outlet, set the safety
sensors at the correct height, set the travel limits and the force, program
the remotes and the wall console, and run the door a dozen times. You get
one year on parts and one year on labor, on the invoice.
