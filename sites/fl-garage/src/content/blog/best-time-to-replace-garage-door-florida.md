## The short answer

March to May. Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, and a door that is ordered in March is installed, inspected and closed out on the permit before the season opens. Lead times on rated doors are at their shortest, the building department is not backed up, and we are not yet working around storm calls.

That is the whole recommendation. The rest of this page is the reasoning, and what happens to prices and availability if you wait.

## Why lead time is the whole story

A garage door in Florida is not a stock item you pick up on the day. It has to be rated for the design wind pressure at your site, which means a specific model in a specific size with a specific approval: a Florida Product Approval across most of the state, a Miami-Dade NOA in [Miami-Dade](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) and [Broward](/service-areas/broward-county/). Manufacturers build those to order.

In a normal spring the wait from order to installation is several weeks. It depends on the manufacturer, the door, and whether you want windows, insulation or a color that is not white. A plain wind-rated steel single is faster than an insulated impact-rated double with glass.

That lead time is the reason we say March, not May. A door ordered in March is on the house in April. A door ordered in the last week of May competes with everyone who saw the June 1 forecast, and it is on the house in July if nothing happens in between.

## The June 1 rush

Every year on June 1 the season opens, the news covers it, and the phone starts. Most of those calls are for a tune-up or for the door someone meant to replace last year. The manufacturers see the same wave, and lead times stretch through June and July as orders stack up.

None of it is a crisis. A door ordered in June still arrives well before the busiest weeks of the season, which historically are August to October. But you are waiting longer for the same door and paying the same price, and if a storm forms in the Gulf while your door is on a truck, your installation date moves.

The [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) side of the rush is easier. At $85 to $160 it is a single visit, and April or May is the right month for it. The pre-season checklist in [prepare your garage door for a hurricane](/blog/prepare-garage-door-for-hurricane/) is built around that visit.

## What a storm does to the market

After a named storm makes landfall, three things happen in the affected counties at once.

**Rated doors sell out.** Every damaged door on every street in the landfall zone needs a rated replacement with a permit, and the manufacturers' stock of common sizes goes in days. Lead times that were weeks become months. After Ian in 2022, [Southwest Florida](/service-areas/southwest-florida/) waited most of a season for some doors, and the same pattern has followed every major storm since.

**Building departments back up.** Permits still have to be pulled and inspections still have to happen, and the counties are dealing with roofs, windows and rebuilds at the same time.

**Prices move, at some companies.** This is the complaint people have after every hurricane, and it is a fair one. The same door that was one price in April is a different price in October, from a company that is suddenly very busy.

Ours does not move. The prices on [our price list](/prices/) are the prices in April and in October. What we cannot control is the door's availability, and we tell you the lead time in writing when we quote rather than promising a date we cannot hold.

## Which doors should be replaced before the season

Not every door needs replacing before June. The ones that do:

- **Unrated doors.** No factory label, no permit on file. These are the largest weak point on the house and the first thing a wind mitigation inspector marks down. See [garage door wind mitigation insurance credit](/blog/garage-door-wind-mitigation-insurance-credit/).
- **Doors from the early 1990s rebuild.** Andrew-era replacements in Kendall and Homestead are past thirty years old, and their springs, cables and bottom brackets have had thirty years of salt air.
- **Doors with two or more damaged sections.** Panel replacement runs $250 to $800 per section when the profile is still made. Two sections and bent track is usually a replacement.
- **Doors in the HVHZ without an NOA.** In Miami-Dade and Broward the accepted route to a rated opening is a rated door, not a bracing kit. See [HVHZ garage doors explained](/blog/hvhz-garage-doors-explained/).

The doors that do not need replacing before June are the rated ones with sound hardware. Those get a tune-up, and [repair or replace](/blog/repair-or-replace-garage-door/) covers the decision in more detail.

## The permit and the calendar

Replacing a garage door in Florida requires a building permit pulled by a licensed contractor, with the rating documented on the permit, and the county inspects the finished installation. In spring the whole cycle, application to closed permit, fits inside the door's lead time. After a storm it does not always, and an open permit waiting on an inspection is one more thing to manage. We pull the permit and it is included in the quote. The process is in [garage door permits in Florida](/blog/garage-door-permit-florida/).
