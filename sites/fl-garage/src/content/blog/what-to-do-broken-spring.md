## Confirm it

Look at the horizontal shaft above the door opening. A broken torsion spring shows a clean gap in the coil, typically an inch or two, with the two halves apart. On a 16 foot double door there are usually two springs, one either side of the center bracket, and one of them will have the gap.

![A snapped garage door torsion spring on its shaft, showing the gap where the coil separated. The door below it is dead weight until the spring is replaced.](/images/broken-torsion-spring.webp)

If you have extension springs instead, which is common on 1950s to 1970s single doors in older neighborhoods like Hialeah, Hollywood east of I-95 and St. Petersburg, they run alongside the horizontal tracks on each side. A broken one is usually obvious: stretched, snapped, or hanging from its safety cable.

If you heard a bang and the door will not lift, it is almost certainly this. Our guide to the [loud bang from the garage](/blog/loud-bang-garage-door/) covers what else it could have been.

## The next ten minutes

**Do not press the opener button.** Not once. The springs carry the door and the opener guides it. Without spring support, the opener will strain the motor, strip the gear, crease the top section, and can pull the door off its track. Every attempt makes the eventual repair larger, and a creased top panel adds a [panel replacement](/services/garage-door-panel-replacement/) at $250 to $800 to a spring job.

**If the door is closed, leave it closed.** This is the good outcome. Your car may be stuck inside but nothing is going to deteriorate overnight, and the house is secure.

**If the door is open, get it down carefully.** Two people, hands flat on the bottom section and clear of the joints between sections, lower it slowly. Fingers get caught in section joints and that is a common injury. Once it is down, slide the manual lock or clamp the track above a bottom roller so nobody tries the opener.

**Do not pull the manual release with the door raised.** Without spring support, disconnecting a raised door lets it come down under its own weight. The [manual release guide](/blog/garage-door-manual-release-how-to/) explains when to use it and when not to.

## Getting a trapped car out

If you need the car and the door is closed:

1. Pull the manual release with the door down.
2. Lift with a second person. Expect it to feel like 130 to 350 pounds, depending on the door. [How much a garage door weighs](/blog/how-much-does-a-garage-door-weigh/) has the figures by size and material.
3. Prop it properly. Locking clamps on the track below the bottom rollers on both sides, or a pair of stepladders under the bottom section. Not a broom, not a chair.
4. Move the car, then lower the door slowly with both people and leave it closed.

If you cannot lift it, stop. Call for [emergency service](/services/emergency-garage-door-repair/) and say the car is trapped, because it changes how the call is prioritized. Our [car trapped in the garage](/blog/car-trapped-in-garage/) article goes through the options in more detail.

## If a storm is coming

Hurricane season runs 1 June to 30 November, and a broken spring during a watch or warning changes the priorities.

A closed door is the safe state. The door is the largest opening in the house, and it does not need working springs to resist wind pressure; it needs to be down, locked, and if you have a bracing kit, braced. If your spring breaks with the door closed and a storm is three days out, you can wait for a normal appointment.

A door stuck open is the opposite. An open garage lets the wind into the house, the house pressurizes, and the roof is what fails next. If you cannot get the door down by hand with help, call and say the door is open and a storm is coming. That is a same-day call for us every time. ## The both-springs conversation

If your door runs two springs, expect to be told the second should be replaced too. On this occasion that is genuine.

Both springs were fitted on the same day and have taken the same number of cycles. The survivor is at the same point in its life as the one that just failed, and in practice it follows within months.

Doing both together adds $120 to $250 rather than doubling, because the visit and most of the labor are shared. You are allowed to decline it, and we will do just the one and put the cost of a return visit in writing.

While the springs are off the shaft is also the moment to look at the cables and the drums. Rust on a cable is the most common visible warning we see in Florida, and replacing rusted cables during a spring job costs less than a second visit when one frays through.

## Why it happened

Springs fail on cycles, not on age. A standard spring is rated for about 10,000 open and close cycles, which is seven to ten years for a light user and five to seven for a Florida family that uses the garage as the front door. In Wesley Chapel, Riverview and Port St. Lucie, where whole subdivisions went up in the 2000s, the first round of spring failures is arriving now, street by street.

Humidity does the rest. Moisture pits the surface of the coil, salt air near the coast pits it faster, and the fatigue crack starts at a pit. Springs in Cape Coral, Jupiter and Melbourne fail earlier than the cycle rating suggests, and they usually look rusty before they go. [How long springs last](/blog/how-long-do-garage-door-springs-last/) has the numbers, and if your door is a busy one, [high cycle springs](/blog/high-cycle-springs-worth-it/) explains the 25,000 cycle option that costs a little more once and lasts two to three times as long.

There is usually nothing you did wrong. What you can do next time is book a [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) at $85 to $160 each April or May, before the heat and before the season, so we see the rust and the balance before the spring sees the end of its life.
