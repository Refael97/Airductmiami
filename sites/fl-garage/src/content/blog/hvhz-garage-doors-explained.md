## What the HVHZ is

The Florida Building Code requires a garage door to be rated for the design wind pressure at the site. Across most of the state, that rating is shown by a Florida Product Approval, an FL number. Two counties are different.

Miami-Dade and Broward are the High Velocity Hurricane Zone, the HVHZ. The zone dates from the code rewrite after Hurricane Andrew in 1992, when the county found that most of the garage doors that failed had been the first thing to go, and that once the door went the roof followed. The HVHZ has its own product approval system, the Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance, and a garage door installed there needs one.

If you live in [Miami-Dade County](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) or [Broward County](/service-areas/broward-county/), everything below applies to you. If you live in Palm Beach, Tampa Bay, Central Florida, [Southwest Florida](/service-areas/southwest-florida/) or North Florida, you are outside the zone, and the article that applies to you is [Miami-Dade NOA vs Florida Product Approval](/blog/miami-dade-noa-vs-florida-product-approval/).

## What an NOA is

A Notice of Acceptance is Miami-Dade County's approval of a specific product, from a specific manufacturer, tested to the county's protocols. For a garage door, the NOA covers the door model, the sizes it is approved for, the design pressures it resists, and, for impact-rated doors, that it passed the large missile impact test. It also specifies how the door has to be installed: the track, the brackets, the fasteners and their spacing.

That last part matters more than people expect. An NOA door installed with the wrong track brackets or fewer lag screws than the NOA calls for is not an NOA installation, and the final inspection is where that gets caught. We install to the NOA sheet, not to habit.

The NOA number is on the factory label inside the door and on the permit. Photograph the label on installation day, because a faded label ten years from now is the difference between a door a wind mitigation inspector can verify and one they cannot.

## Why a door approved elsewhere is not approved here

This is the question we get most often from people who moved to Kendall from Orlando, or who priced a door online.

A Florida Product Approval and a Miami-Dade NOA are two different approval systems with two different test protocols. A door with an FL number has been shown to meet the code for the pressures listed on its approval. That is what the rest of Florida uses. The HVHZ does not accept an FL number as proof for the HVHZ, because the zone's requirements are its own.

Many doors from the major manufacturers carry both. The same steel door model may have an FL number for use in Tampa and an NOA for use in Hollywood, and it may be the same door. Or it may not: sometimes the HVHZ version has heavier struts, thicker steel, a different track, or more fasteners, and the price is different because the door is different.

So when a door is quoted for a Broward address, the question is not "is this door hurricane rated" but "what is the NOA number." A quote that cannot answer that is a quote for the wrong door, and the building department will not permit it.

## Wind-rated, impact-rated, and what the NOA covers

Every HVHZ door is wind-rated, meaning it resists the design pressure. Not every HVHZ door is impact-rated, meaning it was also tested against a large missile, the two by four fired at the door in the lab. Impact-rated is what most homeowners mean by "hurricane door," and it is what most insurers mean by opening protection.

When you compare two NOA doors, ask what each NOA covers. The difference between wind-rated and impact-rated is explained in [wind-rated vs impact-rated garage doors](/blog/wind-rated-vs-impact-rated-garage-door/), and it changes both the price and what the door may do for your [wind mitigation inspection](/blog/garage-door-wind-mitigation-insurance-credit/).

## Bracing kits in the HVHZ

Outside the zone, retrofit bracing kits with a Florida Product Approval are a real option for an older door. Inside the zone, the accepted route is a rated door. If your door in Pembroke Pines or Palmetto Bay is an older unrated one, the honest answer is that a kit is not the path, and a company selling one for an HVHZ address is either unaware of the rule or hoping you are. The full picture is in [garage door hurricane bracing kits](/blog/garage-door-hurricane-bracing-kits/).

## The permit

Replacing a garage door anywhere in Florida requires a building permit pulled by a licensed contractor, with the rating on the permit. In the HVHZ the permit records the NOA, the county inspects the installation, and the inspector checks the label and the attachment against the NOA. We pull the permit, and the fee is in the quote. What that process looks like is in [garage door permits in Florida](/blog/garage-door-permit-florida/).

## Doors from the 1990s rebuild

A lot of the HVHZ's doors went in at once. Andrew rebuilt Homestead and much of Kendall in 1993 under the new code, and those doors are now more than thirty years old. The door itself may still be rated, but the springs, cables, hinges and bottom brackets are the age where salt air has done its work, and a rated door with a rusted bottom bracket is not the door that was tested.

If your door is from that era, a [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) at $85 to $160 before June tells you whether the hardware is holding, and [garage door rust in Florida humidity](/blog/garage-door-rust-florida-humidity/) covers what to look for. When the hardware is gone and the door is thirty years old, the replacement conversation is usually about a new NOA door rather than another set of springs.

## After a storm

When a door in the HVHZ fails in a storm, the replacement has to be an NOA door with a permit, and rated doors are in short supply for months after a major hurricane.
