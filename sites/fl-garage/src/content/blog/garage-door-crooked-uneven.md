## What is happening mechanically

Two cables lift the door, one on each side, running from the bottom brackets up to drums on the torsion shaft. The spring turns the shaft, the drums wind the cables, and as long as both drums wind the same amount the door rises level.

If one cable slips, stretches, or comes off its drum, that side gets less lift. The door goes up crooked, and on the way down one corner touches first while the other is still an inch or two off the slab. Our guide on [what a garage door technician does](/blog/garage-door-technician-what-they-do/) explains where each of these pieces sits if the names are new.

## Why Florida cables fail early

The bottom of a lift cable lives at the worst place on the door. It anchors to the bottom bracket, a few inches off a slab that gets water on it from an afternoon storm most days between June and November, in air that is humid all year. Within a few miles of either coast the salt in that air speeds everything up.

![A frayed garage door lift cable with broken strands standing out from the wind near the bottom bracket, and a rust darkened section where water sat against it. This is the most common warning we see on Florida doors.](/images/frayed-lift-cable.webp)

Rust on a cable is the most common visible warning we see anywhere in the state. The cable does not need to snap to cause a crooked door. As it corrodes it loses diameter, stretches unevenly, and eventually a strand breaks and catches on the drum so the cable winds wrong. A door in [Cape Coral](/service-areas/cape-coral/), [Jupiter](/service-areas/jupiter/) or [St. Petersburg](/service-areas/st-petersburg/) can show this inside five years. Inland in Lakeland or Gainesville it takes longer, but the humidity gets there in the end. Our guide on [rust and Florida humidity](/blog/garage-door-rust-florida-humidity/) covers what to look for on the rest of the door.

## The four causes

**A cable off the drum.** The most common and the most sudden. Look at both drums at the top of the door and you can usually see it: one cable seated neatly in the grooves, the other loose, crossed or bunched. It usually happens when the door closed onto something and the cable went slack for a moment, or when the door was forced.

**A stretched or failing cable.** Gradual. The cable has been under load for years and has lost a little length, or it is fraying and losing effective diameter. In Florida this is nearly always rust.

**A bent track or loose bracket.** One side is no longer holding the door where it should. Sight along both vertical tracks from the floor. A car mirror bumping the track, or a track bracket whose lag bolts have rusted loose in the block wall, will do it. After a storm, wind pressure on the door can push a track out of line too.

**A cracked end hinge or worn roller.** The end hinges carry the rollers. A cracked hinge or a roller with a collapsed bearing lets that corner of the door sit out of line and the door tips on that side. Our guide on [worn rollers](/blog/garage-door-roller-replacement-guide/) shows what a failed one looks like.

## Why it will not stay a small problem

A door lifting unevenly puts side load on the rollers, which they are not designed for. They wear, they bind, and eventually one climbs out of the track. That is the moment a cable job becomes an [off-track job](/blog/garage-door-off-track/), and if the opener keeps running after that, a panel job as well.

## What to do meanwhile

Stop using the opener if the door is visibly binding, if you can hear it scraping, or if the crookedness is more than an inch. If it is slight and the door still runs smoothly, it can wait a few days but not a few months, and in the humid months a frayed cable does not get better while it waits.

Do not attempt to correct the drums. They sit on the torsion shaft under full spring tension, and adjusting them is the same category of work as replacing a spring. Our article on [why torsion work is not a DIY job](/blog/why-never-diy-torsion-spring/) explains what that tension does when it is released wrong.

If the door is stuck open and crooked, keep people and cars clear of it and treat it as an [emergency](/services/emergency-garage-door-repair/), because a door hanging on one cable can drop. Same-day and after-hours dispatch adds $150 to $300, and we tell you that before we leave.

## What the repair includes

A crooked door visit should end with both cables replaced as a pair even if only one failed, because the other one has had the same years and the same weather. The drums get checked for worn grooves. The bottom brackets get inspected for rust, since that is where the cable anchors and a corroded bracket is the next thing to let go. Both tracks get checked for plumb, the rollers get inspected, and the door gets a balance test with the opener disconnected. If the visit ends without that test, the cause has not been confirmed.

If you are on the coast, ask for galvanized or stainless cables. They cost a little more as a part and they are worth it from [Fort Lauderdale](/service-areas/fort-lauderdale/) to [Naples](/service-areas/naples/). Our guide on [salt air corrosion](/blog/salt-air-garage-door-corrosion/) explains which other parts to upgrade at the same time, and a yearly [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) in March or April, before the wet season, is when a rusting cable gets caught before it costs anything.
