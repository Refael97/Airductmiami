## The short answer

Replacing a garage door in Florida requires a building permit. The permit is pulled by a licensed contractor, and the door's wind rating is documented on it. Replacing only the opener normally does not require one. Repairs to an existing door, springs, cables, rollers, a section, do not normally require one either.

That is the whole rule. The rest of this page is why it exists and what it means for you.

## Why a garage door is a permit job

The Florida Building Code requires a garage door to be rated for the design wind pressure at the site. The garage door is the largest opening in the house, and when it fails in a storm the house pressurizes and the roof is at risk. The code treats the door as structural for that reason, and a permit is how the county records that the structural part of your house meets the rating.

Outside the High Velocity Hurricane Zone, the door needs a Florida Product Approval, an FL number. In Miami-Dade and Broward, the HVHZ, it needs a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance, an NOA. Either way the permit records which door went in and what approval it carries, and the county inspects the installation before the permit is closed.

An opener does not change any of that. It is an appliance on the door. So replacing an opener is normally not a permit job, and neither is replacing a spring, a cable, or a section on a door that stays otherwise the same.

## Who pulls the permit

The licensed contractor doing the installation. That is us, when we install your door. The permit is filed with your county or city building department, in your name as the owner and ours as the contractor, with the door model and its approval number attached.

Some counties allow a homeowner to pull a permit as an owner-builder for work on their own home. We do not recommend it for a garage door, because the rating still has to be documented correctly and the inspection still has to pass, and if something is wrong it is your problem rather than ours. When we pull it, the record is ours to get right.

What you should be wary of is a company that offers to skip it. "No permit needed, saves you money" is a sentence to walk away from. It saves the company a fee and a day, and it leaves you with a door that has no record.

## What a permit costs

Permit fees are set by the county or city building department and vary from one to the next. They also change. We do not print a figure here because whatever we print will be wrong somewhere in Florida by next year.

What we do instead is include the fee and the paperwork in the written quote. Our published prices for [new door installation](/services/new-garage-door-installation/), $950 to $4,200 for a standard wind-rated steel single, and [hurricane-rated door installation](/services/hurricane-rated-garage-door-installation/), $1,800 to $6,500 for an impact-rated or HVHZ door, both have the permit included. There is no permit line item added afterward.

If you want to see your county's fee schedule for yourself, the building department publishes it, and most now show permit status online so you can confirm your permit was filed and closed.

## What the process looks like

| Step | Who | When |
|---|---|---|
| Written quote naming the door model and approval number | Us | Before anything is ordered |
| Permit application filed with the building department | Us | After you approve the quote |
| Door ordered, lead time confirmed | Us | Same time as the permit |
| Installation | Us | When the door arrives |
| Final inspection scheduled | Us | After installation |
| Inspector checks the door, the label and the attachment | County | Usually within days |
| Permit closed, record on file | County | After the inspection passes |
| Paperwork handed to you | Us | Permit number, invoice, label photo |

The inspection is real. The inspector looks at the label, checks that the door matches the approval on the permit, and checks the attachment to the jamb and the tracks. If a door was installed with the wrong fasteners or the wrong track brackets for its approval, that is where it gets caught, and it is why we install to the approval sheet rather than to habit.

## Why an unpermitted door is a problem at resale

When you sell a house in Florida, the buyer's inspector and often the buyer's lender look at the permit history. A garage door with no permit on file shows up as unpermitted work. It is one of the more common items that appear on a buyer's list, because doors are replaced often and cheaply by companies that skip the step.

The result is one of three things. The buyer asks for a price reduction. The buyer asks you to get a permit after the fact, which means an inspection of a door that was not installed to be inspected. Or the deal slows down while the county sorts it out. None of those is worth the fee that was saved.

## Why it is a problem for insurance

Florida's wind mitigation inspection, form OIR-B1-1802, records whether the garage door has rated protection. The inspector verifies the rating from the label on the door and from the permit record. A door with a readable label can sometimes be recorded on the label alone. A door with a faded label and no permit is recorded as unrated, whatever it actually is.

An unrated garage door on that form means no credit for the door and, in practice, no path to the better opening protection category for the whole house. A rated door may qualify for a credit under your insurer's schedule. The permit is a large part of what makes that possible. We cover it in [garage door wind mitigation insurance credit](/blog/garage-door-wind-mitigation-insurance-credit/).

The reverse is also true. If a storm damages an unpermitted door and the claim gets looked at closely, the lack of a record can complicate things. We are not adjusters and we will not tell you how your policy would respond, but a clean permit record removes a question before it is asked.

## The HVHZ

In [Miami-Dade County](/service-areas/miami-dade-county/) and [Broward County](/service-areas/broward-county/), the permit records the door's NOA, and a door with only a Florida Product Approval will not be permitted. The building departments in both counties are used to garage doors and they check. If you are replacing a door in Hialeah, Kendall, Coral Springs or Pembroke Pines, this is the process, and the HVHZ rules are in [HVHZ garage doors explained](/blog/hvhz-garage-doors-explained/).

Outside the HVHZ, from Palm Beach north and across to [Southwest Florida](/service-areas/southwest-florida/), the permit records the FL number, and the design pressure is specific to the site. A door approved for one pressure is not approved for a higher one, and the permit is where that match is checked.

## What you should walk away with

When we finish an installation, you have the permit number, the county's inspection result, the invoice naming the door and its approval, and a photograph of the label. Keep them together. That folder is what the wind mitigation inspector wants, what a buyer's inspector wants, and what a storm claim may want. It costs nothing to keep and it is hard to recreate later.
