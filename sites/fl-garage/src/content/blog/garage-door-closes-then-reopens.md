## What the opener is thinking

Modern openers have two independent ways of deciding something is in the way.

The **photo eyes** watch an infrared beam across the opening about six inches off the floor. Break it and the door stops and reverses before it touches anything.

The **force monitor** watches how hard the motor is working. If the door meets more resistance than the setting allows, the opener assumes it has hit something and reverses.

A door that comes all the way down, touches the floor and then lifts is almost always the force monitor, and the reason is usually the travel limit rather than a real obstruction. A door that reverses part way down, before it reaches the floor, is more likely the photo eyes. Notice which one you have, because it decides where to look.

## Cause one: the down travel limit

The limit tells the opener where the floor is. If it is set slightly too far, the opener drives the door into the slab, meets resistance, and reads that as an obstruction.

The tell is that it happens every time, at exactly the same point, with the door fully down.

Florida has a few ways of moving that point on you. A summer garage runs past 100°F, and the door and track grow a little in the heat, so a limit set on a cool morning sits a fraction lower by late afternoon. A new bottom seal that is thicker than the old one does the same. And the seal matters more here than anywhere: for half the year an afternoon storm puts water at the bottom of the door daily, and a vinyl seal that has swollen or a rubber one that has gone stiff changes how far the door travels before it stops. Sand and grit washed under the seal by that rain will do it too.

The fix is adjusting the down limit on the opener, a dial on older units or a button sequence on newer ones. It is a small correction and it is part of every [tune-up](/services/garage-door-tune-up/) we do. If the seal is the problem, replacing it is a cheap part of the same visit.

## Cause two: the photo eyes

Check both indicator lights near the floor. Steady on both means aligned. A flickering or dark receiving light means the beam is not getting across.

The Florida causes are specific. A west facing garage gets low, direct sun into the receiving eye in the late afternoon, and unlike further north that happens all year. A small hood above the sensor solves it. Ants and small lizards get onto the lens and into the housing. Rain splashes in from the driveway and leaves a film on the lens. And the sensor wire runs along the wall at floor level, which is where storm water sits, so the terminals corrode and the connection comes and goes with the humidity.

Look for a bracket that has been knocked, a wire that has been pinched or chewed, or green crust on the small screw terminals at the back of the sensor. Our guide on [safety sensors](/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors-explained/) shows how to realign them, and our guide on [a flashing opener light](/blog/garage-door-opener-blinking-light-codes/) explains what the opener is telling you while this happens.

If the fault is intermittent, or time of day dependent, this is where to look first.

## Cause three: the door got harder to move

Less common as a setting problem and more common as a door problem.

Before turning the force up, check whether the door is actually harder to move than it should be. Pull the manual release with the door closed and run it by hand through its full travel. It should move smoothly with one hand and stay wherever you leave it.

If it binds anywhere, the resistance is real, and turning up the force just means the opener pushes through it. In Florida what we find is rusted hinges and dry rollers from a year of humidity, a track that has drifted, or a spring that has lost tension and left the door heavy. That last one is the important one, because an opener working against a fatiguing spring strips its own gears. ## The one thing not to do

Do not solve this by turning the force up until the door stays down.

That setting is the only thing stopping the door from crushing whatever is under it, and on most openers it is a small dial that is easy to turn too far. A door that reverses is telling you something rather than misbehaving. Our article on [child and pet safety](/blog/garage-door-child-pet-safety/) explains what the force setting is protecting.
