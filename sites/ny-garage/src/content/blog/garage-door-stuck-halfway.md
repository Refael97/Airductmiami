## Same place every time, or different places

This one distinction narrows it down quickly.

**Same point, every time.** Something physical is in the way at that height. Look at both tracks at that exact position for a dent, a bulge, a loose bracket, or a roller that is not sitting properly. Also look at the hinges at that section joint.

**Different points, or gradually worse.** More likely the spring balance, a stretching cable, or an opener that is losing force. A door that used to run cleanly and now stops higher each month is telling you the springs are tired.

## The manual test

Pull the manual release with the door closed if you can. Then lift the door by hand, slowly, through its whole travel.

You are feeling for two things: a point where it becomes hard to move, and whether it holds position when you let go.

A rough spot at one height confirms the mechanical cause. A door that will not hold halfway confirms the spring balance is off.

## The usual causes, in order

**Seized roller.** A roller with a dead bearing does not turn, it slides. It drags, it wears the track, and eventually it jams at the tightest point in the run. Full roller replacement is $100 to $250.

**Bent track.** A bump from a car, a ladder, or years of a dragging roller. Track can sometimes be straightened; badly bent sections get replaced.

**Loose track bracket.** The track spreads under load and the roller comes half out. Common on doors that have never been serviced.

**Broken or stretched cable.** One side loses support, the door goes crooked, and it binds. This one is urgent because the next step is the door coming off the track entirely.

**Cracked hinge.** End hinges carry the roller. A cracked one lets the roller shift out of line.

**Travel limits.** If the opener stops the door at the same point and the door moves freely by hand, the limit setting is the answer and it is adjustable on the opener.

## Getting the car out

If the door is stuck high enough to drive under, do not. A door held by a jam is not a door you want your car under.

The safe order is: pull the manual release, get the door either fully up and securely propped or fully down, then move the vehicle.

If you cannot move the door at all by hand, stop and call somebody. Forcing a jammed door is how a repair becomes a replacement.
