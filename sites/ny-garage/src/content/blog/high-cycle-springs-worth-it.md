## What makes a spring high-cycle

Thicker wire, more coils, greater length. The same energy is stored across more material, so each coil is working further inside its fatigue limit and lasts longer.

It is not a coating or a treatment, it is a different physical size, which is why headroom matters.

## The arithmetic

Cycles are what matter, not years. One cycle is one opening plus one closing.

| Rating | At 4 cycles/day | At 8 cycles/day | Extra cost per spring |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 standard | About 7 years | About 3.5 years | Baseline |
| 20,000 | About 14 years | About 7 years | $80 to $150 |
| 50,000 | About 34 years | About 17 years | $200 to $350 |

Now put a service visit next to it. Spring replacement is $170 to $400 per spring installed. Most of that is the visit, not the spring.

On a busy door, paying $120 more once to avoid a $300 visit in year four is straightforward arithmetic.

## Who should buy them

**Doors used many times a day.** A household with several drivers, or a garage that is the main entrance to the house. In this region that describes most of the under-house garages in Bay Ridge, Marine Park and New Dorp.

**Doors that are hard to service.** If the garage is awkward to reach, tucked under the house, or the service visit is disruptive, fewer visits is worth paying for.

**Heavy doors.** Wood, custom and heavily insulated doors work their springs harder.

**Doors you plan to keep.** If the door has fifteen years left in it, a spring that lasts fourteen makes sense. If the door is close to replacement, it does not.

## Who should not

If your door runs twice a day, a standard spring lasts around thirteen years, which is close to the life of the door itself. The premium buys you very little.

If headroom is genuinely tight, the physical size may rule it out regardless.

## What to ask for

When you get a spring quote, ask for three things: the wire size, the inside diameter and length, and the cycle rating.

A company that gives you all three is a company that measured your door. A company that cannot say what rating it is fitting is fitting whatever was on the van, and you have no way to compare that quote to any other.
