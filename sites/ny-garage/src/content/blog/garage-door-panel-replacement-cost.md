## What a panel costs

| Situation | Price |
|---|---|
| Standard steel section, current profile | $250 to $450 |
| Insulated steel section | $350 to $600 |
| Wood or composite section | $500 to $800 |
| Carriage or decorative section | $500 to $1,000+ |
| Custom or discontinued | Quoted individually |

Those are installed prices. The section itself is often less than half of it; the rest is the visit, removing the door section by section, and re-hanging and re-balancing it afterwards.

## The availability question comes first

Before anyone can give you a real number, the profile has to be checked. Manufacturers discontinue panel designs and colours regularly, and a door that is ten years old may be built from sections nobody makes any more.

Larger manufacturers keep profiles in production longer, which is one practical advantage of the big brands. A ten year old Clopay section is more often available than a section from a boutique manufacturer.

If the profile is gone, there are three honest options and no fourth: a visible mismatch, a full replacement, or living with the damage.

## Which section is damaged matters

**Bottom section.** The most common one to be damaged, because it is the one at bumper height and the one that sits in water. It is also the most visible. A mismatch here shows.

**Top section.** Least visible, and the one where a repair rather than a replacement is most often acceptable.

**Middle sections.** Visible, and on a door with windows the glazed section is usually a middle one, which raises the price.

## When to replace the whole door instead

Do the arithmetic honestly. If a single section is $600 and a new door is $1,600, and the rest of the door is fifteen years old with original springs and rollers, the section is not the better buy.

Replace the door when:

- The profile is discontinued and the mismatch would be visible from the street
- More than one section is damaged
- The rest of the door is at end of life anyway
- The door is uninsulated and the garage is attached to living space
- The section price is more than about a third of a new door

## The bottom section and water

On coastal properties, the bottom section is where doors die. Water sits against it, the seal fails, and steel corrodes from the inside of the section outwards. By the time it shows, the section is usually beyond repair.

The prevention is cheap: keep the bottom weather seal intact, make sure water drains away from the door rather than towards it, and rinse the bottom of the door after storm surges.
