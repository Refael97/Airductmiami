## What the opener is thinking

Modern openers have two independent ways of deciding something is in the way.

The **photo eyes** watch an infrared beam across the opening. Break it and the door reverses.

The **force monitor** watches how hard the motor is working. If the door meets more resistance than the setting allows, the opener assumes it hit something and reverses.

A door that reverses at the very bottom of its travel is almost always the second one, and the reason is usually the travel limit rather than a real obstruction.

## Cause one: the down travel limit

The limit tells the opener where the floor is. If it is set slightly too far, the opener drives the door into the slab, meets resistance, and reads that as an obstruction.

The tell is that it happens every time, at exactly the same point, with the door fully closed.

The fix is adjusting the down limit on the opener, usually a dial or a button sequence depending on the brand. It is part of any tune-up.

## Cause two: the photo eyes

Check both indicator lights. Steady on both means aligned.

Look for a bracket that has been knocked, a cobweb across a lens, a wire that has been caught or chewed, or low direct sunlight into the receiving lens.

If the fault is intermittent, or time of day dependent, this is where to look.

## Cause three: down force set too low

Less common, and usually the result of somebody adjusting it after a different problem.

Before turning it up, check the door is not actually harder to move than it should be. Pull the manual release and run the door by hand through its full travel. If it binds anywhere, the resistance is real and turning up the force just means the opener forces through it.

## The one thing not to do

Do not solve this by turning the force up until the door stays down.

That setting is the only thing stopping the door from crushing whatever is under it. An opener with the force wound up is a genuine hazard, and a door that reverses is telling you something rather than misbehaving.
