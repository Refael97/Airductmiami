## The one question

Is there living space above or beside the garage?

**Yes:** belt drive. **No:** chain drive.

That decides it for the large majority of homes, and everything below is detail.

## The comparison

| | Chain | Belt |
|---|---|---|
| Installed price | $281 to $450 | $350 to $630 |
| Noise | Loud | Very quiet |
| Maintenance | Occasional chain lubrication | None on the belt |
| Durability | Highest | High |
| Typical life | 10 to 15 years | 10 to 15 years |
| Best for | Detached garages | Attached, living space above |

## Why the noise difference is so big

A chain drive is metal chain running against a metal rail. The noise is not just airborne, it is vibration transmitted through the mounting into the building structure. In a room above the garage you feel it as much as hear it.

A belt is steel-reinforced rubber. It does not clatter and it transmits far less vibration into the framing. The mechanism is otherwise identical.

That structural transmission is why isolating the mounting helps a chain drive but never fully fixes it, and why people who try to solve chain noise with rubber isolators usually end up buying a belt drive anyway.

## What the extra money buys

The premium is roughly seventy to a hundred and fifty dollars.

For a garage under a bedroom, that is one of the highest-value upgrades available anywhere in a house. For a detached garage, it buys you almost nothing.

## Where this matters most in New York

This region has an unusual concentration of garages directly beneath living space. Under-house garages are standard in Bay Ridge, Marine Park, Mill Basin and Sheepshead Bay, common in New Dorp and much of Staten Island's attached housing, and frequent in the semi-attached rows in Queens.

In all of those, a chain drive is a decision people regret. In a detached Bayside or Huntington garage, it is fine.

## The other drive types, briefly

**Screw drive**, $300 to $500. Few moving parts, strong lift, but temperature sensitive, which is a genuine issue in an uninsulated New York garage.

**Direct drive**, $400 to $630. The motor rides the rail, so there is a single moving part. As quiet as a belt with usually a longer warranty. Fewer models available.

**Jackshaft, wall mount**, $450 to $800. Mounts beside the door and needs almost no headroom. Not really a competitor to the others: it is the answer when there is no room for a rail at all.

## Features to consider alongside

**Battery backup.** The garage still opens when the power is out. In an area that loses power in storms, this is worth having.

**WiFi control.** Check whether you left it open, and let a delivery in. Adds roughly $50 to $150, or retrofit a controller to an existing opener for less.

**Soft start and stop.** Reduces both noise and wear on the door hardware. Standard on most belt and direct drives.
