## The whole price list

These are real New York market ranges for residential doors. They assume a standard single door in normal condition, which is the honest baseline.

| Repair | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spring replacement, one | $170 to $400 | Average around $282 |
| Spring replacement, both | Adds $150 to $300 | Labor is shared |
| Cable repair | $100 to $350 | Average around $225 |
| Cable and drum together | $250 to $425 | When the drum scored |
| Cable tension adjustment | $95 to $150 | Slipped, not damaged |
| Opener repair | $100 to $350 | Gear kits, boards, sensors |
| Opener installation | $281 to $630 | Average around $453 |

| Emergency call-out premium | $200 to $350 | On top of the repair |

## The four things that move a price

**Door size.** A double door is a bigger job than a single: more weight, more sections, usually two springs instead of one.

**Door type and model.** A wood or custom door weighs more and needs a different spring specification. Discontinued models mean parts availability has to be checked before anything can be promised.

**Condition.** A failure rarely happens alone. On a twenty year old door, the spring that snapped is surrounded by cables, drums, bearings and rollers of exactly the same age.

**Access and timing.** A garage on a shared alley, a steep driveway, or a call at ten at night all cost more to serve, and that should be stated up front rather than discovered on the invoice.

## Repairs that should be cheap and are not

Two jobs get overcharged more than any others in this market.

**Cable tension adjustment.** If a cable has come off the drum but is not damaged, resetting and tensioning it is a $95 to $150 job. It is sometimes billed as a full cable replacement at three times that. Ask to see the cable.

**Safety sensor alignment.** A door that reverses immediately after closing is very often two misaligned photo eyes, which is a ten minute fix. It should not become an opener replacement.

## What a bait quote looks like

The pattern is consistent and easy to spot once you know it. An advertised repair for $49, or a service charge of $29, gets a technician to your driveway. Once the door is apart, the quote becomes several hundred dollars of parts that all apparently need replacing at once.

The defence is simple: ask for the price of the finished repair, in writing, before anyone is dispatched. A company that will only quote on arrival has told you what kind of visit it plans to have.

## What you should get for the money

Whatever you pay, the job should include a written estimate before work starts, the failed part shown to you rather than described, a balance test at the end with the opener disconnected, and a warranty on both parts and labor. Ours is one year on each.
