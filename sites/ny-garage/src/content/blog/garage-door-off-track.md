## The most useful thing to know

The re-seating is not the expensive part. What got bent is.

A door that jumped the track and was left alone is roughly an hour of work: release the tension, re-seat the rollers, check the track, re-tension, test. That is the $150 to $300 end of the range.

A door that came off and was then run four more times because somebody kept trying is a different job. The track is bent, the rollers are destroyed, the bottom section is creased, and the cable is wound around the drum. That is the $700 and up end.

The difference between those two outcomes is entirely about what happens in the first five minutes.

## What to do

1. **Stop pressing the button.** The opener does not know the door is off. It will keep pulling.
2. **Pull the manual release** if the door is closed or nearly closed. If the door is up and partly derailed, leave the release alone: disconnecting it can let the door drop.
3. **Keep people and cars away from it.** A partially derailed door can come down.
4. **Note what happened just before.** A bang, a car bumping it, a cable that snapped. It tells the technician where to look.
5. **Say whether it is open or closed when you call.** That determines whether this is an emergency.

## What causes it

**A broken cable.** The most common cause we see. One side loses support, the door lifts crooked, and the low-side rollers pop out of the track.

**Impact.** A vehicle bumping the door, even at walking speed, knocks the track out of alignment. The door often keeps running for weeks before it finally derails, which is why the connection gets missed.

**Seized rollers.** A roller that does not turn drags in the track, wears it, and eventually climbs out.

**Loose track brackets.** The track spreads under load. Doors that have never been serviced are the usual candidates.

**Something in the way.** A door closing onto an object it cannot crush will lift on one side.

## What a proper repair includes

Getting the door back on the track is the middle of the job. The visit should end with:

- The cause identified and fixed, not just the symptom
- The full length of both tracks checked for straightness and fixing
- Every roller inspected and the failed ones replaced
- Both cables checked and re-tensioned evenly
- A balance test with the opener disconnected
- Panels checked for creasing at the section joints

If nobody tells you why it came off, it is going to come off again.
