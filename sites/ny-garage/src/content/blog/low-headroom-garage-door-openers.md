## The measurement that decides it

Measure from the top of the door opening to the lowest thing in the way above it. Not the ceiling height: the lowest obstruction, which is often a joist, a pipe or a light fitting.

| Clearance available | What fits |
|---|---|
| 14 inches or more | Any standard rail opener |
| 10 to 14 inches | Standard opener, low-headroom track kit on the door |
| 8 to 10 inches | Low-profile rail opener |
| Under 8 inches | Wall mounted jackshaft |

## Why this comes up constantly in New York

This region has an unusual concentration of garages that were never designed for an opener.

**Under-house garages.** Standard in Bay Ridge, Marine Park, Mill Basin and Sheepshead Bay, and common across the attached and semi-attached housing of New Dorp and much of Staten Island. The garage sits below the living space and the header is right above the door.

**Converted garages.** Levittown in particular, where almost every garage is an addition or a conversion rather than original construction. Framing is often lower and rarely square.

**Older detached garages.** Mineola, New Rochelle, Larchmont. Built for cars smaller than what is parked in them now, and never intended to carry a motor overhead.

## The jackshaft answer

A jackshaft opener bolts to the wall beside the door and turns the torsion bar directly. No rail, no ceiling clearance, and the whole garage ceiling stays free for storage.

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| Installed price | $450 to $800 |
| Headroom needed | A few inches |
| Spring type required | Torsion only |
| Noise | Quiet, nothing above your head |
| Usually includes | An integrated deadbolt on the door |

Its requirements are real: torsion springs, and a door that is properly balanced. It drives the bar directly, so it will not tolerate a heavy door and it will tell you so immediately.

## Low-headroom track kits

A separate thing from the opener. A low-headroom track kit changes the geometry of the door's own track so the door needs less clearance to make the turn from vertical to horizontal.

It can buy you a few inches, which is sometimes enough to make a standard opener work. It costs more than standard track and it needs the spring system reviewed at the same time.

## What to ask for in a quote

Give the installer the clearance measurement before they arrive, and ask which option they are quoting and why.

A quote for a standard rail opener in a garage with nine inches of headroom is a quote that will fail on the day, and the conversation you then have in your driveway is the one worth avoiding.
