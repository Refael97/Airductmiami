## What is happening mechanically

Two cables lift the door, one on each side, wound onto drums on the torsion shaft. As long as both drums wind the same amount, the door rises level.

If one cable slips, stretches, or comes off its drum, that side gets less lift. The door goes up crooked, and on the way down one corner touches first.

## The four causes

**A cable off the drum.** The most common and the most sudden. Look at both drums at the top of the door and you can usually see it: one cable seated in the groove, the other loose or coiled wrong.

**A stretched or failing cable.** Gradual. The cable has been under load for years and has lost a little length, or is fraying and losing effective diameter.

**A bent track or loose bracket.** One side is no longer holding the door where it should. Sight along both tracks.

**A broken end hinge.** The end hinges carry the rollers. A cracked one lets the roller sit out of line and the door tips on that side.

## Why it will not stay a small problem

A door lifting unevenly puts side load on the rollers, which they are not designed for. They wear, they bind, and eventually one comes out of the track.

The economics are worth being blunt about:

| Stage | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Cable re-seated, undamaged | $95 to $150 |
| Cable replacement, pair | $100 to $350 |
| Door off track, clean | $150 to $300 |
| Door off track, with damage | $300 to $700 |

Fixing it now is the cheap end of that table.

## What to do meanwhile

Stop using the opener if the door is visibly binding or the crookedness is significant. If it is slight and the door still runs smoothly, it can wait a few days but not a few months.

Do not attempt to correct the drums. They sit on the torsion shaft under full spring tension, and adjusting them is the same category of work as replacing a spring.
