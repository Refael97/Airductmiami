## What it actually does

**Tells you the door is open.** The single most used feature by a wide margin. A notification when the door has been open for a set period ends the leaving-the-house doubt permanently.

**Lets you open and close it from anywhere.** Useful for deliveries, trades, a family member who forgot a code, and for closing the door you left open when you are already at work.

**Logs who opened it and when.** Useful in a household with several people, or when a contractor has temporary access.

**Integrates with the rest of the house.** Works with the main voice assistants and home automation systems. Closing on a voice command is generally allowed; opening usually requires a PIN, deliberately.

## What it costs

| Option | Price |
|---|---|
| Smart opener, installed | $350 to $700 |
| Premium over a comparable standard model | $50 to $150 |
| Retrofit controller on an existing opener | $80 to $200 fitted |
| Retrofit kit, parts only | $30 to $80 |

The retrofit route is the value option and most people do not realise it exists. If your opener is under about twenty years old and works fine, there is no reason to replace it just to get an app.

## When it is genuinely worth it

**You use the garage as the main door.** Then the garage door is your front door, and knowing its state matters.

**You get deliveries you cannot receive.** Letting a courier put a parcel inside and closing the door behind them is the use case people end up valuing most.

**Somebody in the house forgets.** Teenagers, elderly parents, anyone. A notification solves it without a conversation.

**You travel.** Checking the door from another city is worth something.

## When it is not

If the garage is detached, rarely used, and you can see it from the house, an app adds very little.

If your WiFi does not reach the garage reliably, the feature will frustrate you. Test the signal at the opener before buying, and be aware that a detached garage often needs an access point or an extender to make this work.

## Two practical warnings

**LED bulbs.** Many LED bulbs emit interference that kills remote range, and adding a smart controller to an opener with a bad LED in it produces a confusing set of symptoms. Use a bulb rated for garage door openers.

**Battery backup is the more important feature.** If you can only justify one upgrade on a new opener, battery backup beats WiFi. It means the garage still opens when the power is out, which in a region that loses power in storms is worth more than an app.
