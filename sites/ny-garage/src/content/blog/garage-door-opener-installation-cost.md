## Installed prices by drive type

| Drive type | Installed | Noise | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain | $281 to $450 | Loud | Detached garages |
| Belt | $350 to $630 | Very quiet | Living space above |
| Screw | $300 to $500 | Moderate | Heavy doors, insulated garages |
| Direct | $400 to $630 | Very quiet | Long-term reliability |
| Jackshaft, wall mount | $450 to $800 | Quiet | Low headroom garages |

The New York average across all types is around $453, which reflects the fact that most people in this region end up on a belt drive.

## What the price includes

A normal installation price covers the unit, the rail, the mounting hardware, wiring to an existing outlet, the wall control, remotes, safety sensor installation and alignment, travel and force setting, and removal of the old opener.

What it does not cover, and what should be quoted separately if it applies: a new electrical circuit, structural reinforcement of the header, converting extension springs to torsion for a jackshaft, or repairing a door that is not balanced.

## Which drive type to choose

The decision is mostly about what is above your garage.

If somebody sleeps over or beside the garage, buy a belt drive. The premium over chain is roughly seventy to a hundred and fifty dollars and it is one of the few upgrades people consistently say afterwards was worth it.

If the garage is detached, buy a chain drive. Nothing lasts longer for less money, and there is nobody to disturb.

If there is not enough clearance above the door for a rail, you need a wall mounted jackshaft. In this region that is not unusual: under-house garages in Bay Ridge, Marine Park, New Dorp and Sheepshead Bay routinely lack the headroom, and so do many converted Levittown garages.

## Repair or replace the old one

Repair is worth it when the unit is under about fifteen years old and the fault is a gear kit, a safety sensor, a capacitor or a logic board. Those are $100 to $350 jobs.

Replace when any of these are true:

- The opener predates 1993, meaning it has no photo eye safety sensors
- Parts are no longer made for the model
- The repair quote approaches half the price of a new unit
- The motor has already been rebuilt once

The 1993 line is worth taking seriously. Photo eye sensors became mandatory on new openers that year. An opener without them has no reliable way to stop for a child or a pet.

## Features worth paying for

**Battery backup.** New York loses power. An opener with a backup battery still opens when the street is dark, which matters if the garage is the only way in.

**WiFi and smartphone control.** Genuinely useful for checking whether you left it open, and for letting a delivery in. Add roughly $50 to $150 over a comparable non-connected model, or retrofit a controller to an existing opener for less.

**Soft start and stop.** Reduces wear on the door hardware as well as the noise. Standard on most belt and direct drives.
