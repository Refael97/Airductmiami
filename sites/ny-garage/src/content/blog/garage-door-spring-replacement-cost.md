## What you are actually paying for

A garage door spring is not an expensive part. What you are paying for is the tension in it and the fact that releasing that tension safely takes tools and practice most people do not have.

A standard residential torsion spring costs a supplier somewhere between $40 and $100 depending on wire size and length. The rest of the price is the visit, the labor, the disposal, and the fact that the person doing it is carrying a set of winding bars and knows how many quarter turns your specific door needs.

## The New York numbers

| What | Price |
|---|---|
| One torsion spring, installed | $170 to $400 |
| Both springs, done together | Adds $150 to $300 over one |
| High-cycle spring, per spring | $250 to $500 |
| Extension spring pair | $150 to $300 |
| Cables replaced at the same time | $100 to $250 |

The New York average for a single spring lands around $282. That is a real average, not a teaser: it reflects an ordinary single or double residential door with a standard spring.

## Why the quotes vary so much

Four things genuinely move the number.

**Door size and weight.** A sixteen foot double door weighs roughly twice what a single does and usually runs two springs. That is a bigger job with more parts, and it costs more.

**Spring type and rating.** A standard spring is rated for about 10,000 open and close cycles. A high-cycle spring is rated for 20,000 or more and costs more up front. On a door that opens six times a day, high-cycle is usually the cheaper choice over ten years.

**What else failed.** Springs rarely fail in isolation on an old door. Cables fray, drums score, and end bearing plates wear. A good technician quotes those separately and shows you the parts, so you can decline them.

**When you call.** Emergency and after-hours dispatch carries a premium, in this market $200 to $350. That premium should be stated before anyone is sent, and it should not be applied to the repair price itself.

## One spring or two

If your door has two springs and one broke, the other has cycled exactly the same number of times. It is at the same point in its life as the one that just failed and it typically follows within months.

That makes replacing both a genuine recommendation rather than an upsell, and there is a simple test for whether you are being told the truth: an honest company will do just the one if you ask, and will tell you what it costs to come back later.

## What a fair quote looks like

Before anyone is dispatched, you should be able to get:

- A price range for your specific door, not a national average
- Whether the price is for one spring or two
- What happens if cables or drums turn out to be damaged too
- The call-out premium, if any, stated separately
- The warranty on parts and labor

If a company will not give you a number until a technician is standing in your driveway, that is information about how the rest of the visit is likely to go.

## What not to do

Do not run the opener on a broken spring. The springs carry the weight of the door, not the motor. Forcing the opener strains it, bends panels, and can drop the door.

Do not attempt the replacement yourself. A wound torsion spring stores enough energy to break bones and it releases instantly if it is not unwound under control with proper winding bars. This is the one job on a garage door that genuinely should not be a home project.
