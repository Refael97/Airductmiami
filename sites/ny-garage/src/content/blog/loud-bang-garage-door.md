## What you heard

People describe it as a gunshot, a car hitting the house, or something heavy falling over. It is loud, singular, and it comes from the garage.

What happened is that a wound torsion spring, holding a couple of hundred pounds of stored energy, reached the end of its cycle life and let go. The energy discharges against the shaft in a fraction of a second, and the gap in the coil is usually visible from the floor: a clean break with the two halves separated by an inch or two.

## The next ten minutes

**Do not press the opener button.** The springs lift the door, not the motor. Running the opener now will strain the motor, can bend the top section, and can pull the door off the track.

**Look at the spring.** It runs along the shaft above the door opening. A broken one has an obvious gap. If you can see that gap, you have your answer.

**Leave the door where it is if it is closed.** This is the good outcome. Your car may be trapped, but nothing is going to get worse overnight.

**If the door is open**, be careful. Without spring support it is dead weight. Two people can usually bring it down, and it should be done slowly with hands clear of the section joints.

**Do not disengage the manual release with the door up.** Pulling the release on a raised door with a broken spring lets it drop.

## Both springs, and why

If your door has two springs, they were installed together and have taken the same number of cycles. The one that did not break is at exactly the same point in its life as the one that did, and in practice it usually follows within a few months.

Replacing both at once costs $150 to $300 more than one rather than double, because the visit and most of the labor are shared. It is one of the few upsells in this trade that is genuinely arithmetic rather than sales.

You can decline it. A company that will not let you decline it is telling you something.

## Why it is not a DIY job

A torsion spring is wound to a specific number of quarter turns for the weight of your specific door. Winding and unwinding it takes proper winding bars, and doing it with a screwdriver is how people lose fingers and take blows to the head.

This is not caution for its own sake. It is the single most common serious injury in residential garage door work, and it happens to competent people who underestimated the amount of energy involved.
