## Typical weights

| Door | Weight |
|---|---|
| Single steel, uninsulated, 8x7 | 130 to 160 lb |
| Single steel, insulated | 180 to 220 lb |
| Double steel, uninsulated, 16x7 | 200 to 250 lb |
| Double steel, insulated | 250 to 320 lb |
| Double aluminum | 180 to 250 lb |
| Double fiberglass | 200 to 280 lb |
| Double vinyl | 220 to 290 lb |
| Double wood | 300 to 450 lb |
| Full-view glass and aluminum | 250 to 400 lb |

Those are approximate. Actual weight depends on the manufacturer, the gauge, the insulation and the glazing.

## Why the number matters

The springs, not the opener, carry the door. The opener only guides a door the springs are already holding.

Spring size is calculated from door weight: the wire size, inside diameter and length together determine how much weight the spring will hold and for how many cycles. Get it wrong and one of two things happens.

**Spring too weak.** The door is heavy, it will not stay halfway when you lift it, and the opener does the lifting. The opener wears out early, sometimes within two years.

**Spring too strong.** The door wants to fly up. It does not close properly, it slams at the top, and the safety reversal on the opener behaves unpredictably.

## The test

Close the door, pull the manual release, lift it to waist height, and let go.

Stays put: correct. Drops: too weak, or worn out. Rises: too strong.

Do it once a year. It takes ten seconds and it is the single most informative check a homeowner can make.

## Where the mismatch comes from

**A replacement door without a replacement spring.** The most common cause by far. A new insulated door goes on a garage and the installer reuses the existing spring to save money. The door is out of balance from the first day.

**A DIY spring replacement.** Someone matched the length and wire size by eye, or ordered from a generic size chart rather than the door weight.

**A door that has gained weight.** Wood doors absorb water. A wood door whose finish has been neglected for a few winters is genuinely heavier than it was, and the spring that was correct is no longer correct.

## What to ask when getting springs replaced

Ask what wire size, inside diameter and length are being fitted, and what the cycle rating is.

A company that can tell you those four numbers has worked them from your door. A company that cannot is fitting whatever was on the van and hoping it is close enough, which is how doors end up permanently out of balance.
