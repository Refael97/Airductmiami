## The 1993 line

Federal rules in the United States required an entrapment protection system on residential garage door openers manufactured from January 1993. Essentially every manufacturer implemented it as photo eye sensors: a beam across the opening near the floor that reverses the door when broken.

If your opener has no such sensors, it was made before that. It is over thirty years old and it has no automatic way to stop for a child, a pet, or anything else under a closing door.

That is not a maintenance issue. It is the whole argument for replacement on its own.

## How to check

Look at the bottom of the track on each side of the door opening, near the floor. Two small units facing each other, with wires running back along the wall, are photo eyes.

No sensors, no protection, and no adjustment that adds it.

## The parts availability line

Past about twenty years, the question changes from safety to economics.

Manufacturers keep parts for a model for a number of years after production ends, then stop. Logic boards, travel modules and proprietary gear kits are the first to become unavailable.

Once parts are gone, a fault that would have been a $150 repair becomes a replacement, and you find that out at the worst possible time.

## The repair or replace arithmetic

| Situation | Answer |
|---|---|
| Under 15 years, common fault, parts available | Repair, $100 to $350 |
| Repair quote over half the price of a new unit | Replace |
| Parts no longer made | Replace |
| No photo eye sensors | Replace, regardless |
| Motor already rebuilt once | Replace |

A new opener installed is $281 to $630 depending on drive type.

## What to get instead

**Belt drive** if there is living space above or beside the garage. The noise difference is dramatic and the premium is small.

**Chain drive** if the garage is detached. Cheapest and most durable.

**Jackshaft** if there is not enough headroom for a rail, which in this region is common in under-house garages and converted garages.

**Battery backup** on any of them, in an area that loses power in storms.

## The thing to check before you spend

Pull the manual release and lift the door by hand. If it is heavy, or will not stay halfway, the springs are tired.

An opener guides a balanced door. It does not lift a heavy one. Fitting a new opener to a door with tired springs means buying the same opener again in a few years, and it is one of the most common ways people waste money on a garage.
