## How emergency pricing should work

There are two numbers, and they should be quoted separately.

The **call-out premium** covers dispatching outside normal hours: $200 to $350 in this market. It pays for the fact that somebody is coming out at ten at night or on a Sunday.

The **repair** is quoted at the same published price as any other day. A spring is $170 to $400 whether it broke at nine in the morning or nine at night.

When those two numbers are rolled into one, that is usually where the exploitation happens. Ask for them separately.

## When paying the premium is worth it

**Door stuck open on an attached house.** This is a security problem, not an inconvenience. Pay the premium.

**Car trapped and you need it.** If you need the vehicle before the next working day, pay the premium. If you do not, do not.

**Door hanging or partially off the track.** A door in that state can fall. It should be secured the same day.

**Door closed, spring broken, car outside.** This can wait until morning. Do not run the opener and do not force the door.

## What to do while you wait

Do not press the button. If a spring is broken or a cable has failed, the opener will make the damage worse with every attempt.

Find the manual release cord, which is the red handle hanging from the trolley. Pulling it disconnects the door from the opener. Do this only when the door is fully closed, because a door with a broken spring will drop if it is up and you release it.

If the door is stuck open and you cannot get it down, secure the garage another way and say so when you call. It changes the priority.

## What the premium should never be

It should never appear on the invoice without having been stated before dispatch. It should never be applied twice for the same visit. And it should never be used to justify a repair price above the published one.

Every one of those is a version of the same thing: using a stressful situation to charge more than the work is worth. It is the single most common complaint in this trade, and it is the reason our prices are on the website rather than in a technician's head.
