## What the cable does

Two steel cables run from the bottom brackets of the door up to drums on the torsion bar. When the spring unwinds, the drums wind the cables and lift the door. They carry the entire weight of the door, split between them.

That is why a single failed cable is not a small problem: the load transfers to one side, the door lifts crooked, and it jams or comes out of the track.

## What it costs

| Situation | Price |
|---|---|
| Cable slipped off the drum, undamaged | $95 to $150 |
| Cable replacement, pair | $100 to $350 |
| Cable and drum together | $250 to $425 |
| Cable replacement with spring work | Quoted together, usually less than separately |

The New York average for a straightforward cable replacement is around $225.

## The adjustment versus replacement distinction

This is where cable jobs get overcharged. If the cable came off the drum because the door hit something, or because it was left slack, and the cable itself is undamaged, it can be re-seated and tensioned. That is a $95 to $150 job.

If the cable is frayed, kinked or rusted, it has to be replaced. That is the $100 to $350 job.

The difference between the two is visible in thirty seconds. Ask to see the cable. A company that will not show you the part it says needs replacing is a company to be careful with.

## Why cables fail

**Rust at the bottom bracket.** The bracket sits at floor level where water, salt and road grit collect. On coastal properties in Freeport, Long Beach, Howard Beach and Mill Basin this is the single most common cause we see.

**A scored drum.** A drum with a rough groove saws through the cable from the inside. Replacing the cable without replacing the drum buys you a year at most.

**Running out of balance.** A door with a tired spring puts more load on the cables than they were sized for. Fixing the cable without fixing the spring is treating the symptom.

**Age.** Cables are a wear part with a life measured in the same cycles as the springs around them.

## The warning signs

Look at the cable where it wraps the drum at the top of the track. Fraying there is the earliest sign. Near the bottom bracket, look for rust and for kinks.

A door that has started closing unevenly, with one side touching down before the other, is often telling you a cable has stretched or slipped.

Treat a frayed cable as a same-week job rather than a same-year one. When it goes, the door drops on one side, and the repair that follows is off-track correction at $150 to $700 rather than a cable at $225.
