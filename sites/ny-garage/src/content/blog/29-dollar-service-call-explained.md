## The arithmetic

A service visit means a van, fuel, insurance, a stocked parts inventory, and a technician's time including travel. In the New York metro area, an hour of a skilled trade technician's time alone is $50 to $150.

$29 does not cover the fuel and the technician's travel, let alone the visit.

That is not a criticism of the number, it is a description of what the number is. It is an advertising cost, priced deliberately below cost, because it gets somebody into a driveway.

## How the rest gets made

The sequence is consistent.

The technician arrives and takes the door apart, which is a reasonable thing to do to diagnose it. You are now in a position where the door is in pieces and the car may be trapped.

The quote arrives at that point. It covers springs, cables, drums, bearings and rollers, sometimes the opener. The total is several hundred to a couple of thousand.

Saying no is now difficult in a way it was not thirty minutes earlier. That difficulty is the product.

## Why it works on garage doors specifically

Three things make this trade unusually suitable for it.

**Nobody knows what anything costs.** Most people replace a garage door spring once or twice in a lifetime.

**The failure is urgent.** A broken spring traps a car. Urgency compresses the time available to think.

**The parts are invisible.** You cannot easily tell whether a drum is scored or a bearing is failing. You are taking somebody's word for it.

## The defence

**Ask for the price of the finished repair before dispatch.** Not the visit. The repair. In writing.

**Know the ranges.** Spring $170 to $400 each. Two springs $320 to $700. Cables $100 to $350. Opener installed $281 to $630. Off-track $150 to $700.

**Ask to see the failed parts.** All of them. Before they are replaced.

**Be willing to say no with the door open.** It is uncomfortable and it is allowed. Pay the agreed fee, decline the work, get a second quote.

## What an honest low fee looks like

Some legitimate companies do charge a modest diagnostic fee, credited against the work if you proceed. Nothing wrong with that.

The distinguishing question is whether they will also tell you what the repair costs before they come. A company that says "the visit is $59, and a standard spring replacement runs $170 to $400 depending on what we find" is being straightforward.

A company that says only "$29 to come out" and will not discuss anything else has told you where the money comes from.
