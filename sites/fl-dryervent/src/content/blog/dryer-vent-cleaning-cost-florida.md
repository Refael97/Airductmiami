Dryer vent cleaning in Florida costs $100 to $200 with Dryer Vent Florida. Where your visit lands in that range depends on how long the run is, where it ends outside, and how much lint is packed inside it. If the duct is crushed, disconnected, made of foil or simply too long for the dryer, cleaning will not solve the problem, and a repair or replacement at $200 to $600 is the honest answer.

## What the $100 to $200 covers

A proper cleaning is more than pushing a brush through the wall. Every standard visit follows the same steps:

1. We measure airflow at the outside termination before touching anything, so there is a starting number.
2. We pull the dryer out, disconnect the transition hose and check it for kinks, crushing and lint.
3. We run a rotary brush and compressed air through the full length of the duct, from both ends where both ends can be reached.
4. We clear the wall hood or roof cap and make sure the damper swings freely and closes.
5. We reconnect, run the dryer and take a second airflow reading.

The before and after readings are the part most people never get. They show whether the vent now moves air properly, or whether something other than lint is still holding it back.

## What moves the price inside the range

The crew, the tools and the steps are the same in every home. What changes is the time and access the job takes.

| Factor | Toward $100 | Toward $200 |
|---|---|---|
| Run length | Short straight run through an exterior wall | Long run through an attic or across the house |
| Termination | Wall hood reachable from the ground | Roof cap that needs a ladder and roof access |
| Bends | One or two fittings | Several elbows that slow the brush |
| Buildup | A normal layer of lint | Heavy packing, lint caked with moisture |
| Access to the dryer | Open laundry room with space to pull the unit | Stacked unit in a closet or tight alcove |
| Cap condition | Damper moves freely | Damper stuck with lint, light debris in the hood |

A one-story home with the laundry against an exterior garage wall usually sits near the bottom of the range. A two-story home where the dryer is upstairs and the duct climbs through the attic to a roof cap usually sits near the top.

## When the price goes outside the range

Two situations are quoted before we book, so nobody is surprised on the day.

The first is a nest packed into a roof cap. A small nest in a wall hood is part of a normal visit. A cap filled solid with twigs, grass, lint and droppings can take far longer and sometimes needs a new cap. Our article on [bird nests in dryer vents](/blog/bird-nest-in-dryer-vent/) covers what we look for.

The second is a run that has to be opened. If the duct cannot be reached from either end, or a joint has come apart inside a wall or ceiling, the job becomes a repair. We show you what we found and quote it before any work starts.

## When repair or replacement is the real answer

Cleaning restores airflow in a duct that is sound. It does nothing for a duct that is damaged or wrong for the job. These are the conditions where we recommend repair or replacement instead of, or after, a cleaning:

| What we find | Why cleaning will not fix it | Usual answer |
|---|---|---|
| Foil or vinyl hose inside a wall or ceiling | Not allowed concealed, crushes easily, traps lint | Replace with rigid metal duct |
| Duct flattened behind the dryer or in the attic | Air cannot pass the crushed section | Replace the damaged section |
| Joints pulled apart in the attic | Lint and wet air dump into the attic | Reconnect and seal with foil tape |
| Screws through the joints | Screw tips snag lint and build plugs | Rebuild the joints without fasteners |
| Run longer than the code allows | The dryer cannot push air that far | Reroute on a shorter path |
| Cap with a screen, or no damper | Screens clog, an open cap lets pests in | Replace the termination |

Repair, replacement and new installation run $200 to $600. The spread reflects how much duct is replaced, how many fittings are involved and whether the new route passes through a wall, an attic or a roof. The details of that work are on our [dryer vent repair](/services/dryer-vent-repair/) page.

## Condos and apartment buildings

Condo and apartment work is priced per unit, and each building is quoted as a whole. Shared risers, roof access and coordination with management all shape a building quote, so we look at the layout first. If you own a unit and are not sure which parts of the vent are yours to maintain, start with our guide to [condo dryer vent responsibility](/blog/condo-dryer-vent-responsibility/).

## Comparing quotes fairly

You will see lower numbers advertised. Before comparing, ask what is included. A visit that only vacuums the first few feet behind the dryer, or only clears the outside hood, leaves the middle of the run untouched, and the middle of a long attic run is often where lint settles.

Three questions sort out most quotes. Is airflow measured before and after? Is the duct cleaned from both ends? Is the damper checked and freed? Then ask what happens if they find foil hose in a wall or a joint that has separated. A cleaning that ignores those problems costs less on the day and more later.

## How to keep your visit toward the lower end

A few things make the visit faster and simpler:

- Clear a path to the dryer and the floor space around it.
- Tell us where the vent ends outside, if you know, and whether it is on the roof.
- Mention a stacked unit or closet installation when you book.
- Clean the lint screen after every load, which slows buildup between visits.

Regular cleaning also keeps each visit routine. A run that has gone years without attention takes longer and is more likely to hide damage. Our guide on [how often to clean a dryer vent](/blog/how-often-clean-dryer-vent/) sets out sensible schedules by household.
