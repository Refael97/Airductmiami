Water and a dryer vent should never meet, which is why finding drips behind the dryer or a stain on the ceiling under the attic run catches people off guard. The good news is that the cause is nearly always one of two things, and the time the water appears tells you which.

## Rain or condensation: the quick test

Put a dry towel on the floor behind the dryer or under the drip, then watch for a few days.

- **Wet after rain, dryer not used:** water is getting in at the termination.
- **Wet after several loads, no rain:** exhaust moisture is condensing inside the duct.
- **Wet all the time, no pattern:** probably not the vent. Check the washer hoses, the water heater and, if the air handler shares the laundry closet, as it does in some Florida condos, its condensate drain.

That third case saves a lot of wasted effort. A slow washer hose leak can look a lot like a vent problem from the front of the machine.

## When it is rain

Every dryer termination needs a damper that opens with the exhaust and closes when the dryer stops. The damper is the only thing keeping rain out of the duct. Florida is hard on them.

| Failure | Typical cause | What you might notice |
|---|---|---|
| Damper stuck open | Lint packed in the hinge, salt air corrosion | Water after storms, insects in the duct |
| Cracked roof cap | Years of sun on a plastic cap | Drips at the dryer after heavy rain |
| Failed seal around the cap or hood | Old sealant or flashing pulled loose | Wet drywall around the wall hood or a ceiling stain |
| Open pipe, no cap | Cap left off after a reroof | Water, debris and birds in the run |

A screen over the termination is not the answer. It catches lint and blocks the exhaust, and the code does not allow one on a dryer outlet. The fix is a proper dryer termination with a working damper, sealed or flashed for the weather. Our comparison of [roof and wall dryer vents](/blog/roof-vs-wall-dryer-vent/) explains why roof caps are the ones that most often let water in.

After hurricane season it is worth checking from the ground that the hood or cap is still in place and the flap closes.

## When it is condensation

A load of wet laundry sends a surprising amount of water out of the dryer as vapor. While the exhaust stays hot, that vapor stays vapor. Let the air cool along the way and some of it turns back into water inside the duct.

Three conditions make that likely:

1. **A long run.** The exhaust loses heat with every foot.
2. **A run through a cool space.** In a condo, a duct crossing an air conditioned dropped ceiling is cooled from the outside, and moisture condenses on the inside.
3. **A run that rises to the roof.** Water that forms near the top runs back down the duct to the dryer.

Condensation alone is a nuisance. Condensation with a sag is a repair.

## The sagging attic run

In many single story homes the duct lies across the attic between trusses. If it was not supported along its length, it bows over the years and forms a low point. Condensate settles there, lint joins it, and the result is a wet plug that does not move. That plug is usually the musty smell.

It is also heavy. Eventually the weight pulls a joint apart, and the dryer starts exhausting warm, wet, lint laden air straight into the attic beside the insulation and framing. A ceiling stain under the duct route is often the first thing anyone sees.

Cleaning the plug out is only half the job. The run has to be lifted and supported, ideally with a gentle fall toward the termination where the route allows, so water drains out rather than collecting. If the attic section turns out to be foil or plastic hose, it needs to become rigid metal. Our guide to [foil versus rigid dryer duct](/blog/foil-vs-rigid-dryer-duct/) covers why.

## What to do now

- Stop using the dryer until you know where the water is coming from.
- If water is near the outlet or cord, do not touch anything wet. Switch off the dryer breaker if you can reach the panel safely.
- Note whether the water followed rain or laundry.
- From the ground, check whether the outside flap is closed with the dryer off.

## A note on mold

Damp lint inside the duct can turn musty and grow mold, and that goes away when the run is cleaned and the water source is fixed. Mold on drywall, framing or insulation around a leaking duct is a different job: mold remediation in walls and structure is separate licensed work in Florida. We do not sample or test for mold.
