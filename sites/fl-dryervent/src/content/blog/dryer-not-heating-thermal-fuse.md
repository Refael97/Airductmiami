A dryer that tumbles with cold air, or will not start at all, looks like an appliance problem. Sometimes it is. Very often, though, the dryer did exactly what it was built to do: the exhaust got too hot because the vent was restricted, and the thermal fuse cut out to protect the house. If that is the story, a new fuse buys you a few weeks.

## Two safety parts, two jobs

Most electric and gas dryers carry two heat safety parts, and both respond to the same thing: heat that is not leaving with the air.

| Part | What it does | Resets? |
|---|---|---|
| High limit thermostat | Switches the heat off when the cabinet runs hot, back on when it cools | Yes, by itself |
| Thermal fuse | Opens for good when exhaust temperature passes its rating | No, it must be replaced |

On a healthy vent, neither does much. On a restricted vent, the high limit cycles the heat on and off load after load, which is part of why drying times stretch out. When the heat builds past what the limit can manage, the fuse opens. Depending on the model, the dryer then either tumbles with no heat or will not run at all.

## Why the vent is usually behind it

A dryer moves heat out with air. When lint narrows the duct, less air leaves, and the heat stays in the cabinet and the first few feet of exhaust, right where the fuse sits. The fuse is not the fault. It is the messenger.

Look back over the last few months. A blown fuse rarely comes out of nowhere. Common warnings beforehand:

- Towels needing a second cycle.
- A dryer top too hot to rest a hand on.
- A laundry room that turns warm and humid during a load.
- An outside flap that barely lifts.
- Lint collecting around the door seal or the outside hood.

If two or three of those sound familiar, the vent is the likely cause. Our guide to a [dryer that takes two cycles](/blog/dryer-takes-two-cycles-to-dry/) walks through how to separate a vent problem from a load or washer problem.

## Rule out the easy causes first

**The breaker.** An electric dryer runs on a double breaker. If only one half trips, many dryers keep tumbling but the element gets no power. Switching the whole breaker off and on again fixes it. If it trips again, leave it off and call an appliance technician.

**The cycle.** Air fluff and no heat settings do exactly what they say. It is worth checking before anything else.

**A gas supply issue.** On a gas dryer, a closed shutoff, a failed igniter or a faulty gas valve will stop the heat. That is appliance work. If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

## What to do, in order

1. **Stop drying on cold air.** Running the dryer for days without heat does not fix anything and the restriction is still there.
2. **Check the transition hose.** Unplug the dryer, ease it out and look at the hose behind it. A foil hose crushed flat against the wall is enough to cause every symptom above.
3. **Check the outside termination.** If the drum still turns, run a cycle and go outside. The flap should open firmly and you should feel a steady stream of air. A weak flutter, packed lint or a nest means the vent is restricted.
4. **Call an appliance technician for the dryer.** The fuse, element, igniter and gas valve are their work. We do dryer vents only and do not repair dryers.
5. **Clear the vent before the dryer goes back into regular use.** Whether the vent is cleaned before or after the new fuse goes in matters less than making sure the dryer never runs on the blocked vent again.

If the hose disappears into the wall and you cannot tell where the blockage is, our article on a [dryer vent clogged inside the wall](/blog/dryer-vent-clogged-in-wall/) shows how to test the dryer and the wall run separately.

## Florida runs that set it up

Florida dryers work all year, and beach and pool towels shed a lot of lint. In many single story homes the laundry is in the middle of the house or in the garage, so the duct climbs through the attic to a roof cap that nobody looks at. A long run with several bends and a hidden termination is the classic setup for a fuse failure.

Summer attic heat does not help a run that was already marginal. Near the coast, salt air corrodes the damper hinge until the flap no longer opens fully. In condos, runs cross dropped ceilings and sometimes tie into a shared riser that the association maintains. Any of those can be the reason the fuse blew, which is why checking only the first few feet behind the dryer is not enough.

## Treat it as a warning

A blown thermal fuse means the exhaust reached a temperature the dryer was designed never to reach. The US Fire Administration names failure to clean as the leading cause of home clothes dryer fires. Our article on [dryer vent fire risk](/blog/dryer-vent-fire-risk/) sets out those figures.

If you smell burning, stop the dryer, unplug it if the plug is safe to reach, and leave it off until it has been checked. If you see smoke or flame, get everyone out and call 911 from outside.
