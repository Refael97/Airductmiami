**If a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding or you smell gas, stop reading. Get everyone out, pets included, and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.**

Most of what gets written about dryer vents is about lint and fire. For a gas dryer there is a second reason the vent matters: it is the only way the burner's exhaust leaves the house. Carbon monoxide has no color and no smell, so a vent problem on a gas dryer can go unnoticed until someone feels unwell.

## Where the carbon monoxide comes from

A gas dryer heats air with a burner fed by natural gas or propane. Combustion produces heat, water vapor, carbon dioxide and a small amount of carbon monoxide. All of it mixes with the air passing through the drum and travels out the exhaust duct with the lint.

Two things raise the risk. The first is exhaust that cannot get out. The second is a burner that is not burning cleanly, which produces more carbon monoxide than it should. Lint drifting into the burner area can cause that, and so can a burner that needs service. The first is a vent problem. The second is an appliance problem.

## Vent faults that keep exhaust indoors

| Fault | Where the exhaust ends up |
|---|---|
| Transition hose pulled off the dryer or the wall | Straight into the laundry room or garage |
| Hose crushed behind the dryer | Backs up into the cabinet and leaks at the seams |
| Joint separated inside a wall or ceiling | Inside the structure instead of outdoors |
| Lint packed along the run | Leaves slowly, with some held in the dryer and room |
| Stuck damper or nest in the termination | Blocked at the very end of the run |
| Indoor lint trap or bucket instead of a duct | Indoors, every load |

The disconnected hose is the one we would put first. It happens when a dryer is pushed back after cleaning behind it, or when a new dryer is connected to an old hose that was never clamped properly. A gas dryer should never run on an indoor lint trap, and our article on [ventless dryers in Florida condos](/blog/ventless-dryers-florida-condos/) explains why those boxes are not a substitute for a vent.

## Signs that something is wrong

A carbon monoxide alarm is the only reliable way to know the gas is present. Still, some signs deserve attention:

- Headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea or confusion while the dryer is running.
- Several people in the home feeling the same way at once.
- Symptoms that ease when you leave the house.
- A laundry room that turns hot and humid, or windows that fog, during a load.
- Long drying times and an outside flap that barely moves.

If anyone has symptoms, do not wait to find the cause. Get outside and call 911.

## If the alarm sounds or you smell gas

1. Leave the house with everyone and the pets. Do not stay to investigate.
2. If you smell gas, do not switch lights or appliances on or off, and do not use a phone inside.
3. Call 911 or your gas utility from outside. On propane, your propane supplier can also help.
4. Stay out until responders tell you it is safe.
5. Leave the dryer off until the dryer, the gas connection and the vent have all been checked.

## Alarms and placement

A smoke alarm does not detect carbon monoxide. You need a CO alarm or a combination unit. Follow the maker's instructions, and as a general guide:

- Keep at least one CO alarm on each level of the home and near sleeping areas.
- Add one near the laundry if the gas dryer is in a garage or closet.
- Test it monthly and replace it at the end of the service life the maker lists.

Laundry in the garage is common in Florida. An attached garage with a gas dryer on a poor vent can push exhaust into the house through the entry door, so an alarm near that door is worth having.

## Keeping a gas dryer vent safe

- Clean the lint screen every load.
- Have the full vent cleaned once a year. With a gas dryer, do not stretch that interval.
- Check that the transition hose is attached at both ends and not crushed.
- When moving the dryer, do not kink or stretch the flexible gas connector.
- Check monthly from outside that the flap opens when the dryer runs.

Our guide on [how often to clean a dryer vent](/blog/how-often-clean-dryer-vent/) covers intervals for long runs and busy households. If you replaced the dryer recently, it is worth having the connection behind it checked, because that is where installation shortcuts usually show up.
