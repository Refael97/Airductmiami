"Furnace cleaning" is one of the stranger searches we see from Florida, because most houses here do not have a furnace. What they have does need cleaning, though, and the first cold night of the year usually tells people so through every register in the house.

## The short answer

In South Florida the heat almost always comes from **electric heat strips** inside the air handler, from a **heat pump**, or from both. There is no burner and no flame. So what people mean by furnace cleaning here is cleaning the air handler: the blower wheel, the evaporator coil, the drain pan, and the dust sitting on the heat strips. That is our [AC and air handler cleaning](/services/hvac-cleaning/), $200 to $450, and the best time to do it is before the first cold front.

## What is actually heating your house

Open the closet or the garage cabinet where your air handler lives and you will find, in most Florida homes, a blower, a cooling coil and a bank of heat strips. When the thermostat calls for heat, the strips glow like the elements in a toaster and the blower pushes air across them. If you have a heat pump, the outdoor unit runs the air conditioner in reverse and the strips only come on to help on colder nights.

Gas furnaces do exist in Florida, mostly in North Florida and some older Central Florida homes. If you have one, the burner and heat exchanger need a licensed HVAC contractor, and the advice below about the air handler and the ducts still applies to the rest of the system.

## Why the heat smells like burning the first night

It is nearly always dust. In Palm Beach County the heat may run for a few weeks a year, which leaves eleven months for dust to settle on elements that are cold. The first time they energize, the dust burns off and the smell goes through every room.

- **Normal:** a dusty, slightly burnt smell that fades within the first hour of running.
- **Not normal:** a smell that keeps coming back, a hot plastic or electrical smell, visible smoke, or a breaker that trips. Turn the system off at the thermostat and call an HVAC contractor. That is an electrical fault, not a cleaning job.

A clean air handler shortens the first-night smell and makes the strips run cleaner, but it does not replace an electrical check if something is actually wrong.

## What a proper "furnace cleaning" includes in Florida

For an all-electric system, the useful work is:

1. **Blower wheel** removed or cleaned in place. A caked wheel moves far less air, which makes every register weak in summer and winter alike.
2. **Evaporator coil** cleaned with a coil-safe cleaner. In Florida this is where the musty smell and the mold usually start.
3. **Drain pan and condensate line** cleaned and flushed, because a blocked line is the most common reason an air handler shuts itself off in summer.
4. **Cabinet and heat strip area** vacuumed so there is less to burn off next winter.

Duct cleaning is a separate job. If the house is dusty all year or the smell comes from every register even after the air handler is clean, the ductwork is the next place to look, and our [air duct cleaning](/services/air-duct-cleaning/) runs $300 to $600 per system.

## Palm Beach County in particular

Most of the "furnace cleaning" searches we see come from Greenacres, Lake Worth Beach, West Palm Beach and Boynton Beach, which makes sense: these are the places with enough cold fronts for the heat to run, but so few that the strips sit dusty for most of the year. The older houses in [Lake Worth Beach](/service-areas/lake-worth/) often had their air systems added decades after they were built, and in [Greenacres](/service-areas/greenacres/) a lot of the equipment is reaching the age where the blower and coil have never been properly cleaned.

## What not to pay for

- A "furnace inspection" or "heat exchanger check" on a house with no furnace.
- A carbon monoxide test sold as part of cleaning an all-electric system. Heat strips and heat pumps do not burn fuel. Keep a CO alarm if you have gas appliances or an attached garage; that is the right protection.
- A bundled "furnace and duct special" at a price that does not match the work. Ask what is cleaned, and what it costs, before anyone opens the cabinet.

## The bottom line

In Florida, furnace cleaning is air handler cleaning. Do it in the fall, before the first cold front, and treat a burning smell that does not fade in the first hour as an electrical question rather than a dust one.
