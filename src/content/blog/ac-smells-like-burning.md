Most homeowners in Florida go months without thinking about the air handler, so a burning smell coming out of the registers is alarming. Sometimes it should be. Sometimes it is a layer of dust doing what dust does on a hot surface. The difference comes down to what it smells like, when it shows up and whether it goes away.

## Shut it off now if

- It smells like **burning wires, hot plastic or hot metal**.
- The smell **does not fade**, or it comes back every time the system starts.
- You hear **buzzing or humming** at the air handler or the outdoor unit along with the smell.
- A **breaker has tripped**. Do not keep resetting it.

Turn the system off at the thermostat. If the smell continues, switch off the AC breaker too. **If you see smoke or flames, get everyone out and call 911.**

An AC that is off for a few hours in a Florida afternoon is uncomfortable. An electrical fault that keeps running is a different kind of problem.

## Three questions that narrow it down

**What mode is the thermostat in?** A burnt dust smell in heat or emergency heat mode points to the heat strips. The same smell in cooling mode points somewhere else.

**What does it smell like?** Toasted dust is one thing. Burning plastic, a sharp electrical smell or hot metal are another. Musty, damp or dirty socks is not a burning smell at all; that is usually mold on the coil, and our guide on [why an AC smells musty](/blog/why-ac-smells-musty/) covers it.

**Where is it strongest?** At every register equally, at the air handler closet or garage cabinet, or at the outdoor unit. Note it before you call anyone. It saves time.

## What is usually behind it

### Dust on the heat strips

Most South Florida homes do not have a furnace. Heat comes from electric heat strips inside the air handler, from a heat pump, or from both. The strips sit cold for most of the year and collect dust, and the first cold night, or the first time the system drops into emergency heat, that dust burns off and the smell goes through the house. If it fades within the first hour, it is normal. Our page on [furnace cleaning in Florida](/blog/furnace-cleaning-florida/) explains what that heating setup is and when to clean it.

If it returns every time the heat runs, or lingers past the first hour, it is no longer seasonal dust. Turn it off and have it checked.

### An overworked blower motor

The blower in the air handler runs a lot of hours in this climate. Worn bearings, or a motor pushing against a clogged filter and a blower wheel caked with grime, run hotter than they should. The smell is hot metal or varnish, sometimes with a hum. A motor problem is for an HVAC contractor. A motor that is fine but is fighting a dirty wheel is a cleaning problem.

### An electrical part

Capacitors, contactors and wiring, in the air handler or the outdoor condenser, can overheat and smell like burning plastic. This is the smell to respect most. Switch off at the thermostat and the breaker and do not turn it back on to see whether it went away. This is electrical work for an HVAC contractor, and not something we do.

### Smells pulled in from somewhere else

When the air handler sits in the garage or the ducts run through the attic, a leak on the **return side** can pull air from those spaces into the system: car exhaust, stored chemicals, superheated attic air. If the smell arrives in waves, or lines up with something happening outside, the system is not burning; it is breathing from the wrong place. That is an [air duct repair](/services/air-duct-repair/) job, after a pressure test finds the leak.

## What you can check with the system off

1. **The filter.** If it is gray and clogged, replace it.
2. **Around the air handler.** Boxes, laundry or plastic bags pushed up against the cabinet or the return grille should come away.
3. **The breaker panel.** A tripped AC breaker with a burning smell is an electrical warning, not something to reset and forget.

Do not open the air handler or condenser panels. There is live voltage inside even when the thermostat is off.
