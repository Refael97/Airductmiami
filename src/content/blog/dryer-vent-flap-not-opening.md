The flap on the outside of your house is the only part of the dryer vent most people ever see, and it is a surprisingly honest gauge of the whole run. When the dryer is heating, that flap should swing open and stay open on a steady push of warm air. When it stays shut, flutters or barely lifts, the air is either stuck somewhere in the run or leaving through a gap before it reaches the outside.

Either way, **stop using the dryer until you know which**. Hot exhaust that cannot leave stays in the machine, and that is how a vent problem becomes [a dryer that overheats and shuts off](/blog/dryer-overheating-shutting-off/).

## Check it in five minutes

Clear the lint screen, put a couple of damp towels in on a heated cycle and wait about five minutes. Then go outside.

- **Flap fully open, strong warm air:** the vent is moving air.
- **Flap fluttering, weak air:** there is a restriction.
- **Nothing at all:** a full blockage, or the duct has come apart somewhere.

If the vent exits through the roof, do not climb up. You can sometimes see the cap from the ground. If not, what the dryer is doing, long cycles and a hot cabinet, is enough to go on.

## Work from the outside in

### At the hood

Lint leaving a Florida dryer is damp and it sticks. Over time it forms a crust around the mouth of the hood and on the flap hinge until the flap no longer swings freely. With the dryer off you can clear what you can see by hand. If the flap sticks again within a few loads, the lint is coming from further in.

### The cap itself

Two kinds of cap cause most of the trouble. One is a standard hood with a **screen** added to keep birds out. The other is a **louvered cap** with several small slats, each of which catches lint and sticks shut. Both trap lint from the inside, and a freshly cleaned vent with either one on the end starts underperforming within weeks. The cap you want is a hooded cover with **a single free swinging damper** that opens on the exhaust and drops shut when the dryer stops.

### Nests and wasps

Birds and insects stay active most of the year here, and a warm hood is an inviting spot. A nest behind the flap jams it and blocks the duct. If there are no birds or eggs, the visible part can come out with gloves, though material usually goes deeper than you can reach. **If you hear chicks or see eggs, leave it and ask first**: many native birds are protected under federal law. If it is wasps, keep your hands out of it.

### Behind the dryer

The short transition hose between the dryer and the wall gets crushed every time the dryer is pushed back. A hose folded flat can cost more airflow than the entire rest of the run. Pull the dryer out while it is cold and look.

### Inside the wall or attic

If the flap barely moves but you notice warmth, humidity or lint in the laundry room, the attic or the garage, a joint has probably separated. The exhaust is leaving there instead of at the hood, so the flap never gets a push. Cleaning does not fix this. In a Florida attic it also means hot, wet air and lint landing where the house least needs them.

### Lint packed in the run

When the flap moves but weakly, packed lint in the duct is the most common answer, especially at elbows and on long runs up to the roof. It tends to arrive with the other [signs of a clogged dryer vent](/blog/signs-of-clogged-dryer-vent/): damp clothes after a full cycle, two cycles per load, a hot laundry room.

## If something feels wrong

A weak vent paired with a burning smell is the most serious version of this problem. Unplug the dryer and do not run it again until the cause is found. If you ever see smoke or flames, keep the dryer door closed, get out of the house and call 911.

The flap outside tells you how the whole vent is doing. If it will not open, clear the hood, take off any screen and check the hose behind the dryer.
