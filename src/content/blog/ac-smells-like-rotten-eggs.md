## If you smell gas, start here

Natural gas and propane have no smell of their own. Suppliers add an odorant that smells like rotten eggs or sulfur so that a leak is impossible to miss. So a rotten egg smell coming through the vents has to be treated as gas until you know otherwise.

1. **Get everyone out**, pets included.
2. **Leave the switches alone.** Do not turn lights on or off, do not touch the thermostat, do not unplug anything, and do not light a match or lighter.
3. **Call from outside**, well away from the house: your gas utility or 911, and your propane supplier if you use propane.
4. **Stay out** until you are told it is safe.

Everything below assumes gas has been ruled out, or that there is no gas service to the house at all.

## Why the AC carries the smell

Most South Florida air conditioners do not burn gas. Heat comes from electric strips in the air handler. But the system moves a lot of air, and it will happily pick up a smell that starts somewhere else. A gas water heater, range, dryer or pool heater nearby, or a return that draws air from the garage, can put a smell into the airstream. That is why the vents are where people notice it, even when the AC is not the source.

## The thirty minute test

With gas ruled out, turn the system off at the thermostat and wait half an hour.

- **Smell unchanged everywhere:** the source is in the house, not the AC. Check bathrooms, drains and water.
- **Smell fades with the system off and returns when it runs:** the system is either drawing it in or holding it.
- **Strongest at one register:** suspect that duct.
- **Strongest at the air handler closet:** look at the drain pan and condensate line.

## The usual suspects

### Sewer gas from a dry trap

Every drain has a U shaped trap that holds water to block sewer gas. Leave a guest bathroom or floor drain unused for a few weeks and the water evaporates. This is very common in homes that sit empty. In Naples, many seasonal houses reopen in November after a closed up summer, as the [Naples page](/service-areas/naples/) describes, and vacation rentals in [Kissimmee](/service-areas/kissimmee/) can go weeks between guests. Run every tap and flush every toilet, and the smell is often gone within a day.

If it persists, the system may be pulling it in. A return that leaks near a plumbing vent in the attic or a wall, or a condensate line tied into the house plumbing with a dried out trap, can feed sewer gas straight to the blower. The plumbing side is a plumber's job. The leaky return is [air duct repair](/services/air-duct-repair/), priced after a pressure test at $250 to $2,000 and up depending on scope.

### Stagnant water in the drain pan

The coil sheds water into a pan under the air handler, and a condensate line carries it outside. When that line clogs, the water sits, grows slime and bacteria, and starts to smell sour, swampy or sulfurous. A wet closet floor or an AC that keeps shutting off on its float switch often comes with it. [HVAC cleaning](/services/hvac-cleaning/) covers the pan, the drain line, the coil, the blower wheel and the cabinet, at $200 to $450 per air handler. If the smell is closer to damp laundry than eggs, our guide to [why an AC smells musty](/blog/why-ac-smells-musty/) is the better match.

### A dead animal

A rat or lizard that dies in the attic or inside a duct smells sweet and rotten, more like meat than eggs, and gets worse in the heat. Flies near one register are a strong clue. A wildlife or pest control company removes the animal. After that, the affected run is cleaned, sanitized if there is reason to, and repaired if the animal got in through a tear.

### Sulfur in well water

Homes on a well can get a rotten egg smell from the water itself, usually strongest at the hot taps. It has nothing to do with the AC, but it fills the house and gets blamed on the vents. If the shower smells worse than the registers, call a plumber or a water treatment company.
