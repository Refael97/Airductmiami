It is one of the less pleasant Florida experiences: a roach walks out of the ceiling register over your bed, or you pull a supply grille to clean it and find the box behind it full of droppings. People tend to assume the ducts are infested. Usually the ducts are just the corridor. The roaches are coming from somewhere else and the duct system gives them a dark, quiet way in.

## Which roach tells you where to look

Two kinds turn up in Florida homes, and they point in different directions.

- **Large, reddish brown, and able to fly.** The American cockroach, the one many people here call a palmetto bug. It lives outside, in attics, in drains and in leaf litter. When one comes out of a ceiling register, it has most likely come down from the attic.
- **Small, light brown, with two dark stripes behind the head.** The German cockroach. It lives indoors, in kitchens and bathrooms. If these are coming out of a register near the kitchen, the infestation is already inside and the ducts are a hiding place.

## How they get into the system

**The register box gap.** Behind every grille is a metal box, the boot, that connects to the duct. The gap between the boot and the drywall is often never sealed. From the attic side, that gap is an open door.

**Damaged ductwork.** A torn flex jacket, a joint that has pulled apart or a crushed run lets insects in, along with attic air.

**Ducts under the floor.** Tampa's raised bungalows in Seminole Heights, Hyde Park and Tampa Heights run their ducts through the crawl space, within reach of anything living under the house. A run that has separated at a joint or been crushed by storage is open to the crawl space, as the [Tampa service area page](/service-areas/tampa/) describes.

**The air handler closet.** It is damp and warm, especially if the drain pan holds water. Gaps in the return platform let whatever lives in the closet into the system.

**A missing or loose filter.** Without a filter seated properly, the return grille is an open path to the air handler.

## Why it matters after the roaches are gone

Roach droppings and remains are a well known allergen and one of the most overlooked triggers in warm climates. If someone in the house has asthma or allergies that are worse indoors, years of roach debris in the register boxes can be part of the problem. Our article on [air duct cleaning and allergies](/blog/air-duct-cleaning-allergies/) covers the wider picture.

## What not to do

- Do not spray insecticide into the registers. The blower distributes it.
- Do not run a fogger with the system on.
- Do not tape the registers shut for good. You lose airflow to the room and the entry point stays open.

## What you can check yourself

With the system off, take down a ceiling grille and shine a flashlight around the boot. If you can see daylight from the attic or a gap at the drywall edge, you have found an entry point. Look inside the box: pepper sized black specks and small brown capsules are droppings and egg cases. Then check that the filter is in and the return grille closes, and look at the air handler closet for standing water. Leave the air handler panels closed, since there is live voltage inside.
