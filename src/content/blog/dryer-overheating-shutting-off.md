Your dryer runs for a few minutes, the top gets hot enough that you pull your hand back, and then it stops. Or it keeps tumbling but the clothes come out cold and damp. Neither is a random failure. Usually the dryer is protecting itself from heat it cannot get rid of, and the place to look first is not inside the machine. It is the path the air takes to get outside.

**Stop using the dryer until you know why.** Running "just one more load" puts it back against the same blockage.

## What the dryer is actually doing

Every clothes dryer has a small chain of heat protection:

- A **cycling thermostat** that switches the heating element or gas burner on and off to hold normal temperature.
- A **high limit thermostat** that cuts the heat when the temperature climbs past normal. When it trips repeatedly, the dryer seems to shut off, even if the drum keeps turning on cool air.
- A **thermal fuse**, the last line of defense. Once it blows, the dryer loses heat or, on many models, will not start at all.

Some newer dryers also show a vent warning on the display. If yours does, believe it. The machine is measuring that air is not leaving.

What matters is the order of events. Heat builds because exhaust is restricted, the limit trips, and eventually the fuse goes. **A blown thermal fuse is the symptom.** Replacing it without fixing the airflow buys a few loads, sometimes only a few days, before the new one blows the same way. Never bypass a thermal fuse to keep a dryer running.

## The five minute test

You can sort out most of this yourself before calling anyone.

1. **Clean the lint screen** and look into the slot it sits in. Packed lint in the slot is an early clue.
2. **Pull the dryer out a few inches** while it is cold and look at the hose behind it. A foil accordion hose folded flat or bent into a tight kink can choke nearly all of the airflow on its own.
3. **Run the dryer on heat** with a couple of damp towels, then go outside to the vent hood after about five minutes.
4. **Check the flap and feel the air.** You want the flap fully open and a strong, steady stream of warm air.

If the flap barely moves or the air is weak, the problem is between the dryer and the outside wall, and our guide to [a dryer vent flap that won't open](/blog/dryer-vent-flap-not-opening/) walks through what blocks it. If the air outside is strong and the dryer still shuts off, the fault is more likely inside the machine.

If the vent exits through the roof, do not climb up to check it. The behavior of the dryer and the condition of the hose are enough to go on.

## The usual causes, in order

1. **Lint packed in the duct.** The screen does not catch everything, and what gets past it settles at elbows and along long runs. Florida homes often send the vent up through the roof, which makes the run longer and the buildup faster.
2. **A crushed or overlong transition hose.** The dryer gets pushed back after every move or mop, and the hose has nowhere to go.
3. **A blocked hood.** Lint crusted on the flap hinge, a screen someone added to keep birds out, or a nest. Birds and wasps are active here most of the year.
4. **A joint that has come apart** inside a wall or the attic, so the exhaust escapes before it reaches the hood.
5. **Overloading.** A drum packed with wet beach towels cannot move air through the load. It rarely causes the problem alone, but it makes every other cause worse.
6. **A fault in the dryer itself.** A failing thermostat, a worn motor or a bad sensor. That is an appliance technician's job, and it is worth ruling out the vent first, because a motor that has spent years pushing against a restricted run is often the reason it failed.

If the clothes have been taking two cycles for months and now the dryer is shutting off too, bet on the vent. It is the same story as [a dryer that takes too long to dry](/blog/dryer-takes-too-long-to-dry/), further along.

## Why it matters beyond the laundry

Lint is the fuel in most dryer fires, and heat is what lights it. A dryer that keeps reaching its high limit is spending a lot of time at the exact conditions its safety devices exist to prevent. Our page on [dryer vent lint dangers](/blog/dryer-vent-lint-dangers/) covers how that buildup becomes a fire. If you notice a burning smell along with the heat, treat it as the more urgent of the two and stop using the dryer immediately.

If you ever see smoke or flames, keep the dryer door closed, get everyone out of the house and call 911.

## Keeping it from coming back

- Clear the lint screen every load.
- Leave room behind the dryer for the hose to make a gentle curve.
- Have the full vent cleaned at least once a year, more often for long or roof runs.
- Do not run the dryer overnight or while nobody is home.
