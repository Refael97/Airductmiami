A Florida house that smells musty is almost always a house with a moisture problem, and the frustrating part is that several trades each fix one piece of it. Call the wrong one first and you pay for a visit that ends with "that's not us." This guide sorts out who fixes what, including where we stop.

## The short answer

**Start with someone who will measure the humidity and look inside the AC system**, because what fixes the problem depends entirely on where the moisture is coming from. Then:

| What is causing it | Who fixes it |
| --- | --- |
| Dirty or moldy coil, slimy drain pan, clogged drain line | A duct and coil company (us) |
| Dirty ducts, or ducts that leak and pull in hot attic air | A duct and coil company (us) |
| Sweating ducts in the attic, missing duct insulation | A duct and coil company (us) |
| Need a whole-house dehumidifier installed | An HVAC contractor |
| Low refrigerant, a failing compressor, an oversized system | An HVAC contractor |
| A plumbing leak under a sink, in a wall or slab | A plumber |
| A roof, window or stucco leak | A roofer or a window and stucco contractor |
| Mold in drywall, framing or insulation | A licensed mold remediator; a licensed mold assessor for testing |

## Read the clues first

The pattern of the smell narrows it down before anyone comes out.

- **Musty for a few minutes when the AC kicks on, then it fades.** That is the coil, the drain pan or the blower wheel. Our guide to [why the AC smells musty](/blog/why-ac-smells-musty/) explains why it starts there.
- **Musty all the time, in every room, with clammy air and mildew on shoes or closet walls.** The whole house is too humid. That points at the system's ability to dry the air, the way it is set, or air leaking in.
- **Musty in one room or one wall, maybe with a stain.** Think leak first: a pipe, a window, a roof or a shower pan.
- **Vents dripping or ceilings spotting near a grille.** Ducts or grilles are sweating, usually from missing insulation in the attic or a leak pulling in humid air.

A hygrometer from a hardware store is worth having for this. Florida homes should sit between 30 and 50 percent relative humidity, and anything holding above 60 percent grows mildew. Our page on [ideal indoor humidity in Florida](/blog/ideal-indoor-humidity-florida/) covers the numbers.

## What needs an HVAC contractor

We tell you plainly when the answer is not ours. **Installing a whole-house dehumidifier**, adding or recovering **refrigerant**, replacing a compressor, and fixing an **oversized system** that cools the house too fast to dry it are HVAC contractor jobs in Florida. So is moving a thermostat or changing how the system is staged. When the readings and photos point there, you get them in writing so the contractor starts with evidence instead of a guess.

One thing you can try before anyone comes: set the thermostat fan to **AUTO**, not ON. With the fan on ON, the blower keeps running over a wet coil after the compressor stops and blows that water back into the house.

## What needs a plumber or a roofer

If the musty smell is local and there is a stain, a soft spot or a warped baseboard, the moisture is probably coming from a leak, not the air conditioner. A plumber finds pipe and slab leaks. A roofer or a window and stucco contractor finds water coming in from outside, which in Florida often shows up after a storm. Cleaning the AC will not help until the leak is fixed, and any mold in the drywall or framing that the leak caused is separate licensed work.
