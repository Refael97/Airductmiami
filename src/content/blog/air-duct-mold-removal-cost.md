Air duct mold removal in Florida costs **$500 to $3,000** for most homes. The number depends almost entirely on one thing: how far the mold has traveled from where it started. Here is the honest breakdown, what moves the price up, and how to tell a real quote from an inflated one.

## The price ranges

| Situation | Typical cost | What it involves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light growth on the coil only | $500 to $900 | Coil and drain pan cleaning, antimicrobial treatment |
| Coil plus the first runs of duct | $900 to $1,500 | Source-removal cleaning of affected runs, treatment |
| Full system contaminated | $1,200 to $3,000 | Whole-system negative-air cleaning, treatment, verification |
| Porous duct board, heavy growth | $3,000+ | Sections replaced, because contaminated duct board cannot be fully cleaned |

For comparison, a **standard air duct cleaning is $300 to $600**. Mold work costs more because it adds inspection, containment, EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment, and usually coil and drain line work.

## What actually moves the number

- **How far it spread.** Mold that is still on the coil is a contained job. Mold that has moved into the supply runs is a system job.
- **Your duct material.** Sheet metal cleans up well. Flexible duct with a porous inner liner, and duct board, often cannot be fully decontaminated and gets replaced instead.
- **Number of systems.** A two-system home is close to two jobs.
- **The moisture source.** Removing mold without fixing what fed it means it returns in months. A clogged condensate drain is a cheap fix; leaking ductwork in a hot attic is not. Sometimes the real repair costs more than the cleaning.
- **Access.** Attic air handlers in tight spaces take longer than a garage closet.

## Why Florida is its own price category

Mold needs moisture, food, and time. Our climate supplies all three year round: the evaporator coil stays cold and wet almost every day, humidity outside sits above 70 percent for months, and dust inside the ducts is the food. That is why mold in ducts is a routine call here and a rare one in dry states, and why fixing the humidity matters as much as the cleaning. The [ideal indoor humidity](/blog/ideal-indoor-humidity-florida/) target of 30 to 50 percent is the number that keeps it from coming back.

## What a real mold removal visit includes

1. **Camera inspection first**, with the technician showing you what is on the coil and inside the runs. If nobody shows you anything, you cannot verify what you are paying for.
2. **A written quote before work starts**, itemized.
3. **Containment and negative air**, so spores are pulled out of the house rather than pushed around it.
4. **Mechanical cleaning**, brushes and air whips, not just a fogger. Chemical alone on top of buildup is theater.
5. **EPA-registered antimicrobial** applied to cleaned surfaces.
6. **The moisture cause identified**, and told to you plainly even when it is not our work to fix.

## How to tell a fair quote from an inflated one

Mold is the easiest thing in this industry to exaggerate, because you cannot see inside your own ducts. Some ways to keep the conversation honest:

- **A price quoted on the phone before an inspection is not a quote**, it is a hook. Ranges on the phone are fine; a firm number is not.
- **Ask to see it.** A photo or camera feed of the actual growth in your system, not a stock photo of somebody else's ducts.
- **Be careful with "the whole system has to be replaced"** on the first visit. It is sometimes true, especially with duct board, and it should come with photos.
- **Watch for a small advertised fee that grows.** The same bait pattern that shows up in [air duct cleaning scams](/blog/air-duct-cleaning-scams/) shows up here, amplified, because fear does the selling.
- **A second opinion is reasonable** on anything above about $2,000. An honest company will not fight you on it.

## Air duct mold removal cost in Palm Beach County

Palm Beach County prices land in the same **$500 to $3,000** range as the rest of South Florida. Where you live in the county changes which end of it you are likely to be at.

- **Condos east of I-95**, in West Palm Beach, Lake Worth Beach, Boynton Beach and Delray Beach, usually have a closet air handler with short duct runs. Growth caught on the coil and drain pan of a system like that tends to sit at the low end.
- **Single family homes from the seventies through the nineties**, in Greenacres, Palm Springs, Lantana and the older parts of Boca Raton, usually have an attic air handler and long flex runs through a hot attic. When mold has moved into those runs, the flex often cannot be fully cleaned and gets replaced, which is what pushes a job toward the top of the range.
- **Newer homes out west**, in Wellington, Royal Palm Beach and the Acreage, are larger and often have two systems. Two systems is close to two jobs, so the total runs higher even when each system is in decent shape.
- **Homes near the Intracoastal and the beach** take more humid air through every door and window, so the moisture source is often the house rather than the equipment. A cleaning without a humidity fix tends to be a cleaning you repeat.

One thing that matters more than the price: Florida licenses mold remediation under chapter 468 of the Florida Statutes, and growth covering more than 10 square feet in total needs a licensed mold remediator. Ductwork reaches that size quickly, since a single 8 inch run has about two square feet of inside surface per foot of length. Before you pay anyone in Palm Beach County to "remove mold" from your ducts, ask for their state mold remediator licence number and check it. We are a duct cleaning company, not a mold remediator: we clean and sanitize systems to the NADCA source-removal standard at our published price, and when what we find is past that line, we say so and tell you who to call.

## Mold removal or an HVAC cleaning: which one are you pricing?

Not every musty smell is a mold removal job, and the difference is a few hundred dollars. If the camera shows a dirty, damp coil, a slimy drain pan and a caked blower wheel, but no growth on the cabinet walls or inside the ducts, the job is an [HVAC cleaning](/services/hvac-cleaning/) of the coil, blower, cabinet and drain line, at $200 to $450 per system. If the ducts are dusty but free of growth, it is a standard air duct cleaning at $300 to $600 per system. Mold removal pricing applies when there is visible growth that has to be contained, removed and treated, on the coil and cabinet or in the runs themselves.

The useful question to ask any company is which of the three the photos show, and why. If the answer is mold removal, the photos should show growth, not just dark dust on a wet coil.

## Will a UV light replace mold removal?

No, but it can keep you from paying for mold removal twice. A UV-C lamp aimed at the evaporator coil, $300 to $700 installed, keeps a coil that is wet nearly every day from growing a new colony. It does not reach inside the ducts, it does nothing to growth that is already there, and it does not fix the humidity. The order that works is removal first, then the moisture fix, then a lamp if the coil is where growth keeps starting.
