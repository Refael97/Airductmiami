Most of the time you should hear your duct system only as air leaving the registers. When a new sound shows up, a bang at startup, a whistle from the hallway ceiling, something scratching above the bedroom at night, it is usually telling you something specific. The trick is to work out which kind of noise it is and where it is coming from before deciding who to call.

## A quick way to narrow it down

Stand in the hallway near the return grille and listen through one full start, a few minutes of running, and the shutoff. Note three things: **what it sounds like, when it happens, and which register or patch of ceiling it comes from.** That is enough to sort almost every duct noise into one of the groups below.

## Popping and banging

**Sounds like:** a sharp metallic pop or a dull boom, right as the blower starts or right after it stops.

**What it is:** sheet metal moving. The plenum, the big box on top of or beside the air handler, and any metal trunk lines are thin. When the blower kicks on, the pressure inside pushes those walls out, and when it stops they spring back.

**Why it got louder:** in most cases, the system is short of air on the return side. A filter that has gone gray, a sofa pushed against the return grille, or a return that was undersized from the start all make the supply side work harder and the metal move more. Change the filter first. If one section still bangs, it can be stiffened, and if the return is too small, that is a duct design fix.

## Whistling

**Sounds like:** a steady whistle or hiss while the air runs.

**What it is:** air forced through a gap that is too narrow. That might be a nearly closed damper on a register, a filter so loaded the return grille sings, or an opening at a duct joint. Closing registers in spare rooms is one of the most common causes, because it raises pressure across the whole system.

Open every register, replace the filter, and listen again. A whistle that stays put at one spot in the ceiling is often a leak, and leaks rarely come alone. Uneven rooms, dust and higher bills tend to arrive with it, as our guide to [leaky air ducts](/blog/leaky-air-ducts-signs-fixes/) explains.

## Rattling and vibration

**Sounds like:** buzzing or ticking at one register.

Nine times out of ten it is a loose grille. Tighten the screws with the system running and the sound often stops. Some registers have adjustable louvers that rattle on their own. If the noise is deeper inside the duct, something may be loose in there: a strip of foil tape, a piece of insulation, or a toy that went down a floor register. An [air duct cleaning](/services/air-duct-cleaning/) clears that out, at $300 to $600 per system.

## Flapping

**Sounds like:** a plastic bag filling and collapsing.

That is almost always a loose flex duct or tape that has let go at a joint. Flex runs that sag between supports, or were stretched to reach an addition, are where it shows up first. This is repair work, not cleaning.

## Scratching and scurrying

**Sounds like:** footsteps or claws overhead, often at night and often with the AC off.

That is not your ductwork. It is an animal, usually rats or squirrels in the attic, and sometimes inside a duct they have torn open. Get a wildlife or pest control company to remove them and close the way they got in. In Tampa's raised bungalows, where the ducts run through the crawl space within reach of anything under the house, the same goes for noises below the floor; the [Tampa page](/service-areas/tampa/) explains how those systems are built. Afterward the ducts deserve a camera inspection, because animals leave urine, droppings and nesting behind, and that ends up at the registers.

## Noises from the air handler itself

A loud electrical buzz, a squeal like a dry bearing, or knocking from inside the cabinet is coming from the equipment, not the ducts. That is an HVAC contractor's job. The exception is a blower wheel caked with dirt, which throws it out of balance and makes it hum or vibrate. That is part of [HVAC cleaning](/services/hvac-cleaning/), along with the coil, cabinet and drain line, at $200 to $450 per air handler.

Keep the air handler panels closed. There is live voltage inside even with the thermostat off.

## The short version

Bangs at startup and whistles usually mean the system is short of air: start with the filter and the registers. A rattle is a loose grille. Flapping is a loose duct. Scratching is an animal, so call wildlife control first. Motor noises go to an HVAC contractor.
