## What the scope actually covers

Six complete air systems.

| Item | Quantity stated |
|---|---|
| Air Handling Units | 4 |
| Rooftop Units | 2 |
| Supply ductwork systems | 6 |
| Return ductwork systems | 6 |
| Diffuser and grille systems | 6 |
| VAV boxes | named, **no count given** |

Four AHUs plus two RTUs equals six air moving units, which matches six
supply, six return and six diffuser systems. The document is internally
consistent. Six full systems is a real number a contractor can work from.

Work required per system:

- Coils, fan blades, housings and plenum walls washed. Fiberglass linings vacuumed.
- All accessible ductwork interior vacuumed. Access openings cut where needed, closed with sheet metal panels and sealed against leakage.
- Inaccessible runs cleaned by air pressure technique. Areas blocked by plaster ceilings, fire walls and masonry are explicitly excluded from contractor responsibility.
- Dampers and louvers cleaned and reset to original position.
- Removable diffusers and grilles washed and reinstalled.
- Filters: **customer supplies disposable filters**, contractor installs them, or washes metal filters.
- Ceiling around diffusers, registers and grilles vacuumed.
- Fungistat and bacteriostat application after cleaning, on customer request, per manufacturer specification.
- Negative air pressure maintained during cleaning using a HEPA rated vacuum system where necessary.
- Drop cloths, property protection, broom clean handover.
- Customer provides water, electricity and restrooms.

## NADCA

Not required and not mentioned. The document says "per industry standard"
three times and never names NADCA ACR.

Read this two ways. There is no certification barrier to entry, which widens
the pool of contractors who can bid. But "industry standard" in duct cleaning
is NADCA ACR in practice, and any firm worth putting on a federal installation
will hold it. Worth confirming whether certification is scored in the
evaluation, because if it is, it changes which contractor we approach.

## Risks that must be resolved before a number goes out

### 1. Site condition risk sits with the contractor, after award

Section 1.0:

> "Once the contract has been awarded, it becomes the responsibility of the contractor to validate the actual site conditions as well as any specific programming needs of the customer."

The bid is priced blind and every site surprise lands on the contractor after
signature. **No pre-award site visit is offered anywhere in the document.**
Section 3.2 mentions walk-through inspections, but as part of the design
services that follow award.

Either a pre-bid walkthrough gets arranged, or the quote carries an explicit
assumptions and exclusions schedule. A federal cleaning job priced with
neither is how a contractor loses money on a base.

### 2. The document is a repurposed template

Section 1.0 asks for "ongoing service of all items that may be defective due
to manufacturing error as well as replacement or repair of any damaged item/s
after purchase". Sections 4.8 and 4.9 cover storage of material and
assemblies, and "a maximum of 30 days storage for new products".

That is procurement language from an equipment purchase SOW. It does not
belong in a cleaning scope. Either someone reused a template without
cleaning it, or there is an equipment supply or replacement element nobody
has scoped. Must be clarified. A contractor who reads section 1.0 literally
is agreeing to warranty hardware they never sold.

### 3. Restricted areas

JCSE is an operational communications unit. The document points explicitly at
"6th Security Forces Squadron Restricted Area Access citing the appropriate
paragraphs as applicable".

If any part of Building 861 is a controlled or restricted area, technicians
either need clearance or need continuous escort. Escorted work runs at
roughly half productivity and carries scheduling dependency on the escort
being available. That is a cost driver that has to be known before pricing,
not discovered on day one.

## Base access, hours and liability

**Access.** Written request on company letterhead to the Contracting Officer
listing contract number, work site, start and stop dates, and the names of
every employee and subcontractor employee. Valid state identification for
each person. Driver's license, current vehicle registration, valid insurance
certificate and an SF74 for vehicle passes. Identification badges worn and
visible at all times. An after hours contact number or email in the Emergency
Mass Notification System for every person. The document warns that many
installations need this days in advance. Passes returned at completion.
Failure to comply can result in withholding of final payment.

**Hours.** 0700 to 1600, Monday through Friday. Excludes federal holidays,
training days and family days. Work outside those hours requires coordination
and agreement in advance.

**Timeline.** Not stated. No quote due date, no period of performance, no
anticipated award date. This is a gap in the document, not something we
missed.

**Liability.** Section 5.2 puts the contractor on the hook for any damage to
government property. Section 5.4 requires removal of all hazardous material
and debris from the installation. Section 4.4 requires a named contract
manager and alternate, designated in writing to the Contracting Officer
within 10 days of award, with full authority to act.

**What this means for us.** The performing party has to be a firm with real
general liability cover, SAM registration, and personnel who can pass base
vetting. Florida Breeze Air Duct is the intermediary that sourced the work
and holds the margin. It is not the entity that signs a federal cleaning
contract, designates a contract manager, or accepts liability for damage
inside a JCSE facility. This has to be explicit with the contractor from the
first conversation and explicit in whatever agreement follows.

## Sizing, internal only

Six complete systems on a federal installation, with negative air, access
panel fabrication, fungistat application and base access overhead.

The working range of 25,000 to 50,000 USD holds. Base access overhead and
any escorted work in restricted areas push toward the upper end and can
carry it past it. Broker margin of 10 to 20 percent on that range is roughly
3,500 to 7,000 USD.

**These figures do not leave this file.** No number goes to GovCrest until a
NADCA contractor has priced the job off the drawings. The quote that goes out
is the contractor's number plus margin, not an estimate produced here.
