# Contractor outreach, MacDill Building 861

Goal: appoint one contractor to price and perform this job, with Florida
Breeze Air Duct as the party that sourced it and holds a margin.

**Read the section on sequencing before making a single call.** Getting the
order wrong is the way this opportunity gets taken away from us, and it is a
larger risk than failing to find a contractor.

### 911 Restoration, inbound

Came to us, not the other way round. Tomas, who describes himself as the
owner, made contact through an Israeli community group in Miami and gave a
direct number: **954-274-3325**. His claim is capability across Florida and
Maryland on large scale office work.

What is verified about the brand. 911 Restoration is a legitimate national
franchise network founded by Peleg Lindenberg and Idan Shpizear, which
explains the route the lead arrived by. Services are water, fire, mold,
sewage and storm damage, reconstruction, sanitization and crime scene
cleanup. Certification is **IICRC**. The South Florida operation covers
Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach and St. Lucie.

Genuine advantages over a large firm:

- An owner made contact directly and gave a personal number. He can agree terms on a call. Reaching someone at Service-Tech with authority to set a referral fee will take weeks of getting past a service coordinator.
- Shared language and community, which makes the referral negotiation a different conversation.
- HVAC cleaning is not foreign to restoration work. Post fire and post mold remediation both require it. It is simply not their standalone product.
- **A second bidder is worth having regardless of who wins.** Two independent numbers tell us whether the price is right. That was the open risk in pricing this job and this closes it.

Three specific gaps to test:

1. **IICRC is not NADCA.** IICRC is the damage restoration standard. The SOW requires work to industry standard, which in duct cleaning means NADCA ACR. Ask whether they hold NADCA and whether a certified ASCS would be on site.
2. **Geography, and this is the pricing problem.** 954 is Broward. The franchise footprint is the east coast. MacDill is Tampa, west coast, roughly four hours each way. On a multi day six system job that puts lodging and travel into the number, and a Clearwater firm beats him on logistics before anyone touches a duct. "All over Florida" is a claim to test, not to accept.
3. **No trace of federal registration.** No SAM, no CAGE, no government contract history surfaced. Not disqualifying, because GovCrest holds prime and a subcontractor does not strictly need its own SAM registration. But every piece of base access paperwork would be a first for him rather than a routine, and that is schedule risk on a job where access has to be arranged days in advance.

Note also that "owner of 911 Restoration" almost certainly means owner of a
franchise territory rather than the brand. He mentioned Florida and Maryland,
which suggests two territories. Worth establishing precisely which.

**The sequencing rule applies to him unchanged.** Referral terms in writing
before the base or the prime is named. The leak risk is lower than with a GSA
schedule holder, who could be bought from directly, but the principle does
not change.

Five questions, in this order:

1. What is your structure for referred work?
2. Do you hold NADCA certification, and would a certified ASCS be on site?
3. Have you worked on a military installation, and are you SAM registered with a CAGE code?
4. What are your general liability limits, and can you name additional insureds?
5. How do you price a Tampa job out of Broward? Is travel and lodging inside the number or billed separately?

Question 1 first. Question 5 last, because it is the one that reveals whether
statewide coverage is a capability or an ambition.

## Sequencing. This is the part that matters

Service-Tech holds a GSA schedule. GovCrest is a SAM registered federal
contractor. A schedule holder can be bought from directly, off the schedule,
without a separate competition. The moment both parties know each other's
identity, they have everything they need to complete this transaction without
us in it.

So the referral terms get agreed **before** either party is named to the
other.

**Call one, no names.** There is a federal Statement of Work in hand for
cleaning six complete air conveyance systems at a military installation in
the Tampa area. Four AHUs, two RTUs, six supply, six return, six diffuser
systems. The prime is a SAM registered federal contractor. We sourced it and
are not the performing party. **What is your structure for referred work?**

**Get it in writing.** A percentage or a fixed fee, by email, before anything
else moves. A phone agreement is not an agreement.

**Call two, full disclosure.** Base, building, unit, prime, and the SOW as an
attachment.

A firm with sixty years in federal facilities work has a referral structure
ready. Asking for it is a normal commercial question and will not read as
inexperience. Handing over the entire package and then asking for a fee will.

## Qualifying questions

For Service-Tech most of these are already answered. Use the full list for
the backups.

1. Have you performed work on a military installation? MacDill, Tampa, or any DoD site.
2. Are you NADCA certified, and would a certified ASCS be on site?
3. Are you registered in SAM.gov, and do you hold a CAGE code or a GSA schedule?
4. What are your general liability limits, and can you name additional insureds?
5. Can your personnel pass base vetting, and will you submit a full roster with identification to a Contracting Officer in advance?
6. Do you have capacity for six complete systems within a defined window, 0700 to 1600 weekdays?
7. What is your structure for referred commercial work?

Question 7 decides whether the conversation continues. Ask it first, not
last. A contractor who wants the customer relationship directly is not a
partner on this job.

## What to be straight about, once terms are agreed

We sourced the work. We are not the performing contractor and will not
present ourselves as one. The contractor signs, carries the liability under
section 5.2, designates the contract manager under section 4.4, and handles
hazardous material removal under section 5.4. We hold the customer
relationship and take the agreed margin.

Say it plainly. A contractor who discovers it later walks. One who hears it
up front and stays is a partner for the next one.

## What not to do

Do not give the contractor a target price, a budget, or the internal range.
Their number has to be theirs. Anchoring them costs us the only independent
check on whether the job is priced correctly, and we would never know whether
the margin came out of our side or out of the quality of the work.

## Finding contractors without help, next time

Two free public sources produced everything above.

**NADCA member directory**, `nadca.com/find-a-professional`. Search by ZIP.
Use 33621 for MacDill. Returns members with their certified personnel count
and membership date.

**GSA eLibrary**, `gsaelibrary.gsa.gov`. Search by SIN **561210FAC**,
Facilities Support Services. This returns only firms already approved to sell
to the federal government. This is the search that surfaced Service-Tech, and
it is the higher value of the two for any federal requirement.
