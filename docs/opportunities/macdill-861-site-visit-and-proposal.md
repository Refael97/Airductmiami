## What this decides

The three mandatory documents settle the question of who submits.

| Document | What it requires | Can Florida Breeze Air Duct supply it |
|---|---|---|
| Past performance | Typically three similar contracts with customer name, point of contact, value and dates | **No.** No federal past performance and nothing at this scale |
| Technical proposal | Cleaning plan, methodology, equipment, standard compliance, schedule, safety plan, personnel | **No.** Only the performing party can write this |
| Capability statement | Core competencies, differentiators, company data including UEI, CAGE code, NAICS codes and business size | **No.** No UEI and no CAGE code |

**We cannot be the submitting entity, and must not attempt to be.**

Submitting another firm's past performance as our own is a material
misrepresentation in a federal procurement. The consequence is suspension or
debarment from government work, permanently. It is not a paperwork
shortcut and it is not worth a five figure job.

Separately, we could not carry the obligations even if the documents
existed. Section 4.4 requires a named contract manager with full authority,
designated in writing to the Contracting Officer within 10 days of award.
Section 5.2 assigns liability for damage to government property inside a
JCSE facility. Section 5.4 requires removal of hazardous material from the
installation. None of those belong to a lead generation business.

### The structure that works

**Referral.** The contractor submits, performs and signs. We take an agreed
referral fee and step out of the contractual chain.

It is clean, it carries no integrity exposure, and it is the only structure
that can be executed in the two days available.

**Fee expectation, corrected.** The 10 to 20 percent figure in the
opportunity file assumed sitting inside the transaction as an intermediary
holding the customer relationship. A pure referral normally runs 5 to 10
percent. On a 25,000 to 50,000 USD job that is roughly 1,250 to 5,000 USD.

There is room to negotiate above the bottom of that range. This is not a
cold lead. It is a live federal requirement with a scope document in hand, a
named government point of contact, and a site visit already scheduled.

### Why the agreement has to be signed before the introduction

Right now we hold the SOW, the site visit invitation, the relationship with
Maria and the name of the government POC. That is the entire value of our
position.

The moment the introduction is made, all of it transfers. A contractor who
has Maria's address and Luis's address does not need us for anything.

So the fee is agreed in writing, by email, **before** the base, the building,
the prime or the POC is named. Not a handshake on a call. In writing.

## What Maria answered, and what she did not

She came back on two of the questions.

**Proposal due date: 16 August.** That is a Sunday, which is unusual for a
federal submission and often means the practical cut off is close of business
Friday 14 August. Confirm the exact time and time zone. In federal
procurement a late submission is non-responsive and is rejected
automatically, with no discretion and no appeal.

**Restricted areas: not answered.** She restated the SOW back, correctly
noting that it identifies no specific area of Building 861 as controlled or
restricted and only requires compliance where unescorted entry into such an
area is needed. She closed by saying it should be confirmed with the
Contracting Officer.

**Technical drawings: no response at all.** The section 3.6 request went
unanswered.

### What that tells us

Her signature block reads "Federal Sales & Acquisitions". She is business
development, not technical. She passes opportunities along, she does not
administer them. That is not evasion, it is the honest answer of someone who
does not have the information.

**So stop routing technical questions through her.** Luis Lopez-Rosales is
the `.mil` address, he is organising the walkthrough, and the drawings sit on
the government side rather than with GovCrest. Maria pointed there herself.
Both open questions go to Luis, in the RSVP email that was already going to
him.

## What to capture at the site visit

Hand this to the contractor. It is what turns the walkthrough into a
quotable package.

- Square footage served by each of the six systems
- Duct linear footage, supply and return, by system
- VAV box count and locations
- AHU and RTU tonnage, make and model, and physical access to each
- Condition of the interior duct surfaces, and how much is fiberglass lined
- Where access panels will have to be cut, and what the ceiling type is above each run
- Which runs are inaccessible behind plaster, fire walls or masonry. The SOW excludes these, so they need to be identified and written into the exclusions
- Diffuser and grille count, and how many are readily removable versus riveted or plastered in place
- Filter sizes and quantities, since the customer supplies them
- Which spaces are restricted, and whether escorts are required
- Where equipment can be staged, and where the negative air units can discharge
- Photographs of every one of the above
