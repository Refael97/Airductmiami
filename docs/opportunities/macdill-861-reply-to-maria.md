Hi Maria,

Thank you for sending the Statement of Work. I have been through it in
detail. The scope is clear and the equipment count is workable: four air
handling units, two rooftop units, and six supply, return and diffuser
systems.

Before we return a price, there are a few items the document does not cover.
I would rather raise them now than build assumptions into a number.

**1. Technical drawings.** Section 3.6 states that technical drawings are
available outlining equipment locations, duct runs and VAV boxes. Those were
not attached. They are the single most important item on this list, because
they carry the linear footage of ductwork and the VAV box count, and neither
appears elsewhere in the document. Pricing six systems accurately is not
possible without them.

**2. Equipment and building detail.** Alongside the drawings, we need the
approximate square footage served by these systems, the tonnage or model
numbers of the four AHUs and two RTUs, and the VAV box count if it is
recorded separately from the drawings.

**3. Pre-bid site visit.** Section 1.0 places responsibility for validating
actual site conditions on the contractor once the contract has been awarded.
We are willing to carry that responsibility, but we would like to see the
building before we price it rather than after. Can a walkthrough be arranged
during the bid period? If the schedule does not allow it, we will price from
the drawings and attach a written schedule of assumptions and exclusions so
that both sides know exactly what the number covers.

**4. Restricted areas and escort.** The access section references 6th
Security Forces Squadron Restricted Area Access. Is any part of Building 861
a controlled or restricted area? If so, will escorts be provided by the unit,
or is unescorted entry expected? This affects both scheduling and the labour
hours in the estimate, so it needs to be settled before pricing rather than
after.

**5. Scope confirmation.** Section 1.0 refers to ongoing service of items
defective due to manufacturing error and to replacement or repair of damaged
items after purchase, and sections 4.8 and 4.9 cover storage of new products.
Those read as procurement language carried over from a template. Please
confirm that the scope is cleaning and decontamination only, with no
equipment supply, replacement or warranty component. If there is an equipment
element, we would want it scoped separately.

**6. Filters.** Section 2.2.5 states that disposable filters are supplied by
the customer and installed by the contractor. Please confirm, and let us know
the filter sizes and quantities if that is the arrangement.

**7. Standards.** The document specifies work performed to industry standard
but does not name a standard. Is compliance with NADCA ACR required, and is a
NADCA certified Air Systems Cleaning Specialist on site required or scored in
the evaluation?

**8. Schedule.** The SOW does not give a quote due date, a period of
performance, or an anticipated award date. Please advise on all three. The
0700 to 1600 Monday to Friday window is noted, and we will plan around
federal holidays, training days and family days. If any of the work needs to
run outside those hours we will flag it in the proposal.

**9. Contract structure.** Please confirm the contract vehicle, whether this
is a set aside, and that GovCrest is holding prime on this requirement.

Base access requirements in the final section are understood. Company
letterhead request to the Contracting Officer, full personnel roster
including subcontractor personnel, state identification, vehicle
documentation and SF74, badge display, EMNS after hours contacts and pass
return at completion. We will build the lead time for that into the schedule
rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Once the drawings are in hand we can turn a firm price around within five
business days.
